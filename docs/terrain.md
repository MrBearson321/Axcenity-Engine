# Terrain

Read this when changing terrain data, brushes, generation, or how a Terrain is saved and drawn. The Terrain Studio in the editor is `crates/editor` (`terrain_studio.rs`). Its tool palette (Sculpt, Materials, Terrain rows) shows icons, with each tool's name, key and hint on hover; from 400 points wide it also names each tool beside its icon (`CAPTIONS_WIDTH`; test `a_wide_panel_names_its_tools`). This page is the algorithms in `crates/terrain`.

`TerrainData` lives on the Terrain Instance in `core`. This crate only computes on that data. It does not draw, simulate contacts, or touch the disk: `scene` meshes it, `physics` collides with it, and `project` reads and writes the files.

Foliage is painted onto the terrain the way materials are: a brush, a density, and a choice of item from the Assets panel (a built-in prop or a model). Each stroke scatters instances on the surface (sample XZ, so they follow sculpting). They are not Instances. A stroke can mark them solid so parts rest on them, or leave them visual so parts fall through to the terrain. The Terrain Studio's Foliage tool paints them; click a prop or model in Assets to choose what to scatter. Shift erases the chosen item. One stroke is one undo step, including turning collide off. Painted foliage saves as optional `foliage.json` in the terrain's data folder; a missing file means none. Older paints that named a built-in plant still load. Painted plants are drawn only within a cull distance of the camera, fading out before it (grass 120 studs, flowers 150, bushes 300, trees 800, times their scale; props at any distance; a painted model by its size as placed, 120 studs per stud of its largest extent, from 60 to 1,500, so a half-stud fern goes at 60 studs and a 10-stud clump of trees at 1,200, with its levels of detail before that; `scene::foliage::model_cull_distance`). `scene` skips a plant or model past its distance (measured flat, in XZ) before building its draw items, so a terrain with thousands painted costs only those near the camera; the renderer's own cull and fade do the rest (see [rendering/pipeline.md](rendering/pipeline.md)). Painted plants sway in the Workspace's `GlobalWind`, and their shadows with them ([rendering/wind.md](rendering/wind.md)). Painted instances are kept between frames as groups, 48 studs square and short-reach plants apart from trees, rebuilt only when the terrain or a painted model changes, so a painted forest costs the frame what it can see ([rendering/performance.md](rendering/performance.md)).

Scatter rules (procedural worlds PW1, `procedural/src/terrain_scatter.rs`): a `ScatterRule` inside a Terrain places plants, props or models wherever its own conditions hold: `Spacing` (studs between neighbours) and `Density`, a height window (`MinHeight`, `MaxHeight`, `HeightFade`), a slope window (`MinSlope`, `MaxSlope`, `SlopeFade`, in degrees), a material `Layer` (the TerrainLayer's name, with `LayerThreshold` of its weight), `Clumping` and `ClumpSize` (noise that gathers plants into patches), `AvoidRadius` (kept clear of what rules above it placed), `Underwater`, `RotationRange`, `ScaleRange` and `CanCollide`. `Models` lists what it places (`plant:Tree`, `prop:Crate` or a model path, one per line). Rules run in order, like TerrainLayers. Placement is a hash grid: each `Spacing`-wide cell gets one spot from `Seed` and the cell's number, so every 32-sample chunk is worked out on its own, on every device, by `procedural::Generators::update`, and the same terrain, rules and seed give the same layout. The result sits in `TerrainData` beside painted foliage (`generated_chunks`, `all_foliage`), is never saved or sent, and changing it bumps `generated_version`, not `revision`. Only chunks whose ground (or a neighbour's, within the largest `AvoidRadius`) or rules changed are made again: a sculpt stroke on a 2,048-stud forest of 348,000 plants remakes about 16 chunks in 2 to 5 ms; the whole forest takes 0.12 s in release (`bench_forest_on_a_2048_stud_terrain`). Drawing (`scene/src/foliage.rs`) keeps each chunk's groups and builds again only chunks whose list changed; physics, navigation and preloading read `all_foliage`. The Terrain Studio's Scatter Rules section (`editor/src/terrain_studio/scatter.rs`, actions `app/terrain/scatter.rs`) adds rules from presets (the Forest set of three, Forest Trees, Undergrowth, Meadow Flowers, Grass Tufts, or an empty Scatter Rule), edits the selected one, picks its models from Assets, and Bake turns what the rules placed into painted foliage and switches them off, one undo step.

Terrain and objects (procedural worlds PW5): a ScatterRule with `AvoidParts` (on) places nothing under or into an anchored, colliding part whose box comes within 12 studs of the ground over a spot (`procedural/src/obstacles.rs`: the column from a stud under the ground (so a floor flush with it counts) to 12 studs up against the part's box grown sideways by `PartMargin`, 1 stud), so a house, a fence or a placed rock clears its own ground; a platform high above doesn't. `Obstacles` keeps the Workspace's parts between frames (the list read again when the Workspace's structure changes, a part's box when its revision does), and each part's hash goes into the key of the chunks it reaches, so moving one remakes only the ground near it. `AlignToSlope` (0 to 1) leans what a rule places toward the ground's normal and `SinkDepth` (studs at scale 1) lowers it into the ground; they're `FoliageInstance::lean` and `sink`, used alike by drawing (`scene/src/foliage.rs`) and solid foliage's colliders (`physics/src/shapes.rs`); painted foliage stays upright. The rule presets lean grass, flowers and bushes and sink trees a little. Conform to Terrain and Blend into Terrain are in the ribbon's Solid Modeling group ([architecture/solid-modeling.md](architecture/solid-modeling.md)).

Holes and cave pieces (procedural worlds PW7): `TerrainData` keeps a hole mask, one flag per cell (cell (x, z) runs from sample (x, z) to +X and +Z), empty until the first hole (`holes`, `is_hole`, `hole_at`, `holes_mut_in`). A hole change counts as a height change: it bumps the chunk's height version, goes through `read_region`/`write_region` for undo, and is kept by `replace_with` and `resampled`. Holes save as optional `holes.png` (8-bit greyscale, 255 a hole), written only when there are some; `project` removes a leftover when they're all filled, and an older engine ignores the file. A cut-out cell is left out of the drawn chunk (a chunk with holes is always drawn at full detail, so a hole looks the same far away), out of the collider (parry's `HeightField` cell `CELL_REMOVED`), out of the navmesh's ground (a cave floor of parts under it is walkable), out of `ground_y` and raycasts (a ray passes through, and a ray from under the surface never hits it), and scatter rules place nothing on it. A terrain with holes isn't "solid below" for queries (`open_below`), so caves under it work. The Terrain Studio's **Holes** tool (`brush::Operation::Holes`, Shift fills) cuts and fills them, one undo step a stroke; the AI tools' `terrain_edit` sculpt `hole` and `fill` do the same. Cliff and cave pieces are built-in props for rules: `prop:Cliff` (upright slabs for steep ground) and `prop:Rock` (a boulder), with the rule presets **Cliff Rocks** (38 degrees and steeper, leaned a little with the slope and sunk 2.5 studs, solid) and **Boulders** (8 to 40 degrees, in groups); the Alpine world preset adds Cliff Rocks. They aren't in the Assets panel's props. A cave mouth is a hole in a hillside, a tunnel of parts under it, and rock parts round the opening, which rules keep clear of (`AvoidParts`): see `examples/hello-caves` and its game test, which walks in (and fails without the hole).

Roads and paths (procedural worlds PW4): a `TerrainPath` inside a Spline keeps every terrain's scatter rules off its strip, live, and the editor's Apply and Route flatten, paint and lay out the road. See [architecture/splines.md](architecture/splines.md#terrain-paths).

Biomes (procedural worlds PW2, `terrain/src/biomes.rs`): a `Biome` inside a Terrain is an area with its own ground layer (`GroundLayer`, a TerrainLayer's name) and its own ScatterRules (its children). It covers ground by itself where its height, slope and moisture windows hold (`MinHeight`…`SlopeFade`, `MinMoisture`, `MaxMoisture`, `MoistureFade`; moisture is shared noise `MoistureScale` studs across, the same for every biome at the same scale), with edges broken up by `EdgeNoise` over `NoiseScale`, later biomes covering earlier ones (`AutoEnabled` off: only where painted). Painting a biome is painting its ground layer: the ground layer's paint is the biome's paint, and the automatic weights fill only what paint leaves, so changing a biome never loses paint and nothing new is saved, undone or sent. `MaterialRules` holds the biomes too: each biome's ground layer is laid where it covers, under every layer's own rule (rock on cliffs and snow on peaks still win), and its `key` changes with them, so the weight textures follow. A rule inside a Biome places only where that biome covers, its chance times the biome's weight, so it thins out at soft edges (`materials::biome_weights`). The Terrain Studio's Biomes section (`editor/src/terrain_studio/biomes.rs`, actions `app/terrain/biomes.rs`) lists them, edits the selected one, adds them from presets (Lowland Forest, Meadow, Alpine, Beach, an empty Biome; each brings its ground layer, made if the terrain lacks it, and its rules, one undo step), and Paint this biome picks the Paint tool on its ground layer. With a Biome or a rule in one selected, Add rule adds into that Biome.

World presets (procedural worlds PW3, `project/src/world_presets.rs`): a `.axworld` file (JSON, `WorldPreset`) holds a whole landscape setup: the generator's settings, the terrain's TerrainLayers, Biomes (with their rules) and ScatterRules as a prefab file's JSON (so every property is kept), the water level as a share of the height range, and the height range the objects were made on (heights scale to the terrain they're applied to; 10000, "no top", stays). `apply_objects` checks the objects in a world of their own first, then replaces the terrain's layers, biomes and rules and sets its water. Six are built in, made from the studio's own layer, biome and rule presets (`editor/src/terrain_studio/world_presets.rs`: Temperate Forest, Alpine, Desert, Tropical Island, Tundra, Meadow); the project's own are in its `presets/` folder. The Generate tool's World Presets section picks one: **Generate with it** sets the generator and makes a preview, and Apply then puts in the landscape, the objects and the water as one undo step (`app/terrain/world_presets.rs`); **Only its materials and rules** keeps the shape; **Save as preset** writes the terrain's setup, with the settings its landscape was generated with, to `presets/<name>.axworld`.

Water (LE4, `scene/src/terrain_water.rs`): a Terrain's `Water` switch floods every chunk whose lowest ground is below `WaterLevel` (world Y), drawn as the renderer's water (`docs/rendering/water.md`: waves, what's under seen through it, reflections, depth tint and foam where it meets the ground). `WaterColor`, `WaterClarity`, `WaveHeight`, `WaveLength`, `WaveSpeed` and `WaveDirection` are the water material's values. Each flooded chunk gets one water item, the top of a box as wide as the chunk at the level; its waves follow world positions, so neighbours meet seamlessly. A chunk's lowest ground is kept by its heights' version, so only an edited chunk is looked at again. The Terrain Studio's **Water** section has the switch, a level slider over the terrain's height range, the color and the waves. Water is only drawn: nothing swims or floats in it yet. Test: `scene/src/terrain.rs` `terrain_water_fills_only_the_chunks_whose_ground_dips_below_its_level`.

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| Placement | World studs and the sample grid |
| `brush` | Raise, lower, smooth, flatten, sculpt, noise, stamps, holes, material paint, and foliage, with undo records |
| `convert` | A part turned into ground: samples under it raised to its top and painted (the editor's Solid Modeling ▸ To Terrain) |
| `generate` | Landscapes from settings and a seed, including presets |
| `erosion` | Water and gravity |
| `materials` | Layers by height and slope, blended with paint |
| `mesh` | Chunk meshes at several levels of detail |
| `raycast` | Where a ray meets the surface |
| `files` | PNG and JSON codecs (`heights.png`, paint maps, optional `foliage.json` and `holes.png`) |
| `job` | Slow work on another thread, with progress and cancel |

Generators stay deterministic for a seed and `GENERATOR_VERSION`. Saved terrains record both. Bump the version when the output changes. Chunk triangles split cells the same way as collision, so the drawn surface and the collider match.
