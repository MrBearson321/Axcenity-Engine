# Character controllers

A kinematic walk, run, jump and crouch, up slopes and steps within limits, and custom movement modes that scripts move.

- **ThirdPersonController** and **FirstPersonController** are separate classes, and a character has one or the other. They move the same way, with the same movement properties (everything below except Camera), from the same code in `character`; they differ only in their camera. Each saves under its own class name.
- A controller sits under a `Model`, `Part`, or `MeshPart`. The body is that part, or every `Part` and `MeshPart` inside the parent. The first controller of either class in the Workspace that has a body receives input and owns the camera. Others stay where they are.
- **Movement** is `"Walk"`, `"Run"` or `"Crouch"`, in any case. `"Crouch"` walks crouched, the same as setting `Crouch`. A value that isn't text walks. `WalkSpeed` is 16 studs a second and `RunSpeed` is 32, across flat ground, relative to where the camera looks (pitch does not change the speed). Set them from Lua (`controller.Movement = "Run"`), from the set-property block, or from Properties. Holding the `Run` action (LeftShift, or L3 on a controller) runs a walking character at `RunSpeed`; `Movement` stays as it is. Shift still slows the fly camera when no controller owns the view.
- **Custom modes.** Any other name in `Movement`, such as `"Swim"` or `"Hover"`, is a custom mode. While it's active the controller doesn't walk, run, jump, crouch, stand or fall the character: scripts and blocks move its parts (set `Position`, or the move-by block, each frame from `RunService.Heartbeat` or an every-frame block). A `Jump` asked for is used up and does nothing. Held keys, a held C and a change to `Crouch` are ignored until the mode is cleared; a crouched character stays crouched. The camera still follows the body and still turns and zooms. The parts are still anchored before physics. `Movement` reads back the mode's name. Setting `Movement` to `""` clears it: on its next step the controller writes back the built-in mode it last had (`"Walk"` if it never had one), so a script that reads `Movement` in the same frame it cleared it still sees `""`. The character then starts in the air for one step, as at the start of Play, so it lands where the script left it or falls from there, and jumping, steps and slopes work again. The remembered mode isn't saved.
- **Jump.** The `Jump` action (Space, or A on a controller) jumps during Play, and held on landing it jumps again. A script or the set-property block sets `Jump = true` to jump once; the controller sets it back to false on its next step, whether or not the character was on the ground, so a jump asked for in the air does nothing. `JumpHeight` (7.2 studs) is how high it rises under the default gravity (196.2 studs/s²). It falls by `workspace.Gravity`, launched at the same speed whatever that is, so halving the gravity doubles the jump (Phase 34). A ceiling stops the rise.
- **Crouch.** Holding the `Crouch` action (C, or B on a controller) crouches during Play. A script or the set-property block sets `Crouch = true` to crouch and `false` to stand; the character is crouched while either asks. Crouching squashes every body part toward the feet until the body is `CrouchHeight` (3 studs, at least 0.5) tall; a body already shorter keeps its size. The camera drops as far as the top did, and standing puts back each part's height and place. Walls, ceilings and steps use the crouched height, so the character fits under what it could not walk into standing. Standing is refused while anything solid is within the standing height above the middle of the body or the edges of its footprint; the character stays crouched and stands on the first step that's clear, without being asked again. While crouched, walk and run both move at `CrouchSpeedScale` (0.5) of `WalkSpeed` or `RunSpeed`. Jumping still works. The crouch is not saved: Play starts standing, and a world saved during Play is the one from before Play.
- **FootPlacement** (Phase 79, off by default; `StarterPlayer.CharacterFootPlacement` turns it on for each player's character, as the Third Person template does). A skinned character with an Animator and `LeftFoot`/`RightFoot` bones plants each foot on what's under it on slopes and stairs and lowers its hips to match, on each device. A character made of Parts has no bones to move. `docs/rendering/animation.md`, Runtime IK.
- **Ground, slopes and steps.** Movement goes in slices of at most half a stud, so nothing on the way is skipped. Rays from the middle of the body, at heights above `MaxStepHeight`, treat anything that isn't walkable ground as a wall: the move stops at it and slides along it. Ground is walkable when it's no steeper than `MaxSlopeAngle` (45°, at most 89). A face lower than `MaxStepHeight` (1 stud) is a step when walkable ground lies on top of it within that height; the character rises onto it as its front reaches it. A steeper slope or a taller step stops the character; it is never launched up one. Ground is looked for under the middle and the leading edge, so the character keeps to a ledge until its middle leaves it, follows a walkable slope down, and steps down a drop no taller than `MaxStepHeight`. Off a taller drop it falls under gravity and lands on whatever solid is below. Parts with `CanCollide` off are passed through. The character is not a physics body: its parts are anchored after scripts and before physics, so a script cannot drop them for that step, and other parts keep the same gravity and friction. Its fall and jump state isn't saved; Play starts it in the air for one step, which lands it.
- **Seats** (Phase 39). A `Seat` is a Part (`IsA("Part")`, the same properties and drawing, collision and editing) with `Occupant` (the controller sitting on it, or nil), `Disabled` (false) and `SeatOffset` (an Attachment where the feet go, or nil for the middle of the top face). A controller's character whose box comes within 0.1 studs of an empty Seat that isn't Disabled sits on it, unless its `Movement` is a custom mode; walking into a seat taller than a step is enough. `seat:Sit(controller)` sits it on any seat on the controller's next step, Disabled or not: it writes the seat's `Occupant` and the controller's `Sit`, takes the controller off any other seat, and whoever sat there stands up. `Occupant` is read-only from scripts. Sitting puts the feet on the sit point (the character doesn't turn), unanchors the body's parts and welds each to the seat with a `SeatWeld` WeldConstraint inside it, so physics carries the character with an unanchored seat, such as one welded to a cart (its weight joins the cart's; `keep_kinematic` leaves it unanchored). The controller's `Movement` reads `"Seated"` and `Sit` is true; move keys and `Crouch` do nothing, and the camera still turns and zooms. An Animator in the character whose state machine has a `Sit` state plays it, and on standing goes back to the state it was in (its starting state if it hadn't run yet); without one nothing is played or reported. The character stands up when Jump is pressed (or `Jump = true`), when `Sit` is set false, when the seat gets another `Occupant`, leaves the Workspace or is destroyed, or when a script sets `Movement` (which it keeps). Standing up removes the welds, clears `Occupant`, puts back the built-in mode it had, and places the character half a stud in front of the seat (the way its front face looks, along the ground) with its feet as high as the seat's top, from where it falls and lands. `Seated(active, seat)` fires on the controller after the controller's step: true and the Seat when it sits, false and the Seat it left when it stands. Blocks: "when someone sits on *seat*" and "make *controller* leave the seat" ([blocks.md](blocks.md)). Insert ▸ Seat, or the Model tab's Seat button. Sitting isn't saved. `examples/hello-seat` has a chair, a Disabled bench that B sits on from a LocalScript (L stands up), and a cart whose seat starts its drive from a block script for three seconds and then stands the rider up.
- **Vehicles** (Phase 40). A `VehicleSeat` is a Seat (`IsA("Seat")`; everything above applies) that drives the car it's welded into, with `MaxSpeed` (60), `Torque` (80), `TurnSpeed` (120), `SteerAngle` (30), `Throttle`, `Steer` (both -1 to 1) and `HandBrake`. While the controller that receives input sits on one, the Move action sets `Throttle` (W/S or a stick's forward) and `Steer` (D/A) every step, the camera starts behind the car looking the way it faces and then turns with it, as Phase 31's Follow does, while mouse look still adds to that, and standing up sets both back to 0 and leaves the camera on the character. Scripts set `Throttle` and `Steer` to drive an empty car (input overwrites them while someone drives). The wheels are `VehicleWheel`s parented to the car's parts, each with `Position` and `Orientation` relative to its part (as an Attachment's), `Radius`, `SuspensionRestLength`, `SuspensionStiffness`, `SuspensionDamping`, `Friction`, `Steers`, `Driven`, `Brakes` and a `WheelPart` moved to it; how they drive is in [physics.md](../architecture/physics.md), Vehicles. Insert ▸ Vehicle (Explorer, and the Model tab's Effects group) makes `Car`: a red 6×1×10 Chassis with a DriverSeat welded on its left, four wheels whose front two steer, and an anchored, uncolliding cylinder for each (`editor::tree::vehicle`), as one undo step; Vehicle Seat and Wheel insert the pieces. `examples/hello-vehicle` has that car beside the character and a ramp ahead of it, and a blue AutoCar that a Script drives round in circles.
- **Landing** (Phase 43). Each time a character lands after being in the air (a jump, a fall off an edge, or a drop from where a script put it), its controller fires `Landed(fallSpeed)`, how fast it was falling in studs a second, after its step and `Seated` (`Controllers::take_landings`, `axcenity_gameplay::fire_landed`). Settling onto the ground on the first step of Play doesn't count. A Health in its Model with `FallDamage` on takes its fall damage just before.
- **Health** (Phase 43). A `Health` in any Model has `MaxHealth` (100), `Health` (100, kept between 0 and MaxHealth), `RegenRate` (1 a second), `RegenDelay` (5 seconds), `Invulnerable`, and `FallDamage` (off), `FallDamageThreshold` (60 studs a second) and `FallDamageScale` (1). `health:TakeDamage(amount, source?)` does nothing when it's Invulnerable, already at 0, or `amount` isn't above 0; otherwise it lowers Health and fires `HealthChanged(new, old)`, then `Damaged(amount lost, source or nil)`, then `Died` if it reached 0. `health:Heal(amount)` raises it, up to MaxHealth, and does nothing at 0. Setting `Health` from a script fires `HealthChanged` (and `Died` at 0) too, and is the way back from 0; writes from Rust (Properties, a save load) fire nothing. So `Died` fires once, however many hits land in a frame. Dying does nothing else unless the Model has a Humanoid (below): the Model is left for scripts. Each frame, once `RegenDelay` seconds have passed since the last damage (or if it was never damaged), a Health above 0 regains `RegenRate` a second up to MaxHealth; one above a lowered MaxHealth drops to it. A landing faster than `FallDamageThreshold` takes `(speed − threshold) × FallDamageScale` (no source). The Health of a part or controller is the one inside it or its nearest ancestor that has one (`gameplay::health::health_of`). The events are queued (`SharedHealthEvents`, one queue for both sides) and fired on both sides by `ScriptRuntime::fire_changes` (the character system's `fire_events`, after the change events and the Abilities' events). Health is saved in slots like any property. Code: `crates/gameplay/src/health.rs (its game system: `character_system.rs`)`.
- **Damage zones** (Phase 43). A `DamageZone` is a Part (`IsA("Part")`) that starts Anchored, CanCollide off, half see-through and red, with `DamagePerSecond` (10). In frame step 4 (`axcenity_gameplay::step_health`) each zone in the Workspace finds the parts inside it with `GetPartsInPart`'s query (`physics::parts_in_part`) and damages each Health they belong to once, by `DamagePerSecond × frame time`, with the zone as the source; then Healths regenerate. Insert ▸ Health (into the selected character's Model; a warning when there's none) and Insert ▸ Damage Zone (8×4×8 on the ground at the camera's focus), also on the Model tab. `examples/hello-health` has a character with a Health (RegenRate 10, RegenDelay 2, FallDamage on), a `Lava` zone (20 a second), a tower to drop off, health bars, and a Script that prints hard landings and puts the character back at the start two seconds after it dies.
- **ProximityPrompts** (Phase 44). A `ProximityPrompt` under a part (it shows over the part's middle) or an Attachment (over the Attachment) has `ActionText` ("Interact"), `ObjectText`, `Action` (an input map action, `Interact` by default: E, or X on a controller), `HoldDuration` (0), `MaxActivationDistance` (10), `RequiresLineOfSight` (true), `Enabled` and `Exclusivity` (`Enum.ProximityPromptExclusivity`: `OnePerButton`, `OneGlobally` or `AlwaysShow`). Each frame after the input (`ScriptRuntime::step_prompts`, `script::prompt`), the enabled prompts in the Workspace within reach of the middle of the player character's box (or of the camera, with no character) are candidates; one needing line of sight is left out when a ray from the character to it hits anything first, other than its own part and the character (`physics::cast_ray_excluding`). Of those, every AlwaysShow prompt shows, the nearest of the rest shows if it's OneGlobally, and the nearest OnePerButton prompt for each action shows. A prompt that starts showing fires `PromptShown`; one that stops fires `PromptHidden` (after ending its hold). The action must go down while the prompt shows (walking up with it held does nothing): with HoldDuration 0 that fires `Triggered`; otherwise `PromptButtonHoldBegan`, and once it's been held HoldDuration seconds `PromptButtonHoldEnded` then `Triggered`. Holding on doesn't trigger again; letting go fires `TriggerEnded` (or `PromptButtonHoldEnded`, let go early). Every event but Shown and Hidden passes the Player; all fire in Scripts and then LocalScripts. The held state is the game's (input the UI took doesn't count), with the player's rebinds. The prompt shows the action's first key, or its first controller button once a controller was used last (a key, mouse button or wheel goes back to keys; `input::Action::label`): `ButtonX` is X, `ButtonL1` LB, `LeftShift` Shift. Its UI and CSS are in [../ui.md](../ui.md). `Dialogue` (Phase 77) names a `.dialogue.json` it starts when triggered, with no script ([api-dialogue.md](api-dialogue.md)). Insert ▸ Prompt (Explorer, and the Model tab's Prompt) goes into the selected part or Attachment.
- **ClickDetectors** (Phase 44). A `ClickDetector` in a part (or in a Model or part around it: the nearest one going up counts) makes it clickable, with `MaxActivationDistance` (32) and `CursorIcon` (a CSS cursor name such as `crosshair` or `grab`; empty is the pointing hand). Each frame, in the same step as prompts, the ray from the camera through the mouse (`Camera:ScreenPointToRay`, as `Mouse.Target` casts it, leaving out the player character) finds the first part; it's pointed at when that part has a detector and the hit point is within MaxActivationDistance of the character's middle (or the camera). Pointing at a new one fires `MouseHoverEnter` (and `MouseHoverLeave` on the last); the host shows its CursorIcon where the game UI doesn't ask for a cursor (`ScriptRuntime::click_cursor`). A left button pressed and let go on the same detector fires `MouseClick`, the right `RightMouseClick`; clicks the UI took don't count. Each passes the Player. Insert ▸ Click Detector goes into the selected part. `examples/hello-prompt` has a door to hold E at (it opens and closes), a chest behind a wall whose prompt sits on an Attachment and shows only round the wall's end, a red button whose block script turns it green when clicked, and `prompt.css`.
- **Tools and Inventory** (Phase 45; `gameplay::tool`, its game system `gameplay/src/tool_system.rs`, and `core::tool`). A `Tool` is a Model of parts (`IsA("Model")`, `GetPivot`/`PivotTo`) with `Handle` (a part in it; nil is its child part named "Handle"), `Grip` (an Attachment in the Handle; nil is the Handle's Attachment named "Grip", else the Handle's middle), `RequiresHandle` (true; off, a Tool with no Handle can still be carried but is never picked up by touch), `CanBeDropped` (true), `ToolTip`, `TextureId` (the hotbar slot's image) and `Item` (a data-table row as `"table:key"`, such as `"weapons:sword"`, for scripts to read with DataService). Events `Equipped`, `Unequipped`, `Activated` and `Deactivated`, with no arguments, on both sides. An `Inventory` goes in a character's Model: `Capacity` (10), `Equipped` (the Tool in hand; read-only) and `HotbarEnabled` (true). Its Tools are its children, in hotbar order; the one in hand stays a child. `GetItems()`, `Add(tool)` (false when full; takes it out of any other Inventory first), `Remove(tool)` (parent nil), `Equip(tool)` (it must be in the Inventory) and `UnequipAll()`; `tool:Drop()` (false if not carried or `CanBeDropped` is off). A script's calls change the world at once; `Unequipped`/`Equipped` fire once the script has run (after its resume), for what changed, and also when a save slot loads another Tool in hand (`Tools::sync` compares each Inventory's `Equipped` with what it last fired). Equipping sets the Bool parameter `Equipped` of the character's Animator, if its state machine has one.
  - **Where carried Tools are.** A Tool in an Inventory but not in hand is put away (`core::tool::Carried::Stored`): physics, queries, the draw list and picking leave its parts out. The one in hand (`Held`) is drawn, held still by physics (anchored) and doesn't collide; `character::body_parts` leaves carried Tools out of the body. After the Animators pose (`axcenity_gameplay::place_tools`, with their `Poses`), each Tool in hand moves so its Grip is at the character's right hand, turned as the hand is: a skinned MeshPart's right hand bone (`anim::ragdoll::right_hand`: `RightHand`, `mixamorig:RightHand`, `hand.R`…, placed by `bone_in_world`), else a part named `RightHand` (its middle) or `Right Arm` (its bottom), else half a stud to the right of and in front of the body's biggest part. A Grip with no turn holds the Tool pointing the way the hand (or the body, -Z) faces.
  - **Each frame** (`axcenity_gameplay::step_tools`, after `step_prompts`): each character with an Inventory picks up the dropped Tools (in the Workspace, not in an Inventory) whose Handle's box touches its body's box (within 0.25 studs), while it has room; a full one leaves them. A Tool a character dropped isn't picked back up by it until it has stepped off it. Then the player character's Inventory follows the game's input (not what the UI took): with `HotbarEnabled`, number keys 1–9 and 0 pick slots 1–10 (the slot in hand puts it away), the controller's RB and LB go to the next and previous slot (from none: the first and last), and the mouse wheel goes through the slots while a Tool is in hand (with none, it zooms the camera; the player doesn't zoom while a Tool is in hand). The input map's `Drop` action (Backspace, or Y) drops the Tool in hand, and `Primary` (the left button, or RT) fires `Activated` on it as it goes down and `Deactivated` as it's let go (or the Tool leaves the hand). A dropped Tool goes into the Workspace with its Handle in front of the character's biggest part, clear of it, and falls.
  - **Hotbar.** The default hotbar is drawn by the host over the game UI, from `axcenity_gameplay::player_inventory` (`ui::hotbar_items`; its CSS is in [../ui.md](../ui.md)). Turn `HotbarEnabled` off for a game's own, made from `GetItems`, `Equip` and `Equipped`.
  - **Saving.** Inventories and their Tools are world state; a load matches Tools by Name and `Item` wherever they are ([../architecture/saves.md](../architecture/saves.md)), so a dropped sword goes back into the Inventory as the same Instance, its Attributes as saved.
  - Insert ▸ Tool makes a Tool with a 1×1×4 Handle and a Grip at its back end; Insert ▸ Inventory goes into the selected character's Model. `examples/hello-tools` has a sword, a torch and a shield, an Inventory of 2, a data table of their damage, `tools.css` and K/L to save and load slot 1.
- **Humanoid** (Phase 46; `gameplay::humanoid`, the character system `gameplay/src/character_system.rs`). A `Humanoid` in a character's Model (or part) is a Roblox-familiar front for its pieces, and stores nothing of its own but `BreakJointsOnDeath` (true) and `RagdollOnDeath` (false). It finds the Model's controller, Health, Inventory and Animator (the first Animator inside that isn't in a Tool) and, during Play, makes each one missing (a ThirdPersonController, a Health, an Inventory, an Animator with no state machine), when a script first uses it or on the next frame. Its `WalkSpeed`, `RunSpeed`, `JumpHeight`, `Sit`, `PlatformStand`, `AutoRotate` and `MoveDirection` are the controller's, and `Health`, `MaxHealth`, `DisplayName` and `HealthDisplayDistance` the Health's: reading or setting one reads or sets the piece's (setting `Health` fires `HealthChanged` as the Health's own does), so there's one value, and a Model with only the pieces works the same. Methods: `MoveTo(position, part?)`, `Move(direction, relativeToCamera?)` (sets `MoveDirection` across the ground, turned by the camera's yaw on the client, and stops a MoveTo), `Jump()`, `TakeDamage(amount, source?)` (the Health's), `EquipTool(tool)` (into the Inventory, an error when it's full, then in hand) and `UnequipTools()`, `GetState()` and `ChangeState(state)`.
  - **Controller properties it relies on.** `MoveDirection` (Vector3, zero): a controller that doesn't receive input (an NPC's) walks it at its speed (a length under 1 walks slower; Y is ignored), and the player's controller walks it while no move key is held. A controller without input is left where it is until it's given a `MoveDirection` or `Jump`; from then on it falls and lands like the player's (its first step settles it, keeping the Jump for the next). `PlatformStand` (false): on, the controller neither moves nor jumps the character. `AutoRotate` (true): the character turns about the middle of its box to face the way it walks (a first-person one, the way the camera looks); it's taken to face -Z when the controller first steps it, and how far its first part has turned since is where it faces, so a script turning it is seen. A controller whose `Movement` is `"Dead"` (`character::DEAD`, a custom mode) doesn't move the character, and `keep_kinematic` doesn't anchor its parts.
  - **MoveTo.** Each frame (`axcenity_gameplay::step_humanoids`, after the controllers and `Landed`), the walk finds a path from the feet (the bottom middle of the body's box) on the navigation mesh (`nav::NavMesh::find_path`, built the first time a Humanoid walks and again at most every 0.5 s once the world changes), then points the controller's `MoveDirection` at the next corner (passed within 0.3 studs), so it walks there on the next step. Within half a stud of the point across the ground it stops (`MoveDirection` zero) and fires `MoveToFinished(true)`; no path, 8 seconds, or dying stops it with `MoveToFinished(false)`. With `part`, the point keeps its offset from the part as it moves, and the path is found again once it has moved a stud. A new `MoveTo` replaces the last without firing, and `Move` stops it.
  - **States.** `Enum.HumanoidStateType`: Running, Jumping, Freefall, Landed, Seated, Swimming (reserved: there's no water yet), Ragdoll and Dead. Each frame the state comes from the pieces: Dead at Health 0, Ragdoll while a Ragdoll in the Model is on, Seated while the controller's `Sit` is true, Jumping while rising after a jump and Freefall while falling (`Controllers::in_air`), Landed for the frame after either, else Running. `StateChanged(old, new)` fires when it changes (not when the Humanoid is first seen). `GetState()` is the state as of the last frame (Running before the first). `ChangeState` asks for Running (off a Seat, out of PlatformStand, Ragdolls off), Jumping (`Jump`), Ragdoll (an error with no Ragdoll) or Dead (Health to 0); the others are an error.
  - **Dying.** When its Health dies (straight after the Health's `Died`, or the next frame for a Health set to 0 from Rust), the Humanoid fires `StateChanged(old, Dead)` and then `Died`, once: the controller's `Movement` becomes `"Dead"` and its `MoveDirection` zero; with `RagdollOnDeath` every Ragdoll in the Model turns on; with `BreakJointsOnDeath` its WeldConstraints and joints outside Ragdolls turn off and its body parts are unanchored, so they fall. A script setting Health above 0 brings it back: the state goes on from Dead and `Movement` goes back to the built-in mode.
  - **Animation.** Its Animator plays the state named `Idle`, `Walk` or `Run` on the ground (by how fast its feet moved since the last frame: under half a stud a second is Idle, over halfway from WalkSpeed to RunSpeed is Run), `Jump` while Jumping and `Fall` in Freefall, when its state machine has a state by that name, each time that changes. Seats play `Sit` themselves. Test: `a_humanoid_plays_its_animators_idle_walk_run_jump_and_fall_states` (`player/src/humanoid_tests.rs`). Insert ▸ Character's clips start empty, so the states switch but nothing visibly moves until clips are put in them.
  - Insert ▸ Humanoid goes into the selected character's Model. Insert ▸ **Character** (Explorer, and the Model tab) makes `Character`: Torso, Head, Left Arm, Right Arm, Left Leg and Right Leg parts (5 studs tall, feet at the insert point, facing -Z) welded to the Torso, a Humanoid, a ThirdPersonController, a Health, an Inventory and an Animator playing `assets/animations/Character/Character.statemachine.json`, which the first insert writes with an empty one-second clip for each of Idle (the default), Walk, Run, Jump, Fall and Sit (`editor::tree::character`), as one undo step. Without a saved project the Animator has no state machine, with a warning.
- **Players, spawns and teams** (Phase 47; `script::players`, `project::load_starter_character`). One local player now; the same paths take many in Phase 64.
  - **Joining.** The player's `Player` joins Players at the first step, goes on the `AutoAssignable` Team with the fewest players (the first of those tied), and `Players.PlayerAdded(player)` fires. `Players:GetPlayers()` lists them; `Player.Character` (the character Model, or nil), `Player.Team` (a Team or nil; setting it also sets `Neutral`) and `Player.Neutral` (true: on no team) are its properties. In a hosted game (Phase 63), players on other devices join the same way, without a character yet, and `Players.PlayerRemoving(player)` fires as one leaves; `Players.MaxPlayers` (8) is how many a host takes ([../architecture/networking.md](../architecture/networking.md)).
  - **The first character.** Hosts call `ScriptRuntime::step_players` each frame after `step_humanoids`. Its first call gives the player a character: one already in the Workspace (the first controller with a body, as before) becomes `Player.Character`, and a copy of it (with no parent, so out of the game) is what they respawn as; otherwise, with `Players.CharacterAutoLoads` (true) on, a copy of the StarterCharacter spawns. `CharacterAdded(character)` fires either way. The examples without a character of their own turn `CharacterAutoLoads` off. A runtime whose host never calls `step_players` (library tests) spawns nothing. `character::player_character` prefers the controller of a Player's `Character`.
  - **StarterCharacter.** `StarterPlayer.StarterCharacter` names a prefab (`assets/prefabs/*.prefab.json`). The player and editor Play read it into StarterPlayer as a Model named `StarterCharacter` before scripts start (`project::load_starter_character`: a prefab of one Model is that Model, anything else is put in a new one); a bad path warns and the default is used. Empty is the character Insert ▸ Character makes (`character::rig::character`, with no state machine). The order is: that Model, else the placed character's copy, else the default.
  - **LoadCharacter and respawning.** `player:LoadCharacter()` (Scripts only) takes the old character out of the Workspace, fires `CharacterRemoving(old)` and destroys it, then copies the StarterCharacter (its scripts start once it's in), names it after the player, stands its feet (the bottom middle of its parts' boxes) on the top middle of a SpawnLocation and parents it to the Workspace, sets `Character` and fires `CharacterAdded`. When the Humanoid of a Player's `Character` dies, `Players.RespawnTime` (5) seconds later the player gets a new one the same way, while `CharacterAutoLoads` is on. A character with no Humanoid is never respawned.
  - **SpawnLocation.** A Part (`IsA("Part")`, Anchored and 12 × 1 × 12 by default) with `Enabled` (true), `Neutral` (true), `TeamColor` (white) and `Duration` (10). A player on a team spawns on an enabled SpawnLocation whose `Neutral` is off and whose `TeamColor` is their Team's (within a 255th per channel), else on an enabled Neutral one; a neutral player only on Neutral ones. One is chosen at random each time. With none, the character stands at the origin and Output warns (a placed character's copy stands where that one was, without a warning). For `Duration` seconds a `ForceField` sits in the character: a Health with a ForceField beside it takes no damage (TakeDamage, DamageZones, falls). Scripts can add their own ForceFields.
  - **Teams.** The `Teams` service (saved in the world file, `"teams"`) holds `Team`s with `TeamColor` and `AutoAssignable` (true). `team:GetPlayers()` lists the players on it.
  - **Leaderboard.** A child named `leaderstats` (usually a Folder) under a Player, holding IntValues, NumberValues, StringValues or BoolValues, shows on the default leaderboard at the top right: a row per Player (sorted by the first stat, highest first) and a column per stat, by name. Hosts draw it (`ui::leaderboard_items`) while `ScriptRuntime::leaderboard_shown` (Tab toggles it); `Players.LeaderboardEnabled` off hides it for a game's own. Its CSS is in [../ui.md](../ui.md).
  - Insert ▸ Spawn (SpawnLocation, on the ground at the insert point) and Insert ▸ Team (always into Teams), in the Explorer and the Model tab. Players' and StarterPlayer's settings are in their Properties. `examples/hello-spawn` has red and blue SpawnLocations, Red and Blue teams (only Red AutoAssignable), lava to die in and a coin that adds to `leaderstats.Coins`.
- **Third-person camera.** `CameraDistance` (12) is how far behind the character the camera sits, and `CameraHeight` (4) is how far above the feet it looks. Play starts behind the character, looking slightly down. Right-drag (the player: right button) orbits. The mouse wheel zooms, like Roblox: each notch toward the screen moves the camera a fifth of the distance closer (at least a stud), and away moves it out, between `CameraMinZoomDistance` (0.5) and `CameraMaxZoomDistance` (128). The minimum is never under 0.5 and the maximum never under the minimum; `CameraDistance` outside them is drawn at the nearer one. The wheel writes the zoom to `CameraDistance` in the Play world, so a script reads it and can set it; Stop puts back the saved value. The same number in both limits locks the distance: the wheel does nothing. Zoomed fully in, the camera is half a stud from the point it looks at, close to first person.
- **First-person camera.** The camera sits at the character's eyes, `EyeHeight` (4.5) above its feet over the middle of the body, and looks where the player looks: Play starts looking straight ahead, and right-drag (the player: right button) turns it. Walking goes where it faces. Crouching lowers the eyes as far as the top of the body drops. The wheel does nothing. Back faces aren't drawn, so the body doesn't block the view from inside it.
- **Both cameras.** The `Move` action moves: WASD, the arrow keys, or a controller's left stick, which walks slower pushed part way. The right stick turns the camera (the `Look` action), as the right mouse button does. Every key and button here comes from the input map (`input.json`; [client-server.md](client-server.md#input-phase-30)), with the player's rebinds, in the player and in the editor's Play (`MoveInput::from_actions`). During Play the editor fly camera is left where it was and returns on Stop. `workspace.CurrentCamera` reports the controller's view. A LocalScript can read it; a move it writes is not applied while a controller owns the camera.
- **Insert.** Model tab, Effects (Third Person or First Person), or Explorer Insert ▸ Third Person or First Person. If a Model, Part, or MeshPart is selected and no controller already moves it, the controller is parented there. Otherwise (nothing selected, or a character that already has a controller, which keeps it) the editor also inserts a Model named Character in the Workspace and a Part named Body (2×5×1).
- **Sample.** `examples/hello-character` hovers with H from a LocalScript (`hover.client.lua`, a custom `"Hover"` mode: E rises, Q sinks, W goes where the camera looks, at 12 studs a second) and clears it with H again; 6 and 7 set and clear the same mode from a block script, and 6 prints the mode it reads back. It switches with Shift from a LocalScript and with 1 and 2 from a block script, next to a falling crate. J jumps from the LocalScript and 3 from the block script. K crouches or stands from the LocalScript, and 4 and 5 crouch and stand from the block script. A green `LowCeiling` at x = 30 stops a standing character; crouched, it goes under, and stands again on the far side. Grey obstacles to the right (a 0.8-stud `LowStep`, and a 30° `Ramp` up to a `Platform`) are crossed; red ones to the left (a 2-stud `TallStep` and a 60° `SteepRamp`) stop the character.
