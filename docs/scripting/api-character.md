# Character controllers

A kinematic walk, run, jump and crouch, up slopes and steps within limits, and custom movement modes that scripts move.

- **ThirdPersonController** and **FirstPersonController** are separate classes, and a character has one or the other. They move the same way, with the same movement properties (everything below except Camera), from the same code in `character`; they differ only in their camera. Each saves under its own class name.
- A controller sits under a `Model`, `Part`, or `MeshPart`. The body is that part, or every `Part` and `MeshPart` inside the parent. The first controller of either class in the Workspace that has a body receives input and owns the camera. Others stay where they are.
- **Movement** is `"Walk"`, `"Run"` or `"Crouch"`, in any case. `"Crouch"` walks crouched, the same as setting `Crouch`. A value that isn't text walks. `WalkSpeed` is 16 studs a second and `RunSpeed` is 32, across flat ground, relative to where the camera looks (pitch does not change the speed). Set them from Lua (`controller.Movement = "Run"`), from the set-property block, or from Properties. Holding the `Run` action (LeftShift, or L3 on a controller) runs a walking character at `RunSpeed`; `Movement` stays as it is. Shift still slows the fly camera when no controller owns the view.
- **Custom modes.** Any other name in `Movement`, such as `"Swim"` or `"Hover"`, is a custom mode. While it's active the controller doesn't walk, run, jump, crouch, stand or fall the character: scripts and blocks move its parts (set `Position`, or the move-by block, each frame from `RunService.Heartbeat` or an every-frame block). A `Jump` asked for is used up and does nothing. Held keys, a held C and a change to `Crouch` are ignored until the mode is cleared; a crouched character stays crouched. The camera still follows the body and still turns and zooms. The parts are still anchored before physics. `Movement` reads back the mode's name. Setting `Movement` to `""` clears it: on its next step the controller writes back the built-in mode it last had (`"Walk"` if it never had one), so a script that reads `Movement` in the same frame it cleared it still sees `""`. The character then starts in the air for one step, as at the start of Play, so it lands where the script left it or falls from there, and jumping, steps and slopes work again. The remembered mode isn't saved.
- **Jump.** The `Jump` action (Space, or A on a controller) jumps during Play, and held on landing it jumps again. A script or the set-property block sets `Jump = true` to jump once; the controller sets it back to false on its next step, whether or not the character was on the ground, so a jump asked for in the air does nothing. `JumpHeight` (7.2 studs) is how high it rises under the default gravity (196.2 studs/s²). It falls by `workspace.Gravity`, launched at the same speed whatever that is, so halving the gravity doubles the jump (Phase 34). A ceiling stops the rise.
- **Crouch.** Holding the `Crouch` action (C, or B on a controller) crouches during Play. A script or the set-property block sets `Crouch = true` to crouch and `false` to stand; the character is crouched while either asks. Crouching squashes every body part toward the feet until the body is `CrouchHeight` (3 studs, at least 0.5) tall; a body already shorter keeps its size. The camera drops as far as the top did, and standing puts back each part's height and place. Walls, ceilings and steps use the crouched height, so the character fits under what it could not walk into standing. Standing is refused while anything solid is within the standing height above the middle of the body or the edges of its footprint; the character stays crouched and stands on the first step that's clear, without being asked again. While crouched, walk and run both move at `CrouchSpeedScale` (0.5) of `WalkSpeed` or `RunSpeed`. Jumping still works. The crouch is not saved: Play starts standing, and a world saved during Play is the one from before Play.
- **Ground, slopes and steps.** Movement goes in slices of at most half a stud, so nothing on the way is skipped. Rays from the middle of the body, at heights above `MaxStepHeight`, treat anything that isn't walkable ground as a wall: the move stops at it and slides along it. Ground is walkable when it's no steeper than `MaxSlopeAngle` (45°, at most 89). A face lower than `MaxStepHeight` (1 stud) is a step when walkable ground lies on top of it within that height; the character rises onto it as its front reaches it. A steeper slope or a taller step stops the character; it is never launched up one. Ground is looked for under the middle and the leading edge, so the character keeps to a ledge until its middle leaves it, follows a walkable slope down, and steps down a drop no taller than `MaxStepHeight`. Off a taller drop it falls under gravity and lands on whatever solid is below. Parts with `CanCollide` off are passed through. The character is not a physics body: its parts are anchored after scripts and before physics, so a script cannot drop them for that step, and other parts keep the same gravity and friction. Its fall and jump state isn't saved; Play starts it in the air for one step, which lands it.
- **Seats** (Phase 39). A `Seat` is a Part (`IsA("Part")`, the same properties and drawing, collision and editing) with `Occupant` (the controller sitting on it, or nil), `Disabled` (false) and `SeatOffset` (an Attachment where the feet go, or nil for the middle of the top face). A controller's character whose box comes within 0.1 studs of an empty Seat that isn't Disabled sits on it, unless its `Movement` is a custom mode; walking into a seat taller than a step is enough. `seat:Sit(controller)` sits it on any seat on the controller's next step, Disabled or not: it writes the seat's `Occupant` and the controller's `Sit`, takes the controller off any other seat, and whoever sat there stands up. `Occupant` is read-only from scripts. Sitting puts the feet on the sit point (the character doesn't turn), unanchors the body's parts and welds each to the seat with a `SeatWeld` WeldConstraint inside it, so physics carries the character with an unanchored seat, such as one welded to a cart (its weight joins the cart's; `keep_kinematic` leaves it unanchored). The controller's `Movement` reads `"Seated"` and `Sit` is true; move keys and `Crouch` do nothing, and the camera still turns and zooms. An Animator in the character whose state machine has a `Sit` state plays it, and on standing goes back to the state it was in (its starting state if it hadn't run yet); without one nothing is played or reported. The character stands up when Jump is pressed (or `Jump = true`), when `Sit` is set false, when the seat gets another `Occupant`, leaves the Workspace or is destroyed, or when a script sets `Movement` (which it keeps). Standing up removes the welds, clears `Occupant`, puts back the built-in mode it had, and places the character half a stud in front of the seat (the way its front face looks, along the ground) with its feet as high as the seat's top, from where it falls and lands. `Seated(active, seat)` fires on the controller after the controller's step: true and the Seat when it sits, false and the Seat it left when it stands. Blocks: "when someone sits on *seat*" and "make *controller* leave the seat" ([blocks.md](blocks.md)). Insert ▸ Seat, or the Model tab's Seat button. Sitting isn't saved. `examples/hello-seat` has a chair, a Disabled bench that B sits on from a LocalScript (L stands up), and a cart whose seat starts its drive from a block script for three seconds and then stands the rider up.
- **Third-person camera.** `CameraDistance` (12) is how far behind the character the camera sits, and `CameraHeight` (4) is how far above the feet it looks. Play starts behind the character, looking slightly down. Right-drag (the player: right button) orbits. The mouse wheel zooms, like Roblox: each notch toward the screen moves the camera a fifth of the distance closer (at least a stud), and away moves it out, between `CameraMinZoomDistance` (0.5) and `CameraMaxZoomDistance` (128). The minimum is never under 0.5 and the maximum never under the minimum; `CameraDistance` outside them is drawn at the nearer one. The wheel writes the zoom to `CameraDistance` in the Play world, so a script reads it and can set it; Stop puts back the saved value. The same number in both limits locks the distance: the wheel does nothing. Zoomed fully in, the camera is half a stud from the point it looks at, close to first person.
- **First-person camera.** The camera sits at the character's eyes, `EyeHeight` (4.5) above its feet over the middle of the body, and looks where the player looks: Play starts looking straight ahead, and right-drag (the player: right button) turns it. Walking goes where it faces. Crouching lowers the eyes as far as the top of the body drops. The wheel does nothing. Back faces aren't drawn, so the body doesn't block the view from inside it.
- **Both cameras.** The `Move` action moves: WASD, the arrow keys, or a controller's left stick, which walks slower pushed part way. The right stick turns the camera (the `Look` action), as the right mouse button does. Every key and button here comes from the input map (`input.json`; [client-server.md](client-server.md#input-phase-30)), with the player's rebinds, in the player and in the editor's Play (`MoveInput::from_actions`). During Play the editor fly camera is left where it was and returns on Stop. `workspace.CurrentCamera` reports the controller's view. A LocalScript can read it; a move it writes is not applied while a controller owns the camera.
- **Insert.** Model tab, Effects (Third Person or First Person), or Explorer Insert ▸ Third Person or First Person. If a Model, Part, or MeshPart is selected and no controller already moves it, the controller is parented there. Otherwise (nothing selected, or a character that already has a controller, which keeps it) the editor also inserts a Model named Character in the Workspace and a Part named Body (2×5×1).
- **Sample.** `examples/hello-character` hovers with H from a LocalScript (`hover.client.lua`, a custom `"Hover"` mode: E rises, Q sinks, W goes where the camera looks, at 12 studs a second) and clears it with H again; 6 and 7 set and clear the same mode from a block script, and 6 prints the mode it reads back. It switches with Shift from a LocalScript and with 1 and 2 from a block script, next to a falling crate. J jumps from the LocalScript and 3 from the block script. K crouches or stands from the LocalScript, and 4 and 5 crouch and stand from the block script. A green `LowCeiling` at x = 30 stops a standing character; crouched, it goes under, and stands again on the far side. Grey obstacles to the right (a 0.8-stud `LowStep`, and a 30° `Ramp` up to a `Platform`) are crossed; red ones to the left (a 2-stud `TallStep` and a 60° `SteepRamp`) stop the character.
