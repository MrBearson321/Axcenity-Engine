# Part properties

Transparency, collision, glow, wind, and Anchored.

- Parts (Phase 35) also have `Transparency` (0–1), `CanCollide` (off: passed through, still fires `Touched`), `EmissiveColor` and `Emission` (a glow of any color), and `TileSize` (studs one copy of a child SurfaceAppearance's textures covers, default 8; 0 leaves the coordinates unscaled). `WindAffected` (Part and MeshPart, default false) lets `workspace.GlobalWind` (a Vector3 in studs/s, default zero, saved in the world file only when set) push an unanchored part.
- **Bodies from scripts** (Phase 34, Part and MeshPart): `AssemblyLinearVelocity` and `AssemblyAngularVelocity` (read and write; physics updates them, setting throws or spins the part; radians/s for spin), `part:ApplyImpulse(impulse)` (velocity changes by impulse ÷ mass; several in one frame add up; anchored parts ignore them), `part:ApplyImpulseAtPosition(impulse, worldPosition)` (also spins it about its center of mass), `part:ApplyAngularImpulse(impulse)`, `part:GetMass()` (density × volume: a 1×1×1 Plastic block weighs 1), and the read-only `AssemblyMass`, `AssemblyCenterOfMass` (world position) and `CurrentPhysicalProperties`. `Massless` is saved but changes nothing until welds exist. Setting a read-only one is an error.
- **Physical properties** (Phase 34): `CustomPhysicalProperties` is nil (the Material's: Ice slippery, Rubber bouncy, Metal dense) or `PhysicalProperties.new(density, friction, elasticity, frictionWeight?, elasticityWeight?)` (weights 1 when left out; numbers are clamped to density 0.0001–100, friction 0–2, elasticity 0–1, weights 0–100). `PhysicalProperties.new(Enum.Material.Ice)` gives a material's. Its fields are `Density`, `Friction`, `Elasticity`, `FrictionWeight` and `ElasticityWeight`. `workspace.Gravity` (196.2 by default) pulls parts and the characters. How they're simulated: `docs/architecture/physics.md`. The Properties panel shows them under **Physics**, with a Custom box that starts from the material's numbers.
- `Part.Anchored` (default `false`): unanchored parts fall under physics. Setting `Position` or `Orientation` on one teleports it and stops it; physics writes both back as the part moves. See Physics in `docs/architecture/physics.md`.
