# Part properties

Transparency, collision, glow, wind, Anchored, welds and attachments.

- Parts (Phase 35) also have `Transparency` (0–1), `CanCollide` (off: passed through, still fires `Touched`), `EmissiveColor` and `Emission` (a glow of any color), and `TileSize` (studs one copy of a child SurfaceAppearance's textures covers, default 8; 0 leaves the coordinates unscaled). Phase 38: `CollisionGroup` (default `"Default"`; see `docs/architecture/physics.md`), `CanQuery` (off: scripts' queries pass it by) and `CanTouch` (off: no `Touched` or `TouchEnded`), on Parts and MeshParts, and the `TouchEnded` event, which fires on both parts when they stop touching. `WindAffected` (Part and MeshPart, default false) lets `workspace.GlobalWind` (a Vector3 in studs/s, default zero, saved in the world file only when set) push an unanchored part.
- **Bodies from scripts** (Phase 34, Part and MeshPart): `AssemblyLinearVelocity` and `AssemblyAngularVelocity` (read and write; physics updates them, setting throws or spins the part; radians/s for spin), `part:ApplyImpulse(impulse)` (velocity changes by impulse ÷ its assembly's mass; several in one frame add up; anchored parts ignore them), `part:ApplyImpulseAtPosition(impulse, worldPosition)` (also spins it about its center of mass), `part:ApplyAngularImpulse(impulse)`, `part:GetMass()` (density × volume: a 1×1×1 Plastic block weighs 1), and the read-only `AssemblyMass`, `AssemblyCenterOfMass` (world position) and `CurrentPhysicalProperties`. `AssemblyMass` and `AssemblyCenterOfMass` count every part welded to it except Massless ones (a part alone keeps its own mass). Setting a read-only one is an error.
- **Forces, drag and momentum** (cohesion pass CP6, Part and MeshPart): `part:ApplyForce(force, seconds?)` and `part:ApplyTorque(torque, seconds?)` are an impulse of force (or torque) times time, one physics step (1/60 s) if left out; call them every Heartbeat with its time to push steadily, or use a VectorForce or Torque. `AssemblyLinearMomentum` (read-only) is the assembly's mass times its velocity. `LinearDrag` (0) and `AngularDrag` (0.5) are air resistance on speed and spin (about that share lost each second: 1 takes away ~63%), `RollingFriction` (0) slows spin only while touching something; welded parts use their root part's. See `docs/architecture/physics.md`.
- **Physical properties** (Phase 34): `CustomPhysicalProperties` is nil (the Material's: Ice slippery, Rubber bouncy, Metal dense) or `PhysicalProperties.new(density, friction, elasticity, frictionWeight?, elasticityWeight?)` (weights 1 when left out; numbers are clamped to density 0.0001–100, friction 0–2, elasticity 0–1, weights 0–100). `PhysicalProperties.new(Enum.Material.Ice)` gives a material's. Its fields are `Density`, `Friction`, `Elasticity`, `FrictionWeight` and `ElasticityWeight`. `workspace.Gravity` (196.2 by default) pulls parts and the characters. How they're simulated: `docs/architecture/physics.md`. The Properties panel shows them under **Physics**, with a Custom box that starts from the material's numbers.
- `Part.Anchored` (default `false`): unanchored parts fall under physics. Setting `Position` or `Orientation` on one teleports it and stops it; physics writes both back as the part moves. See Physics in `docs/architecture/physics.md`.
- **Welds** (Phase 35): a `WeldConstraint` (`Part0`, `Part1`, `Enabled`, default on) holds two parts where they were when it was made or enabled; it can be parented anywhere. Welded parts are one assembly: they fall and tumble together, never touch each other, and share `AssemblyLinearVelocity`. An anchored part anchors its whole assembly. Turning `Enabled` off lets them move apart; on again welds them where they are then. Setting a welded part's `Position`, `Orientation` or `CFrame` moves that part alone and the weld holds it there. `part:GetConnectedParts(recursive?)` is the part, then the parts welded to it (with `true`, its whole assembly); `part:GetRootPart()` is the assembly's root: an anchored part, then one that isn't Massless, then the largest (the first made on a tie).
- **Joints** (Phase 36): `HingeConstraint`, `BallSocketConstraint`, `PrismaticConstraint`, `CylindricalConstraint`, `SpringConstraint`, `RopeConstraint` and `RodConstraint` (all `IsA("Constraint")`) join `Attachment0` to `Attachment1`, with `Enabled`, `Visible` (drawn in the game in `Color`) and their own properties: hinge `LimitsEnabled`, `LowerAngle`/`UpperAngle` (degrees), `ActuatorType` (`Enum.ActuatorType.None`, `Motor` or `Servo`, or their names), `AngularVelocity` (rad/s) with `MotorMaxTorque`, `TargetAngle` (degrees) with `AngularSpeed` (rad/s) and `ServoMaxTorque`; ball socket `LimitsEnabled` with `UpperAngle` (the cone) and `TwistLimitsEnabled` with `TwistLowerAngle`/`TwistUpperAngle`; prismatic `LimitsEnabled`, `LowerLimit`/`UpperLimit`, `ActuatorType`, `Velocity`/`MotorMaxForce`, `TargetPosition`/`Speed`/`ServoMaxForce`; cylindrical the prismatic's plus `AngularLimitsEnabled`, `LowerAngle`/`UpperAngle`, `AngularActuatorType` and the hinge's motor and servo; spring `Stiffness`, `Damping`, `FreeLength`, `MinLength`, `MaxLength`, and `Coils`, `Radius`, `Thickness` for drawing; rope `Length`, `WinchEnabled`, `WinchTarget`, `WinchSpeed`, `WinchForce`, `Thickness`; rod `Length`, `Thickness`. Max torques and forces default to 100000 (so motors and servos work without setting them; Roblox's are 0) and accept `math.huge`. Read-only `CurrentAngle` (hinge, cylindrical; degrees from -180 to 180), `CurrentPosition` (prismatic, cylindrical; studs along Attachment0's axis) and `CurrentLength` (spring, rope, rod) are where the attachments are now. An enum item property takes only its own enum's items or their names. How they simulate: `docs/architecture/physics.md`.
- **Breaking** (Phase 42): every joint and `WeldConstraint` has `BreakForce` and `BreakTorque` (0, never). Held together past either (force in mass × studs/s², so a 1×1×1 Plastic block weighs 196.2), it turns `Enabled` off and fires `Broken` once. A weld with either set holds its parts as two bodies, so they aren't one assembly (`GetConnectedParts` and `GetRootPart` don't follow it). How it's measured: `docs/architecture/physics.md`, Breaking.
- **Fracture** (Phase 42): put one in a Part or MeshPart and press Generate in its Properties. `Pieces`, `Seed`, `Pattern` (`Enum.FracturePattern.Uniform` or `Impact`) and `InteriorMaterial` shape the pieces, saved in `PiecesId` (`<name>.fracture.glb`). In the game, a hit faster than `Threshold` studs/s, or `fracture:Shatter(point?, force?)`, replaces the part with a Model of MeshPart pieces named `<part>Pieces` (the part's Parent becomes nil) flying from the point, and fires `Shattered`; they fade out and are destroyed after `DebrisLifetime` seconds (0 keeps them). `examples/hello-destruction` throws a crate through a glass pane and breaks a shelf. How it works: `docs/architecture/fracture.md`.
- **Movers** (Phase 37): `LinearVelocity` (`VectorVelocity` studs/s, `MaxForce`, `RelativeTo`), `AngularVelocity` (`AngularVelocity` rad/s, `MaxTorque`, `RelativeTo`), `VectorForce` (`Force`, `RelativeTo`, `ApplyAtCenterOfMass`), `Torque` (`Torque`, `RelativeTo`), `AlignPosition` (`Mode`, `Position`, `MaxForce`, `MaxVelocity`, `Responsiveness`, `RigidityEnabled`) and `AlignOrientation` (`Mode`, `CFrame`, `MaxTorque`, `MaxAngularVelocity`, `Responsiveness`, `RigidityEnabled`), all `IsA("Constraint")` with `Attachment0`, `Attachment1` and `Enabled`, push or turn Attachment0's part. `RelativeTo` is `Enum.ActuatorRelativeTo` (`World`, the default for the velocities, `Attachment0`, the default for the force and torque, or `Attachment1`); `Mode` is `Enum.PositionAlignmentMode` or `Enum.OrientationAlignmentMode` (`TwoAttachment`, the default, follows Attachment1; `OneAttachment` goes to `Position` or `CFrame`). Responsiveness defaults to 10 (about 1/10 s to close most of the gap). Max forces, torques and speeds default to 100000 and accept `math.huge`. A VectorForce of `part:GetMass() * workspace.Gravity` upward makes a part hover; LinearVelocity and AlignPosition count gravity themselves. How they simulate: `docs/architecture/physics.md`. Example: `examples/hello-movers`.
- **Attachments** (Phase 35): an `Attachment` under a part has `Position` and `Orientation` (and so `CFrame`) relative to the part, so it moves with it. The world-space `WorldCFrame`, `WorldPosition` and `WorldOrientation` can be read and set (setting moves it on the part); `Axis` and `SecondaryAxis` are its X and Y on the part (settable: the other stays as close as it can), `WorldAxis` and `WorldSecondaryAxis` the same in the world (read-only). Not on a part, its world is its own frame. A Model's `PivotTo` leaves its attachments' offsets alone.
- **Network ownership** (Phase 66, Scripts only): `part:SetNetworkOwner(player)` gives the part's whole assembly to that player's device to simulate (nil gives it back to the server), `part:GetNetworkOwner()` is the Player simulating it now or nil, `part:SetNetworkOwnershipAuto()` lets the engine choose (the player driving a VehicleSeat in it, else the nearest player whose character is within 40 studs, else the server) and `GetNetworkOwnershipAuto()` says whether it's chosen. Without a setting, an assembly with a VehicleSeat someone drives belongs to them, and the rest to the server. An anchored part has no owner (an error). In single-player everything is simulated on the one device. See `docs/architecture/networking.md`.
