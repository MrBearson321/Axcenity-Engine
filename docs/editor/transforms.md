# Transform tools and undo

Move, scale, rotate, snapping, drag-to-place, and undo.

- **Tools**: the Home tab's Tools group has Select, Move, Scale (labelled Resize), Rotate and Transform, in Studio's order. While editing (not typing, not during Play, not over the Model Studio), the plain keys **1** Select, **2** Move, **3** Resize, **4** Rotate and **5** Transform pick them, as in Studio (`Tool::key`), and **Escape** also goes back to Select (read, not taken, so it still closes a menu or stops the Constraint tool). Ctrl with a number and F3X's Z/X/C don't pick tools. With a terrain brush up, 1–5 are its tools instead (`terrain_keys` runs first). Select is the default. Every tool still selects on a plain click; a click that grabs a handle doesn't. Test: `keys_1_to_5_pick_the_tools_as_in_studio_and_ctrl_z_and_ctrl_y_undo_and_redo`.
- **Handles** (`crates/editor/src/gizmo.rs`), painted by egui over the viewport like the selection box, a fixed size on screen (90 points), red/green/blue for X/Y/Z, yellow when hovered or held:
  - Move: an arrow per **world** axis from the part's center. Arrows pointing almost straight at the camera are hidden.
  - Scale: a ball on each of the 6 faces, along the part's **own** axes. Dragging one moves that face, and the opposite face stays put (Position moves by half the change). Size stops at 0.001.
  - Rotate: a ring around each **world** axis through the center. A ring that faces the camera follows the mouse round: the part turns by how far the mouse has gone around the ring's middle, whatever path it takes and however far out it is, so circling the part turns it (`gizmo::ring_angle`, where the mouse ray meets the ring's plane). The mouse right by the middle (under 12% of the radius) holds the part still, as its angle means nothing there. A ring seen nearly edge-on (`FOLLOW_MIN_FACING`, chosen when it's grabbed) is too thin to say where around it the mouse is, so it turns by how far the mouse moves along it, at a steady `gizmo::TURN_PER_POINT` (one ring radius of travel is one radian). Hold **Shift** while dragging for fine turns (a quarter of the speed); only the movement while Shift is held is slowed, so pressing or letting go of it never jumps. The angle counts past ±180°, so you can keep turning. Tests: `a_ring_facing_the_camera_turns_as_far_as_the_mouse_goes_round_it`, `going_right_round_a_facing_ring_keeps_counting_past_a_whole_turn`, `the_middle_of_a_ring_holds_the_part_still`, `a_ring_seen_nearly_edge_on_turns_steadily_with_how_far_the_mouse_moves`, `shift_turns_a_quarter_as_fast_and_letting_go_does_not_jump` (`gizmo/tests.rs`).
  - **Transform** (`gizmo::transform_shapes`): all three at once, like Studio's. Rotate's rings and Resize's balls are where their own tools put them, and Move's arrows start at the rings and reach out to 1.4 times their radius (`TRANSFORM_ARROW_REACH`), so they don't run through the balls. Each handle (`gizmo::Shape`) carries the frame it was placed by, and a drag starts from the grabbed handle's (`gizmo::frame_of`). Its options panel shows the last selected part's **Position**, **Rotation** (Orientation, in degrees) and **Size** as X/Y/Z numbers to drag or type: a new Size or Rotation is set on every selected part, and a new Position moves the whole selection by as much (`ToolAction::MoveBy`, one "Move" undo step). Under them are the Axes and Pivot choices and both snap steps. Test: `transform_shows_every_kind_of_handle_and_each_drags_from_its_own_frame` (`gizmo/tests.rs`).
  - Phase 35 adds F3X's axes and pivot modes and multi-part handles (see Building tools).
  - **Attachments** (Phase 35, `constraints.rs`): a selected Attachment gets Move and Rotate handles at its place in the **world** (its part's CFrame times its offset; `viewport::selected_parts` gives it a half-stud box), and a drag's world values are turned back into its `Position` and `Orientation` on its part (`constraints::localize`) before they're applied. Resize leaves it alone. An Attachment whose part is moving too (both selected, or the part's Model) is left out, since it follows its part, so it never moves twice. Tests: `attachments_keep_their_offsets_when_the_part_moves_and_save_and_reload`, `moving_a_selected_attachment_moves_it_in_the_world_and_stores_its_offset_on_the_turned_part` (`app/constraint_tests.rs`).
- **Snapping**: the Home tab's Snapping group has "Move" (studs, also used by Scale) and "Rotate" (degrees), each with its own checkbox and step, both on at 1 stud and 15° by default. Steps count from where the drag started, so a part off the grid stays off it. Rotations are tidied to 3 decimals so 15° steps store as 15, not 14.999999. Tool and snap settings are preferences: they survive opening another project but aren't saved between sessions (like the fly speed).
- **Geometric snapping** (`snapping.rs`; the same group, like Studio's). Each part counts as its `Size` box turned by its `Orientation` (`snapping::Obb`; balls and cylinders by their box). When a drag starts, the viewport gathers every other part in the Workspace (`snapping::obstacles`; not Terrain), with whether it's solid (`CanCollide`) and visible (Transparency under 0.99).
  - **Surfaces** (on by default): a Move drag (Global or Last axes) lands flush against, or in line with, a face of a visible part in its way when it comes within 10 points on screen of one (`snapping::flush_moves`, `SNAP_POINTS`); that wins over the grid step. A Resize face (Normal directions) snaps flush the same way (`flush_faces`). Only faces square to the drag, of parts it would meet going that way (its box stretched along the drag, half a stud of slack), count, so nothing across the map pulls. Dragging a part by its body lines its faces up the same way along the two directions across a flat or upright surface (`placing.rs`).
  - **Collisions** (off by default): moves stop flush at solid parts instead of going into them, checked every quarter stud along the way so a fast drag can't pass a thin part (`limit_travel`); a resize stops growing at one; a turn sweeps from the last angle that fit, every 2°, and stops before the first that doesn't. A body drag waits where it last fit. Parts a part already overlaps when the drag starts don't hold it, so a stuck part can always be moved out, and resting flush on a surface never counts (overlaps under 0.01 stud are ignored, `PENETRATION`).
  - **Join Surfaces** (off by default): when a viewport drag is let go, each moved part is welded to the parts it's left touching (within 0.02 stud, not inside: `snapping::joins`), a WeldConstraint inside the moved part with Part0 the other, as one undo step "Join N surfaces" after the move's (`EditorApp::join_surfaces`). Parts already welded together aren't welded again.
  - **Snap Points** (on by default; `snapping/points.rs`): only once a part has left where it started, small markers show on the parts within about 50 points of it: squares for corners, diamonds for edge middles, dots for face middles and green rings for Attachments. A move clicks to the nearest pair within `SNAP_POINTS` of each other, which beats Surfaces and the grid step and is marked with a yellow ring. A handle stays on its line and a body drag stays on its surface (`Freedom`). A part's Attachments count, and move with it. The Constraint tool shows the points of the part under the mouse and a click near one lands exactly on it (`pull_to_point`). Local axes don't click, as each part goes its own way. Tests in `snapping/points.rs`, `with_snap_points_a_move_clicks_to_a_corner_near_it_and_reports_how_far_it_came` (`gizmo/tests.rs`), `with_snap_points_a_body_drag_clicks_a_corner_to_a_nearby_corner_and_stays_on_the_floor` (`placing.rs`).
  - **Collision Groups…** opens the Collision Groups window (see `docs/editor/shell.md`).
  - Tests: `snapping.rs`'s own, `with_surfaces_a_move_lands_flush_against_the_part_in_its_way`, `with_collisions_a_move_or_a_resize_stops_at_a_solid_part`, `with_collisions_a_turn_stops_where_it_meets_a_solid_part` (`gizmo/tests.rs`), `with_surfaces_it_lines_up_flush_with_a_wall_it_comes_near`, `with_collisions_it_waits_where_it_last_fit_instead_of_going_into_the_wall` (`placing.rs`), `join_surfaces_welds_a_moved_part_to_the_parts_it_is_left_touching_as_one_undo_step` (`app/constraint_tests.rs`).
- **While a part changes** (`viewport/move_overlay.rs`, from `DragReport`; Move, Resize, Rotate and Transform handles and body drags, once something has changed): the part stays solid as it started and is drawn see-through as it's becoming (`ghost_moving`, on the draw list, so nothing else sees it), with one number. **Move**: a thin white guide line through both, a white dot at the start's trailing face, a yellow ring at its leading face (or the point it has clicked to), and the straight-line distance in studs past the leading face. **Resize**: the same line through the face being pulled, and the studs it has come (negative when shrinking); with Normal directions the face clicks to snap points like a move. **Rotate**: an arc the size of the rings from where it began, and the degrees turned. A free-standing light's or emitter's icon shows Move's line and number. Shown with Snap Points off too; markers are what that switch hides. UI handles keep their own measures. The guide (line, markers, ring and number; not the see-through copy) can be hidden with **Viewport > Movement Guide** or Settings > Viewport > Show the movement guide (`EditorApp::move_guide`, saved between runs as `movement guide`, on by default); moving, snapping and the handles work the same with it off.
- **Measuring grid** (`viewport/grid.rs`; Test ▸ View ▸ Grid, the Viewport menu, Settings > Viewport, saved between runs, on by default, never in Play or a built game): on top of the Workspace's part named Baseplate, or else its widest flat part (at least 32 studs wide and deep), and stopping at its edges; with neither, on the ground at Y = 0, fading out around the camera. Lines every move step (1 stud with Move snapping off), a heavier one every 10, and the world's X and Z axes in red and blue. Lines are drawn by `render` (`DrawList::grid`, `renderer/grid.rs`), so parts hide them, and a set fades out before it gets too dense. The lines are soft: the surface's own color (its `Color`, moving to its `EmissiveColor` as `Emission` goes to 1) shifted partway to black on a bright surface or white on a dark one, so they keep its hue. Settings > Viewport has **Grid strength** (25–250%, saved between runs). Lines near the selected part, or else near the closest glowing part (`Emission` above 0 or a glowing Material, within 80 studs of the camera, looked for 8 times a second), take on its color and stand out a little (`grid::tint`, `GridPlane::tint`). The lines are always there; the numbers show only while a part is being moved or resized. They're printed along two rows crossing where the middle of the view meets the grid, spaced so they stay readable (the heavy line distance × 1, 2, 5, 10…), left out behind parts (a ray test, about 8 times a second) and where they'd overlap; a label in the corner says what a square is.
- **View cube** (`viewport/view_cube.rs`, made like Fusion 360's on request, with Roblox Studio's rounded edges and corners): while editing, a small shaded block (lit from above, shaded smoothly round its rounded rim; built as a grid of cells per face bent round by `ROUND`, convex so the cells facing the eye need no sorting, with an anti-aliased outline) in the viewport's top-right corner is turned like the world as the camera sees it, its faces named FRONT (+Z, so the starting view reads Front with Right on the right), BACK, LEFT, RIGHT, TOP and BOTTOM, with the X, Y and Z axes in red, green and blue running out of it (tips pointing away are dimmed and drawn under it). The names are printed on the faces: each glyph's corners are put on the face in 3D and seen in perspective (`paint_label`), made smaller to fit, fading in as a face turns toward the camera. Each face is split three by three: the middle looks from that face, an edge strip from that edge, a corner square from that corner, the rounded rim belonging to the strip or square beside it (26 views); what's under the mouse lights up on every face it's on. Clicking swings the camera smoothly round its focus point to look from there over 0.45 s, easing in and out the short way round, as far away as it was, at the same fly speed (`view_cube::Swing` toward `view_cube::looking_from`; straight down leans a hair toward -Z, and the camera stops at 89°); moving the camera yourself stops the swing, and with reduced motion it's instant. The house above it goes back to the starting angle; while a face is seen straight on, four arrows beside it (in place of the axis tips) turn to the face next to it. Hide it with Test > Viewport > View Cube, Viewport > View Cube or Settings (saved between runs, on by default). Tests in `view_cube.rs`.
- **Drag to place** (added to Phase 10 on request; `crates/editor/src/placing.rs`): with any tool, left-dragging a part by its body (not a handle) moves it like Studio. It starts only after egui's drag threshold, so a click still just selects, and grabbing an unselected part selects it. Each frame a ray from the mouse finds the nearest surface, skipping the dragged part (`scene::pick_hit`, which reports the surface normal). The part rests against that surface without turning: its center is lifted off the surface by its drawn box's reach along the normal (`scene::part_half_extent`), so it sits on floors, stacks on parts and goes beside walls. The grab point stays under the mouse sideways along the surface. Over empty sky it stays put. With Snap move on, the two coordinates along a flat or upright surface snap to the **world** grid (unlike the handles, which snap relative to the start); slopes and balls don't snap. One drag is one "Move" undo step. Balls and cylinders rest by their box, as in Studio.
- **Undo** (`crates/editor/src/history.rs`): every change goes through `EditorApp::change`, which snapshots the Instance's name and properties before and after and records the differences as one step. Side effects are recorded too (a new Material's Metalness and Roughness), and undo restores Material first so they come back right. Changes merge into the open step while they edit the same field of the same Instance, until the gesture ends: the mouse is let go (a handle, a number drag, the color picker) and no text box has focus (typing a name or a number). So one drag or one typed name is one step. A drag that ends where it started leaves no step. Creating, renaming, duplicating or deleting a clip, an animator controller or a prefab is one file step (`History::record_asset`): `None` is a missing file, so undoing a delete writes the contents back. Renaming a clip rewrites controllers that play it in that step; renaming a controller also updates Animators whose `Controller` was that path. The Assets panel and the Animation library list the file again without reopening the project. Saving still only remembers which step was on top.
- **Shortcuts**: Edit > Undo/Redo name the step ("Undo Move Pad"). **Edit > Undo History** (usability pass, `menus.rs` `undo_history`) lists the steps, newest first (25 of each at most), with the undone ones dimmed above them: clicking a step undoes it and every step after it, clicking a dimmed one redoes up to it, each one step at a time as Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y do (`EditorApp::jump_in_history`; test `undo_history_jumps_back_and_forward_several_steps`). Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redo (Cmd on macOS). While a text box has focus, Ctrl+Z goes to the text box instead. Undo is ignored mid-drag.
- **Saving keeps the history.** It only remembers which step was on top, so undoing back to that point counts as saved (no `*`), and undoing past it is unsaved again. The history isn't written to disk, and opening a project starts a new one.
