# Render pipeline

What the renderer draws, materials, built-in shapes, and which platforms it runs on.

`render` takes a **draw list** (meshes, materials, transforms, lights, camera, UI draw data) and draws it with `wgpu`. It has no knowledge of Instances, scripts, physics or the editor. The editor viewport and the player both feed it the same way.

Draw list conventions: Y up, right-handed, and colors as sRGB from 0 to 1 (the renderer converts them to linear). Built-in shapes are 1×1×1 at the origin, so a part's transform is translation × rotation × scale(Size). The Instance-to-draw-list step lives in the `scene` crate (`build_draw_list`), shared by the player and the editor. UI entries are pixel-positioned rectangles, images and shaped text; the renderer draws them over the 3D scene, in the list's order. It batches them into layers (`ui_pass::ui_layers`): each layer draws its rectangles, then its images, then its text (one glyphon `TextRenderer` per layer with text, kept in a pool), and a new layer starts only where an item would otherwise go under an earlier image or text it overlaps (text boxes count their outline and shadow reach). So a Frame over a TextLabel covers it, and a loading screen at the end of the list covers all of the game's UI, while UI that doesn't overlap stays one layer.

- `Renderer` draws into a window surface it creates itself (the player).
- `TextureRenderer` draws into a texture on a GPU device someone else created (the editor, where eframe owns the device and egui shows the texture). Both share the private `ScenePipeline`, so they draw identically.
- **Frame cost** (v2 Phase 5, graphics Phase 11; for the editor's Profiler and the benchmarks): `TextureRenderer::stats` gives the last frame's draw calls, triangles, items submitted and drawn, batches, GPU bytes by kind, and a recent GPU time for the frame and each `FramePass`. GPU time needs `Features::TIMESTAMP_QUERY`, which only the editor (`main.rs`) and the benchmarks ask for, where the adapter has it; the player never does, so it never times anything. `renderer/gpu_timer.rs` writes a timestamp with an empty compute pass before each pass and after the last, resolves them into a buffer, and reads it with `map_async` once the GPU is done, a frame or two later; a frame isn't timed while the previous reading is still being read, so the CPU never waits for the GPU. Details and the baseline: [performance.md](performance.md).
- **Levels of detail** (graphics Phase 13; `renderer/lod.rs`): before uploading, `LodStates::resolve` turns each draw item into the copies the frame draws (`Draw`). A caller's mesh with simpler copies (`MeshSource::lod_count`, `lod`) is drawn with one by its size on screen: its unit-cube bounding sphere's share of the screen's height, so all the parts of one model switch together. Below 0.3 it draws its first copy, below 0.12 its second (`LOD_SCREEN_SIZES`). The level is kept between frames by `DrawItem::id` and mesh key (`scene` hashes the Instance's ID; foliage models hash the Terrain's and their index), and changes only once the size is 15% past a threshold, so an item on one doesn't flicker. A switch cross-fades over 12 frames: both levels draw, and `dithered_out` in shader.wgsl (scene and prepass) leaves out complementary pixels by interleaved noise, so each pixel shows exactly one (the fade is `InstanceRaw::emissive_fade.w`: 1 whole, below 1 fading in, above 1 fading out). Only the level fading in casts a shadow. An item with ID 0 picks its level afresh each frame. Only the levels drawn are uploaded. `DrawItem::cull_distance` (painted plants, by kind and scale: grass 120 studs, flowers 150, bushes 300, trees 800; painted models by their size, [../terrain.md](../terrain.md)) leaves an item out past that distance from the camera, counted in `items_culled`, and fades it out by the same dither over the last tenth. `scene` already leaves out painted foliage past it when building the list, so what reaches the renderer is near enough to be drawn or shadowed. The editor can force one item's level (`TextureRenderer::preview_lod`, the LOD Inspector's preview) and ask the level an item was last drawn at (`lod_level_of`); the inspector, the LOD Levels legend and what the system does and doesn't do are in [performance.md](performance.md) (Levels of detail, inspected).
- **Frustum culling** (graphics Phase 12; `renderer/culling.rs`): each view is a view-projection matrix, turned into six planes (`Frustum::of`); a thing is culled when its bounding sphere is wholly outside one (`Frustum::touches`; a sphere that isn't a number is never culled). An item's sphere comes from its mesh's box in its own space and its transform (`Bounds::sphere`): the unit cube for built-in shapes, and for a caller's mesh the box its corners span, worked out at upload and again when a mesh's corners are rewritten (`GpuMesh::bounds`), so terrain chunks (placed by their corners, not their transform) are bounded right; a skinned item's box is its joints' boxes as its pose moves them (`ScenePipeline::item_bounds`, see GPU skinning in [animation.md](animation.md)), so raised arms are too. `plan_draws` batches only the items the camera sees, and lists every solid shadow caster in view or not (`DrawPlan::casters`), since one out of view can still shadow what's in it. Each shadow view then copies the casters that reach it after the scene's instances (`lighting::casters_in_views`): each cascade's box (which already reaches 500 studs back toward the sun), and each shadowing spot light's view and beam sphere. Sprites are culled against the camera too (`SpritePass::prepare`). `RenderStats` counts `items_culled`, `shadow_casters_drawn` and `shadow_casters_culled` (summed over views) and `sprites_culled`. For the editor's **Culling** overlay, `TextureRenderer::freeze_culling(Some(camera))` culls as if from that camera and keeps each item's sphere and whether it was drawn (`culling_bounds`).
- The texture is `Rgba8Unorm` holding sRGB-encoded colors, because that's what egui 0.36 expects of native textures. Drawing goes through an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` view so the GPU encodes the shader's linear output. Sampling an sRGB texture would hand egui linear values and everything would look far too dark.
- **Known issue (found in Phase 8):** on at least one driver (AMD, Vulkan, Windows), the background clear through an sRGB view is *not* sRGB-encoded while shaded pixels are. The player's window surface behaves the same way, so on that machine the sky shows as `(90, 154, 227)` instead of the intended `(161, 204, 242)`. Other drivers may encode it, so the sky color can differ between machines. Fix when the sky becomes a drawn pass (stage 4), or sooner by drawing the background with a full-screen triangle instead of a clear.

Every material carries: base color, metalness, roughness, emissive, opacity. Presets like plastic, metal, glass and neon are just preset values for these fields. Don't add stage-specific material fields that a later stage would have to undo.

How Part properties map onto these (decided in Phase 3):

- `Color`, `Metalness` and `Roughness` are Part properties and feed the draw list directly.
- `Material` (Plastic, SmoothPlastic, Wood, WoodPlanks, Marble, Granite, Concrete, Slate, Brick, Fabric, Sand, Metal, Glass, Neon, Ice, Rubber) is a preset. Choosing it sets `Metalness` and `Roughness` to the preset's values, which you can then adjust (for example, a rougher Metal). See `Material::preset()` in `core`. Wood through Sand also have a look (`scene/src/surfaces.rs`): tiling color, normal and roughness maps made from noise the first time a part of that material is drawn, in `scene` so the editor and the player match, tiled by the part's `TileSize`; the part's `Color` tints the color map, and a SurfaceAppearance or `MaterialVariant` replaces them. A part's `MaterialVariant` (a material file) replaces the preset; see `docs/rendering/models.md`.
- Glass is drawn see-through, and Neon gives off its `Color` as emissive light instead of being shaded.
- `Transparency` (Phase 35, Parts and MeshParts, 0 to 1) multiplies the preset's (or the model's) opacity by `1 - Transparency`; a fully transparent part isn't drawn at all, but can still be picked in the editor.
- `EmissiveColor` and `Emission` (Phase 35, Parts) add `EmissiveColor × Emission` to the emissive light on top of any Neon glow, so any material can glow in any color; above 1 it blooms.
- Metals reflect the sky through environment lighting (stage 4).
- Joint constraints with `Visible` on (Phase 36, `scene/src/constraints.rs`) are drawn in their `Color` from built-in cylinders: a spring as a coil of 12 pieces a turn (`Coils`, `Radius`, `Thickness`), a rope, rod or prismatic as a cylinder between its attachments, a hinge or cylindrical as a short axle along its axis, a ball socket as a ball. They cast no shadow.

Transparency: opaque items are drawn first. Then see-through items are drawn, farthest to nearest by their centers, with blending on and depth writes off. Overlapping see-through items can still blend in the wrong order; that's accepted for now.

Built-in primitives are block, sphere, cylinder and wedge (a Part shaped `Capsule` is drawn as a cylinder between two spheres, `scene::draw_list::part_pieces`, Phase 41), plus imported glTF and FBX models (MeshParts, Phase 17; see Imported models). Part shape rules match Roblox: a Ball's diameter is the smallest of its `Size` axes, a Cylinder lies along X (`Size.X` is its length, its diameter is the smaller of Y and Z), and a Wedge's slope rises from the bottom front (-Z) edge to the top back (+Z) edge. `Orientation` is degrees, applied around Z, then X, then Y.

Each stage must leave existing scenes rendering correctly. `examples/hello-graphics` is the graphics reference scene: plastic, metal, glass and Neon parts, a textured PBR MeshPart, a SurfaceAppearance block with `TileSize`, a stretched wedge, a point and a spot light in lamp parts, sun shadows in all three cascades and an emissive part that blooms. Its `reference.png` is the whole 480×270 frame, and the player's `every_example_renders` test fails if the frame drifts from it by 6 or more per channel on average (an example's `reference.png` may also be just its top-left corner, as hello-css's is). After a deliberate change to the look, save the new frame by running that test with `AXCENITY_SNAPSHOTS` set to a folder, check `example-hello-graphics.png` by eye, and copy it over the reference. The render tests also check that draw-list colors survive the sRGB→linear→sRGB round trip, and that a stretched block shades like a unit one. Each graphics preset has its own reference of the same frame ([settings.md](settings.md)); `reference.png` is High's.

`wgpu` backends: DirectX 12 or Vulkan on Windows, Metal on macOS, Vulkan on Linux. Avoid features that aren't available on all three.
