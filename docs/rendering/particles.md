# Sprites and particles

How particle emitters become sprites.

- `DrawList::sprites` holds `Sprite { position, size, rotation_degrees, color, opacity, brightness, light_emission, texture }`: camera-facing pictures, unlit and unshadowed. `renderer/sprites.rs` and `sprite.wgsl` draw them last in the HDR scene pass, one instanced 6-corner quad each, turned to face the camera, depth-tested against solid items without writing depth, and sorted farthest first. Sprites whose turned square can't reach the camera's view are left out (`RenderStats::sprites_culled`). The color is premultiplied by opacity and the alpha written is `opacity × (1 − light_emission)` with a `One, OneMinusSrcAlpha` blend, so light emission 0 paints over what's behind and 1 adds light. Runs of the same texture are one draw call; `None` (or a texture that couldn't be uploaded) is a plain square. Brightness above 1 blooms.
- **Weather**: the host's `Particles` also keeps the world's weather and lets its rain or snow fall around the camera (`Particles::add_weather`). See [weather.md](weather.md).
- **Wind**: an emitter with `WindAffectsDrag` slows its particles toward `Workspace.GlobalWind` instead of to a stop (`v = wind + (v − wind) / (1 + Drag·dt)`), so it needs a `Drag` above 0, as in Roblox. The editor's viewport applies the wind while editing too.
- `scene::Particles` (owned by the player and by the editor's viewport, which shows emitters while editing too) turns ParticleEmitters into sprites: it gives off and moves particles each frame in world space (at most 4000 per emitter, 1000 births per frame) from inside the emitter's part (or its `Shape`, below), or, for one not in a part, from a 0.1-stud box at its own `Position` turned by its `Orientation` (`scene::mount_of`, `FREE_EMITTER_SIZE`), and `sprites` reads each emitter's look. Hosts call `Particles::draw`, which adds the sprites and the GPU emitters below to the draw list. The six built-in pictures (`ParticleType`) are drawn in code (64×64, white, shape in the alpha) and shared (`scene::built_in_particle`). A `Texture` is loaded through `Assets` like any texture; Kenney's CC0 Particle Pack images work as they are (white shapes on transparency).

## GPU particles

Emitters with many particles move them on the GPU (graphics Phase 36). The properties and the look stay the same.

- **When.** The host calls `Particles::set_gpu` each frame with its renderer's `Capabilities::compute_shaders`. It's off by default, so tests and the headless player stay on the CPU. With it on, an emitter whose `Rate × Lifetime` reaches `scene::GPU_PARTICLE_THRESHOLD` (3000, from the benchmark in [performance.md](performance.md)) moves on the GPU. It stays there until that falls below half. An `Emit` of the threshold or more goes there too. Without compute shaders, every emitter stays on the CPU with its 4000 cap.
- **Who does what.** `scene` still decides births each step, with the same Rate and owed fraction. It tells the renderer a `render::ParticleEmitter`: its key (new each time an emitter goes to the GPU), a step counter, the seconds, the births so far and this step's, the spawn transform, direction, spread, speed, lifetime, acceleration, drag, the air (wind when `WindAffectsDrag`), and a `ParticleLook` read when drawn. It keeps only each step's birth count, to estimate `count()` and `is_active` (nothing is read back). An emitter leaves the GPU when its last particles there should have died.
- **The renderer** (`renderer/gpu_particles.rs`, `particles.wgsl`) keeps each emitter's particles in a ring of `capacity` slots. Birth `n` goes in slot `n % capacity`. The capacity is a power of two, from 1024 up to `MAX_GPU_PARTICLES` (2¹⁸). It fits `Rate × Lifetime × 1.2` and only grows, and a new capacity starts the emitter empty. Each frame one compute pass (`FramePass::Particles`) runs three steps per emitter. `simulate` gives births a hashed random spot, heading and life, and moves the others as the CPU does. A bitonic `sort` puts them farthest first. `write` turns them into `SpriteRaw`s, and dead or invisible particles become squares of no size. The sprite pipeline draws that buffer as it is. Drawing the same step again (a second view) moves nothing. A frame that isn't drawn doesn't move them.
- **Order.** Each emitter's particles are sorted. Whole emitters are drawn farthest first by their part's position, before the list's CPU sprites (so nearby rain and snow go over them).
- **Cost.** Buffers exist only for emitters in the frame's list, and the whole of it only while there's one (`RenderStats::gpu_particles` counts slots). Each slot is 88 bytes.

## Curves over life

An emitter's look can follow a curve over each particle's life instead of blending its start and end values (roadmap R17.1, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Values.** `NumberSequence` (2 to 20 keypoints of time 0–1, value and envelope) and `ColorSequence` (keypoints of time and `Color3`) are `Value` types in `core/src/sequence.rs`, so saves, world files (`[[time, value, envelope], ...]` and `[[time, r, g, b], ...]`), replication, undo and prefabs carry them. Keypoints start at 0, end at 1 and are in time order; anything else is refused with a message. Lua builds them with `NumberSequence.new(n)`, `NumberSequence.new(a, b)` or `NumberSequence.new({NumberSequenceKeypoint.new(t, v, e), ...})`, and the same for `ColorSequence`.
- **Properties.** `SizeOverLife`, `TransparencyOverLife` (NumberSequence) and `ColorOverLife` (ColorSequence) are empty by default. Empty (or `nil` in Lua) means the pair (`Size`/`SizeEnd` and so on) blends as before, so older projects and scripts don't change. Set, the curve is used instead, and Properties greys out the pair. Properties draws each curve or gradient with a row per keypoint (time, value, ± envelope), adds a keypoint in the widest gap and clears the curve.
- **One table on both paths.** `render::ParticleCurves` samples an emitter's curves into 32 evenly spaced entries (`CURVE_ENTRIES`): size and its envelope, transparency and its envelope, and color. `scene` builds it only when a curve is set (`ParticleLook::curves`), the CPU path's `sprites` reads it, and the GPU emitter uniform carries the same entries for `write` in `particles.wgsl`, which blends the two nearest. An envelope is a per-particle spread: each particle gets two fixed numbers in −1..1 from a hash of its life (and its slot on the GPU) and adds that much of the envelope. The GPU particle struct stays 88 bytes; each GPU emitter's uniform grows by 1 KB.
- **Tests.** `scene`'s `particles_follow_their_curves_over_life_and_the_pairs_when_unset` and the renderer's `gpu_particles_draw_their_curves_in_place_of_the_start_and_end_values` check both paths against the same samples.

## Spawn shapes and speed over life

Where particles are born and how their speed changes over life (roadmap R17.2, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Properties.** `Shape` (`Enum.ParticleEmitterShape`: `Box`, `Sphere`, `Cylinder`, `Disc`) and `ShapeStyle` (`Enum.ParticleEmitterShapeStyle`: `Volume`, `Surface`) pick where in the part (or a free emitter's 0.1-stud box) particles start. Each shape fills the part's box and stretches with it: a sphere in a 4 × 1 × 2 part is an ellipsoid, a cylinder stands along the part's Y, and a disc lies across the part's middle, facing its Y. `Surface` keeps them to the shape's outside: a box's six faces (picked by their area), a sphere's skin, a cylinder's round side or a disc's rim. The defaults (`Box`, `Volume`) give off from anywhere in the part as before. `SpeedOverLife` (NumberSequence, empty by default) scales how far particles move each step at their age; its envelope isn't used yet. Direction, acceleration and drag are unchanged.
- **One sum on both paths.** `render::SpawnShape::point` turns three random numbers into a point in the centered unit cube, and `spawn_point` in `particles.wgsl` is the same sum, fed the same three randoms in the same order (a box's volume uses them as before). The GPU emitter uniform carries the shape as `spawn_shape`. `SpeedOverLife` is sampled into `ParticleCurves::speed`, a 32-entry table read with `render::curve_at` on the CPU and `speed_at` on the GPU (the `w` of each color entry), at the particle's age at the start of the step.
- **Not yet.** Bursts, `VelocityInheritance`, flipbooks and heading out from the shape's middle are the next stage (R17.2b).
- **Tests.** The renderer's `spawn_shapes_stay_inside_their_shape_or_on_its_surface` checks every shape's bounds and the box surface's split by area; `gpu_particles_are_born_in_their_spawn_shape` and `gpu_particles_move_at_their_speed_curve` draw a disc's rim and a half-speed curve on the GPU, and `scene`'s `particles_are_born_in_their_shape_and_move_at_their_speed_curve` checks the CPU's sphere surface and speed.
