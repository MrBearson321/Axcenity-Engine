# Sprites and particles

How particle emitters become sprites.

- `DrawList::sprites` holds `Sprite { position, size, rotation_degrees, color, opacity, brightness, light_emission, texture }`: camera-facing pictures, unlit and unshadowed. `renderer/sprites.rs` and `sprite.wgsl` draw them last in the HDR scene pass, one instanced 6-corner quad each, turned to face the camera, depth-tested against solid items without writing depth, and sorted farthest first. Sprites whose turned square can't reach the camera's view are left out (`RenderStats::sprites_culled`). The color is premultiplied by opacity and the alpha written is `opacity × (1 − light_emission)` with a `One, OneMinusSrcAlpha` blend, so light emission 0 paints over what's behind and 1 adds light. Runs of the same texture are one draw call; `None` (or a texture that couldn't be uploaded) is a plain square. Brightness above 1 blooms.
- **Weather**: the host's `Particles` also keeps the world's weather and lets its rain or snow fall around the camera (`Particles::add_weather`). See [weather.md](weather.md).
- **Wind**: an emitter with `WindAffectsDrag` slows its particles toward `Workspace.GlobalWind` instead of to a stop (`v = wind + (v − wind) / (1 + Drag·dt)`), so it needs a `Drag` above 0, as in Roblox. The editor's viewport applies the wind while editing too.
- `scene::Particles` (owned by the player and by the editor's viewport, which shows emitters while editing too) turns ParticleEmitters into sprites: it gives off and moves particles each frame in world space (at most 4000 per emitter, 1000 births per frame) from inside the emitter's part (or its `Shape`, below), or, for one not in a part, from a 0.1-stud box at its own `Position` turned by its `Orientation` (`scene::mount_of`, `FREE_EMITTER_SIZE`), and `sprites` reads each emitter's look. Hosts call `Particles::draw`, which adds the sprites and the GPU emitters below to the draw list. The six built-in pictures (`ParticleType`) are drawn in code (64×64, white, shape in the alpha) and shared (`scene::built_in_particle`). A `Texture` is loaded through `Assets` like any texture; Kenney's CC0 Particle Pack images work as they are (white shapes on transparency).

## GPU particles

Emitters with many particles move them on the GPU (graphics Phase 36). The properties and the look stay the same.

- **When.** The host calls `Particles::set_gpu` each frame with its renderer's `Capabilities::compute_shaders`. It's off by default, so tests and the headless player stay on the CPU. With it on, an emitter whose `Rate × Lifetime` reaches `scene::GPU_PARTICLE_THRESHOLD` (3000, from the benchmark in [performance.md](performance.md)) moves on the GPU. It stays there until that falls below half. An `Emit` of the threshold or more goes there too. Without compute shaders, every emitter stays on the CPU with its 4000 cap.
- **Who does what.** `scene` still decides births each step, with the same Rate and owed fraction. It tells the renderer a `render::ParticleEmitter`: its key (new each time an emitter goes to the GPU), a step counter, the seconds, the births so far and this step's, the spawn transform, direction, spread, speed, lifetime, acceleration, drag, the air (wind when `WindAffectsDrag`), and a `ParticleLook` read when drawn. It keeps only each step's birth count, to estimate `count()` and `is_active` (nothing is read back). An emitter leaves the GPU when its last particles there should have died.
- **The renderer** (`renderer/gpu_particles.rs`, `particles.wgsl`) keeps each emitter's particles in a ring of `capacity` slots. Birth `n` goes in slot `n % capacity`. The capacity is a power of two, from 1024 up to `MAX_GPU_PARTICLES` (2¹⁸). It fits `Rate × Lifetime × 1.2` and only grows, and a new capacity starts the emitter empty. Each frame one compute pass (`FramePass::Particles`) runs three steps per emitter. `simulate` gives births a hashed random spot, heading and life, and moves the others as the CPU does. A bitonic `sort` puts them farthest first. `write` turns them into `SpriteRaw`s, and dead or invisible particles become squares of no size. The sprite pipeline draws that buffer as it is. Drawing the same step again (a second view) moves nothing. A frame that isn't drawn doesn't move them.
- **Order.** Each emitter's particles are sorted. Whole emitters are drawn farthest first by their part's position, before the list's CPU sprites (so nearby rain and snow go over them).
- **Cost.** Buffers exist only for emitters in the frame's list, and the whole of it only while there's one (`RenderStats::gpu_particles` counts slots). Each slot is 104 bytes (a particle is 48: position and age, velocity and life, and seconds since it last met a surface).

## Curves over life

An emitter's look can follow a curve over each particle's life instead of blending its start and end values (roadmap R17.1, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Values.** `NumberSequence` (2 to 20 keypoints of time 0–1, value and envelope) and `ColorSequence` (keypoints of time and `Color3`) are `Value` types in `core/src/sequence.rs`, so saves, world files (`[[time, value, envelope], ...]` and `[[time, r, g, b], ...]`), replication, undo and prefabs carry them. Keypoints start at 0, end at 1 and are in time order; anything else is refused with a message. Lua builds them with `NumberSequence.new(n)`, `NumberSequence.new(a, b)` or `NumberSequence.new({NumberSequenceKeypoint.new(t, v, e), ...})`, and the same for `ColorSequence`.
- **Properties.** `SizeOverLife`, `TransparencyOverLife` (NumberSequence) and `ColorOverLife` (ColorSequence) are empty by default. Empty (or `nil` in Lua) means the pair (`Size`/`SizeEnd` and so on) blends as before, so older projects and scripts don't change. Set, the curve is used instead, and Properties greys out the pair. Properties draws each curve or gradient with a row per keypoint (time, value, ± envelope), adds a keypoint in the widest gap and clears the curve.
- **One table on both paths.** `render::ParticleCurves` samples an emitter's curves into 32 evenly spaced entries (`CURVE_ENTRIES`): size and its envelope, transparency and its envelope, and color. `scene` builds it only when a curve is set (`ParticleLook::curves`), the CPU path's `sprites` reads it, and the GPU emitter uniform carries the same entries for `write` in `particles.wgsl`, which blends the two nearest. An envelope is a per-particle spread: each particle gets two fixed numbers in −1..1 from a hash of its life (and its slot on the GPU) and adds that much of the envelope. The GPU particle slot stayed 88 bytes then (104 since R17.4b); each GPU emitter's uniform grows by 1 KB.
- **Tests.** `scene`'s `particles_follow_their_curves_over_life_and_the_pairs_when_unset` and the renderer's `gpu_particles_draw_their_curves_in_place_of_the_start_and_end_values` check both paths against the same samples.

## Spawn shapes and speed over life

Where particles are born and how their speed changes over life (roadmap R17.2, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Properties.** `Shape` (`Enum.ParticleEmitterShape`: `Box`, `Sphere`, `Cylinder`, `Disc`) and `ShapeStyle` (`Enum.ParticleEmitterShapeStyle`: `Volume`, `Surface`) pick where in the part (or a free emitter's 0.1-stud box) particles start. Each shape fills the part's box and stretches with it: a sphere in a 4 × 1 × 2 part is an ellipsoid, a cylinder stands along the part's Y, and a disc lies across the part's middle, facing its Y. `Surface` keeps them to the shape's outside: a box's six faces (picked by their area), a sphere's skin, a cylinder's round side or a disc's rim. The defaults (`Box`, `Volume`) give off from anywhere in the part as before. `SpeedOverLife` (NumberSequence, empty by default) scales how far particles move each step at their age; its envelope isn't used yet. Direction, acceleration and drag are unchanged.
- **One sum on both paths.** `render::SpawnShape::point` turns three random numbers into a point in the centered unit cube, and `spawn_point` in `particles.wgsl` is the same sum, fed the same three randoms in the same order (a box's volume uses them as before). The GPU emitter uniform carries the shape as `spawn_shape`. `SpeedOverLife` is sampled into `ParticleCurves::speed`, a 32-entry table read with `render::curve_at` on the CPU and `speed_at` on the GPU (the `w` of each color entry), at the particle's age at the start of the step.
- **Headings.** Since R17.2b, round shapes (`Sphere`, `Cylinder`, `Disc`) send particles away from their middle instead of along `Direction`; see below.
- **Tests.** The renderer's `spawn_shapes_stay_inside_their_shape_or_on_its_surface` checks every shape's bounds and the box surface's split by area; `gpu_particles_are_born_in_their_spawn_shape` and `gpu_particles_move_at_their_speed_curve` draw a disc's rim and a half-speed curve on the GPU, and `scene`'s `particles_are_born_in_their_shape_and_move_at_their_speed_curve` checks the CPU's sphere surface and speed.

## Bursts, inherited velocity and headings

Bursts, a moving part's velocity and which way particles head from their shape (roadmap R17.2b, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Bursts.** `Bursts` (NumberSequence, empty by default) lists bursts over a loop of `Duration` seconds (default 1, at least 0.05): each keypoint's time is how far through the loop it fires, its value how many particles it gives off and its envelope a random ± spread on that count (rounded, never below 0). A keypoint at time 1 never fires, so the curve's required end point can be a count of 0. Bursts add to `Rate`, loop while the emitter is `Enabled` and start over from the loop's beginning when it's turned on again. `scene`'s `Settings::bursts_between` counts the bursts whose time falls in a step (at or after its start, before its end), wrapping into the next loop. A burst of `GPU_PARTICLE_THRESHOLD` or more goes to the GPU, as a big `Emit` does.
- **Inherited velocity.** `VelocityInheritance` (0 to 1, default 0) adds that share of the emitter's own velocity to each new particle. `scene` works the velocity out from how far the emitter's spawn point moved since the last step, so it covers parts moved by physics, scripts and tweens alike, and an emitter that jumps (a new part, the first step) starts at rest. The GPU emitter uniform carries it as `inherit`.
- **Headings.** `ShapeInOut` (`Enum.ParticleEmitterShapeInOut`: `Outward`, `Inward`, `InAndOut`) picks which way particles head. A `Box` heads along `Direction`, and `Inward` turns that around. Round shapes head away from their middle (a sphere's center, a cylinder's or disc's axis), inside the part's turn and stretch, with `SpreadAngle` around it; `Inward` heads toward the middle and `InAndOut` picks either at random per particle. A particle born at the very middle heads along `Direction`. `render::SpawnShape::outward` and `born` in `particles.wgsl` are the same sum (the in-or-out random is drawn after the life).
- **Not yet.** `SpeedOverLife`'s envelope is still unused. Flipbooks are R17.2c, below.
- **Tests.** `scene`'s `bursts_fire_at_their_times_once_a_loop`, `round_shapes_head_out_from_their_middle_in_or_both` and `particles_inherit_their_moving_parts_velocity`, and the renderer's `gpu_particles_head_inward_and_inherit_velocity` (a disc rim heading inward meets in the middle; a box inheriting 10 studs/s along X moves that way).

## Flipbooks

A particle Texture cut into a grid of frames, each particle showing one at a time (roadmap R17.2c, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Properties.** `FlipbookLayout` (`Enum.ParticleFlipbookLayout`: `None`, `Grid2x2`, `Grid4x4`, `Grid8x8`) cuts the Texture into 4, 16 or 64 frames, read left to right, then top to bottom. `FlipbookMode` (`Enum.ParticleFlipbookMode`) picks the frame for a particle's age: `Loop` (the default: in order at `FlipbookFramerate` frames a second, default 1, then from the first again), `OneShot` (every frame once over the particle's life, whatever the rate), `PingPong` (in order, then back) or `Random` (a frame at random, picked again at the frame rate). `FlipbookStartRandom` starts each particle on a frame of its own (`Loop`, `PingPong`). Only a Texture has frames: with the built-in `ParticleType` pictures, or `None`, the whole picture shows as before.
- **Sprites.** `Sprite::frame` (`FlipbookFrame { grid, frame }`) goes into `SpriteRaw::params`' last two numbers, and `sprite.wgsl` shrinks the quad's texture coordinates to that frame's square. Frames share the texture's mip levels, so at a distance a frame's edge can blend a little with its neighbours'.
- **One sum on both paths.** `render::Flipbook::frame` (age, life and a seed that stays the same over the particle's life) picks the frame for the CPU's sprites, and `flipbook_frame` in `particles.wgsl` is the same sum for GPU particles: the emitter uniform carries the grid, mode and random start in `spawn_shape.w` and the frame rate in `inherit.w`. Their seeds differ (the CPU uses the particle's life, the GPU mixes in its slot), so random starts and `Random` frames match in spread, not particle by particle.
- **Tests.** The renderer's `flipbook_modes_pick_their_frames_by_age` checks each mode and the random start, `a_sprite_draws_its_flipbook_frame_of_the_texture` draws each of four colored frames, and `gpu_particles_show_their_flipbook_frame_for_their_age` draws a GPU particle's frame after 0.8 seconds at 4 frames a second; `scene`'s `particles_show_their_flipbook_frame_of_a_texture` checks the CPU's OneShot frames, and that the built-in pictures and `None` stay whole.

## The Effect tab and effect assets

A place to build and preview one effect (roadmap R17.3, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md); `editor/src/effect_editor.rs`, its actions `editor/src/app/effect_tab.rs`).

- **What it shows.** The Effect tab (Window menu or Ctrl+K) follows the selection: a part, model, Attachment or Folder holding ParticleEmitters, or an emitter (its part is the effect, through an Attachment too; a free emitter in the Workspace is its own). Selecting something else keeps the last effect. It copies the effect alone into a world of its own (`effect_editor::follow`), with a checkered floor and a sky or a dark backdrop, and plays it with its own `scene::Particles`, so nothing it plays touches the scene. When only values change (a slider, an undo), they're copied onto the preview's Instances and its particles keep flying; when Instances are added or removed it's copied again and starts over.
- **Playing.** Play/Pause, **Restart**, **Loop** (start again every `effect_editor::loop_seconds`: the longest burst `Duration` or `Lifetime`, plus a `Lifetime` to fade, 1 to 20 seconds), slow motion (0.1×, 0.25×, 0.5×, 1×), **Floor** and **Sky**. Drag to orbit, the wheel zooms, double-click frames it again. Steps longer than 0.1 s are cut, so a stall doesn't burst particles.
- **The module stack** (D6). Each emitter, by name (click to select it), then five modules in the order a particle lives them: **Spawn** (`Enabled`, `Rate`, `Bursts`, `Duration`, `Lifetime`, `Shape`, `ShapeStyle`, `ShapeInOut`, and a sub-emitter's `EmitOn` and `EmitCount`), **Birth** (`Speed`, `VelocityInheritance`, `Direction`, `SpreadAngle`, `Size`, `Color`, `Transparency`, `Rotation`, `RotSpeed`), **Over life** (the end values and curves, `SpeedOverLife`, `Acceleration`, `Drag`, `WindAffectsDrag`), **Collision** (`Collides`, `Bounce`, `LifetimeLoss`) and **Render** (`ParticleType`, `Texture`, the flipbook, `LightEmission`, `Brightness`). Every emitter property is in exactly one module (`every_emitter_property_is_in_one_module`). The rows are Properties' own (`properties::property_rows`: descriptions on hover, ↺ to the default, curve and gradient editors, pairs greyed while their curve is set), and each edit goes through `EditorApp::apply`, one undo step, during Play too (where it lasts until Stop).
- **Counts and costs** (D7). Under the preview: particles moved on the CPU, emitters on the GPU (from `Rate × Lifetime` 3,000), the CPU step (eased) and the GPU's particle pass (`FramePass::Particles` from the preview renderer's `RenderStats`, on a device with timestamps; "–" otherwise).
- **Effect assets** (D5). **Save as Effect** (the floppy disk) saves the effect as a prefab in `assets/effects` (`<name>.prefab.json`, numbered when taken) and makes it a copy of it, as Save as Prefab does, so placing, overrides, Apply and Revert work as for any prefab, and the Toolbox, packages and global assets carry it. The Assets panel lists effects under **Particles** as well as **Prefabs** (`asset_browser::is_effect`). D5's own `*.effect.json` and stored preview setup were left out: a prefab in its own folder keeps every prefab tool working unchanged, and the tab frames each effect itself.
- **Tests.** `effect_editor`'s tests: the effect a selection belongs to, the loop's length, every property in one module, the preview playing a copy that keeps its particles while values change and starts over when Instances change, pause, slow motion and loop. `app/effect_tab.rs`: a stack edit is an undo step, and Save as Effect writes `assets/effects/<name>.prefab.json`, links the effect to it and never overwrites.

## Collisions and sub-emitters

Particles that land on surfaces, and emitters that give off where other emitters' particles are born, die or land (roadmap R17.4, [vfx-editor.md](../research/vfx-editor.md)).

- **Collides.** With `Collides` on, each step casts every particle's path (from where it is to where it would go) against the world's solid surfaces: parts with `CanCollide` on and a Terrain's ground and colliding foliage, passing by the emitter's own part and starting points inside a collider. Hosts pass `Particles::update_against` a `scene::Surfaces` closure over `physics::surface_hits`, which walks the Workspace once for all of an emitter's paths (`update` alone hits nothing). A particle that meets a surface stops 0.02 studs above it, keeps its speed along the surface and `Bounce` (0 to 1, default 0.5) of its speed into it, back out; bounced back slower than 1 stud a second, it rests. Each landing (after 0.1 seconds off surfaces, so resting or skidding isn't landing) takes `LifetimeLoss` (0 to 1, default 0) of its life away: 1 dies where it lands. Particles are points: a large sprite sinks half into the floor.
- **Budget.** At most `scene::MAX_SURFACE_RAYS` (8000) paths are cast a frame over every emitter, in tree order; particles past it move without colliding that frame.
- **Sub-emitters.** A ParticleEmitter inside another whose `EmitOn` (`Enum.ParticleEmitterEmitOn`) is `Birth`, `Death` or `Collision` gives off `EmitCount` (default 10) particles where each of its parent's particles is born, dies (of age or `LifetimeLoss`) or lands, from a 0.1-stud box turned by its own `Orientation`, heading along its own `Direction` within its `SpreadAngle`, with `VelocityInheritance` of the parent particle's velocity. It gives off nothing by its `Rate` or `Bursts`, only while `Enabled`. `Always` (the default) is an ordinary emitter at its own `Position`, as any emitter outside a part. Sub-emitters can have their own, up to four levels of events a frame.
- **In the Effect tab.** Its preview lands colliding particles on its floor (`EffectEditor::step` passes `physics::surface_hits` too), and the Collision and Spawn modules hold the new properties. A sub-emitter shows as an emitter of its own in the stack, and selecting one shows its parent's effect.
- **Sub-emitters stay on the CPU.** Nothing is read back from GPU particles, so an emitter that has sub-emitters or is one moves its particles on the CPU (4000 each), however many it gives off.
- **On the GPU** (R17.4b). A big colliding emitter moves on the GPU like any other, and its particles land on what the depth and normal prepass shows: `render::ParticleEmitter::collision` (`ParticleCollision { bounce, lifetime_loss }`) is set from `Collides`, and `particles.wgsl`'s `collide` runs in `simulate`. The renderer keeps the prepass alive while a drawn emitter collides (`ScenePipeline::particle_collisions`, like outlines and decals), and the particle pass runs after the prepass, so it reads this frame's. A particle lands when it went from in front of the surface at its new pixel to behind it, no further behind than 1 stud or twice its step (compared by distance from the camera, which keeps its precision far away). It rests on the surface along the prepass's normal (turned back to world space by `SceneDepth::inv_view`), with the same skin, bounce, rest speed and landing gap as the CPU (a particle's `extra.x` is its seconds off surfaces). It's screen-space: only what the camera sees stops particles, so they fall through floors off screen or hidden behind something, and the surface point is where the camera's ray meets it rather than where the path does. The same emitter below `GPU_PARTICLE_THRESHOLD` collides on the CPU against physics.
- **Tests.** `scene`'s `colliding_particles_land_on_a_floor_and_others_fall_through_it`, `a_bouncy_particle_bounces_back_up_and_lifetime_loss_shortens_its_life`, `a_death_sub_emitter_bursts_where_its_parents_particles_die`, `birth_and_collision_sub_emitters_give_off_at_their_events_only`, `colliding_emitters_and_sub_emitters_keep_their_particles_on_the_cpu` and `surface_rays_are_capped_a_frame_over_every_emitter` (`particles/collision_tests.rs`); the editor's `colliding_particles_land_on_the_preview_floor`; `physics`' `particle_paths_meet_solid_surfaces_but_not_their_own_part_or_sensors`; on the GPU, `scene`'s `big_colliding_emitters_land_on_the_gpu_and_sub_emitters_stay_on_the_cpu` and the renderer's `colliding_gpu_particles_land_on_the_wall_in_front_of_them` (particles thrown at a wall stop on its face, pass behind it without a collision, and die there with a lifetime loss of 1).
