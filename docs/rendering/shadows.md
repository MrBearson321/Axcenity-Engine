# Shadows

Sun shadows and contact shadows. Spot and point light shadows are in [lights.md](lights.md); the shadow quality setting sizes all three maps.

- `DrawList::shadows` (`Shadows { enabled, distance, softness }`) comes from the `Lighting` service's `ShadowsEnabled`, `ShadowDistance` and `ShadowSoftness` properties (`scene` reads them). The defaults are on, 150 studs, softness 0.2.
- **Graphics settings** (the presets, overrides, capabilities, `UserGameSettings` and where they're saved: [settings.md](settings.md)) are the machine's ceiling on the world's request: shadows draw only if both `ShadowsEnabled` and the settings' shadow quality allow them, out to the smaller of `ShadowDistance` and the settings' shadow distance (`lighting::shadows_drawn`). Shadow quality picks the map: Off (no map is allocated), Low 1024² × 2 cascades, Medium 2048² × 2, High 2048² × 3 (today's look), Ultra 4096² × 4. `SHADOW_MAP_SIZE` and `SHADOW_CASCADES` are the maximums (the shader's `cascades` array holds 4). `effective` lowers a quality whose maps the device can't hold (`Capabilities::max_texture_size`, `max_texture_layers`) to the best that fits, with a note. A map of the wrong size is freed and remade at the new size when next drawn, and a distance ceiling of 0 draws no shadows and makes no map.
- **Render scale and texture quality** (graphics Phase 3) are graphics settings too. Render scale (50% to 200%; Low 75%, the other presets 100%) sizes the scene's HDR, depth and bloom targets (`FrameTargets`, at `scene::scene_size`: the output's size times the scale, at most the GPU's largest texture). Lights are tiled in the scene's pixels; the composite pass's linear sampler scales the scene to the output, and the UI is always drawn at the output's size. Bloom blurs down to `BLOOM_LEVELS` halvings of the output's size whatever the scale, so the glow spreads as far. A new scale or output size remakes the targets and frees the old. Picking is unaffected: it works from the viewport's rectangle (`editor::screen`). Texture quality (Full, Half, Quarter; Ultra and High Full, Medium Half, Low Quarter) leaves out that many of a texture's largest mip levels at upload (`gpu::create_texture`), and halves terrain layer arrays the same way. UI images and terrain weight maps keep their full size (`ScenePipeline::full_textures`). A new quality empties the scene's texture and material caches (and the sprite and terrain bindings using them), so textures are uploaded again at the new size. `RenderStats::texture_bytes` and `mesh_bytes` count what's on the GPU, added at upload and taken off when freed (`GpuBytes`); the editor's Profiler shows them.
- **Texture memory budget** (graphics Phase 15; Low 256 MB, Medium 512, High 1024, Ultra 2048, MB being MiB) caps `texture_bytes`. After each frame's textures are noted and before its materials are bound, `ScenePipeline::fit_texture_budget` (`renderer/texture_budget.rs`) works out how many of each surface texture's largest mip levels to leave out (`levels_to_drop`): none while everything fits; otherwise the least recently drawn lose a level at a time first (a texture kept but not drawn lately goes before one drawn this frame) and, among those drawn as recently, the largest. Each keeps its 1×1 level. Shrinking copies the smaller levels into a new texture on the GPU (`gpu::shrink_texture`), so the caller's pixels aren't needed. A shrunk texture drawn this frame whose full size fits again is uploaded again at that size. The materials and sprite bindings of a replaced texture are remade. UI images, terrain and the white texture count toward the budget but are never shrunk, so if they alone pass it, every surface texture ends at its smallest. Within the budget the check is one pass over the cache, and nothing changes.
- **Anti-aliasing** (graphics Phase 4; `AntiAliasing`: Off, FXAA, MSAA 2×, MSAA 4×, and from graphics Phase 35 TAA, which no preset picks ([temporal.md](temporal.md)); Ultra and High MSAA 4×, Medium FXAA, Low Off) is a graphics setting too. MSAA draws the scene pass into a multisampled `Rgba16Float` color and depth (`FrameTargets::msaa`, `depth_view`), resolved into the single-sample HDR texture that bloom reads; the sky, solid, see-through, terrain and sprite pipelines are built for the current sample count (`scene::scene_pipelines`, `TerrainPass::set_samples`, `SpritePass::set_samples`) and rebuilt when it changes. Shadow passes stay single-sample. MSAA smooths triangles' edges, not the holes a texture cuts in them, so a standard material's cut-out (`alpha_cutoff`) depth fill turns its edge into coverage when multisampled (`fs_depth_coverage`, `alpha_to_coverage_enabled` on `DepthUse::Fill`): the alpha it returns ramps from 0 to 1 over one pixel around the cut-off, the GPU writes depth to that share of the samples, and `fs_prefilled` lights the samples whose depth is its own. With one sample per pixel the fill discards at the cut-off instead (`fs_depth_only`). A surface shader's cut-outs still discard per pixel, so theirs aren't smoothed. `Capabilities::msaa_samples` holds the sample counts both scene formats support (`graphics::SCENE_FORMATS`); devices are opened without adapter-specific format features, so these are wgpu's guaranteed ones (1 and 4). `effective` lowers a count the GPU lacks to the next mode down (4× → 2× → FXAA) with a note. FXAA runs after the composite pass: the composite draws into an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` target at the output's size (`FrameTargets::fxaa`, `FXAA_FORMAT`), then `fs_fxaa` in `post.wgsl` smooths it into the output before the UI. The multisampled and FXAA targets exist only while their mode is on; a new mode remakes the targets and frees the old (`TargetsAlive` counts them for tests).
- **Ambient occlusion** (graphics Phase 9; `AmbientOcclusion`: Off, Low, High; Ultra and High High, Medium Low, Low Off) is a graphics setting too; every GPU can draw it, so `effective` never lowers it. While it's on, `FrameTargets::occlusion` (`OcclusionTargets`, `renderer/occlusion.rs`) holds a depth and normal prepass at the scene's size (single-sampled `Depth32Float`, kept for reading, and view-space normals in `Rgba16Float`) and two `R8Unorm` occlusion targets at half that size; they're made and freed with the other frame targets (`TargetsAlive::occlusion` counts them). Each frame, after the shadow passes, the prepass draws solid batches (items with `fs_prepass` in shader.wgsl, cutting out what `alpha_cutoff` cuts, double-sided ones with both faces; terrain with `fs_terrain_prepass`), so `RenderStats::draw_calls` counts those draws too. `ssao.wgsl`'s `fs_occlusion` then tests a hemisphere of 8 (Low) or 16 (High) points around each pixel's normal, out to `OCCLUSION_RADIUS` (1.5 studs), turned per pixel by the same interleaved gradient noise as shadows, with a range check so a part far in front doesn't shade what's behind it, and `fs_blur` averages 3×3 of the result, leaving out pixels at a different distance. The scene pass reads it (frame bindings 7 and 8, a 1×1 white stand-in while it's off) through `screen_occlusion` in lighting.wgsl, raised to the world's strength, and multiplies it into the `occlusion` that `light_surface` applies to ambient and sky light only, so sun, lamp and emissive light are unchanged. See-through items (`base_color.a < 1`) and the sky don't read it. The world's look is the Lighting property `AmbientOcclusionIntensity` (0 to 2, default 1; `AmbientLight::occlusion` in the draw list, clamped by `scene` and `lighting::occlusion_drawn`): 0 is no darkening, 2 squares it. The prepass's depth is there for later screen-space effects.
- **Contact shadows** (graphics Phase 18; `contact_shadows`, on at High and Ultra, off at Low and Medium) catch the small gaps the shadow maps are too coarse for, such as under a part resting on the floor. They're drawn when the world's `ShadowsEnabled` is on and the setting allows them (`lighting::contact_drawn`), whatever the shadow maps' quality. They need the prepass's depth, so the prepass and its targets (`FrameTargets::occlusion`) exist while ambient occlusion or contact shadows are on; with only contact shadows, the ambient occlusion passes aren't drawn and the scene reads the white stand-in for occlusion. The prepass draws with its own frame bindings (`prepass_frame_bind_group`) holding a 1×1 stand-in at binding 15, as it can't read the depth it's drawing; the scene pass reads the prepass depth there (`prepass_depth` in lighting.wgsl). For each solid surface (see-through ones aren't in the prepass), `contact_visibility` takes 12 steps toward the sun, and toward each lamp that casts shadows, over `CONTACT_SHADOW_LENGTH` (1 stud, or the distance to the lamp if less), starting a little off the surface and jittered per pixel by the interleaved noise. A step blocked by what's on screen there (behind it by more than a bias that grows with distance, and by less than `CONTACT_SHADOW_THICKNESS`, 0.5 studs, so a part well in front doesn't count) shades the surface, darkest where the blocker is nearest and fading out toward the end of the march. They multiply only that light's direct share, like the shadow maps. `Frame::contact` holds on or off, the length and the thickness.
- **Cascades**: at High, three 2048² depth maps (layers of one array texture) each cover a slice of the view out to `distance`, nearest first. The slices' ends are mostly geometric (`CASCADE_SPLIT_BLEND`), so the first covers only the closest dozen or so studs and is the sharpest. Each map covers a sphere around its slice (so turning the camera doesn't change its scale), snapped to whole texels so edges don't shimmer. Casters up to 500 studs toward the sun are included. Each cascade draws only the casters whose bounding spheres reach its box, whether or not the camera sees them, so a part behind the camera still shadows what's in view (see Frustum culling in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md)). The shader uses the nearest cascade that covers a point and blends into the next over the outer 20% of each, so the change in sharpness doesn't show.
- **Soft shadows** (percentage-closer soft shadows): the shader first searches the map around the point for what blocks the sun and averages its depth, then blurs over a width proportional to the distance from that blocker to the surface times the sun's apparent size (`ShadowSoftness` × `MAX_SUN_SIZE`). So shadows are crisp where a part touches what it shades and soften further away, like real ones; softness 0 is crisp everywhere. Both steps take 16 samples on a Poisson disc, turned per pixel by interleaved gradient noise, and each comparison is itself filtered between four texels. The blur is at most 32 texels.
- **Acne and peter-panning**: the point is offset along its surface normal by 1.5 texels, the depth bias grows with the blur width and the surface's slope to the sun, and the shadow pass adds a slope-scaled hardware bias.
- `DrawItem::casts_shadow` (from a Part's or MeshPart's `CastShadow`, default true) leaves an item out of the shadow pass: it's still shaded by others. See-through items never cast (cut-out textures cast as solid). Batches are split by it. An item with a `cull_distance` (painted foliage) casts only to `SHADOW_REACH` (35%) of that distance from the camera (`renderer/lod.rs`), so thousands of pebbles and grass tufts aren't drawn into every cascade where their shadows can't be told apart; it's still drawn past that, seated by ambient occlusion.
- Shadows fade out over the last 20% of `distance`. The sun's shadows darken only sun light (not ambient); a spot light's darken only its own light. Shadows off allocates no shadow map and skips the passes.
