# Imported models

glTF and FBX meshes, texture maps, and SurfaceAppearance.

- **Draw list:** `Shape::Mesh(Arc<dyn MeshSource>)` is a mesh the caller owns (positions, normals, optional texture coordinates, `u32` indices), and `Material::base_color_texture` an `Arc<dyn TextureSource>` (sRGB RGBA8). They're traits so `scene` hands over the loaded model's data without copying it each frame. The renderer uploads each on first sight, keyed by `key()`, and frees what hasn't been drawn for 600 frames. Broken data (an index past the last corner, the wrong number of pixels) is logged once and not drawn.
- **Vertices** gained texture coordinates (location 12). Built-in shapes carry real ones: a box face runs 0–1 along its edges, a cylinder's side goes once around its circumference, and a sphere covers its surface. Tangents go with them. Textures are bound as group 1 with a repeating, mipmapped linear sampler; an item with no texture binds a 1×1 white texture. Mipmaps are made on the CPU at upload by 2×2 averaging (in sRGB, slightly off but not visibly). Levels bigger than the GPU allows are left out, so huge textures show less sharp instead of failing.
- **Model space:** `assets` fits every model into the 1×1×1 cube centered on the origin, like the built-ins, so `Size` stretches its box. It walks the default scene's node tree (or every mesh, if there's no scene), applying node transforms (mirrored nodes get their triangles flipped back); missing normals are made smooth from the triangles. Only triangle lists are drawn; points, lines, strips and fans are skipped.
- **FBX** files are read by `ufbx`, which converts them to glTF's space (Y up, right-handed, meters) whatever axes and units they were saved in, and triangulates polygons. Every visible node with a mesh is drawn, at its world transform (pivots and geometry offsets included), one draw item per material it uses. Normals the file lacks are made by `ufbx`. Materials become metallic-roughness: `ufbx` maps Lambert/Phong and other FBX shading models (and Blender's Principled BSDF) to PBR values; base color × factor, opacity (below 1 is `BLEND`; an opacity map makes it `MASK` at 0.5), metalness (default 0), roughness (default 1, Phong shininess converted), emission × factor, and the base color texture with V flipped to the engine's top-left origin. Their other maps are read too (see Texture maps). Skeletons, skin weights and takes are read too (see Animation). Not yet: per-UV-set texture choice (the first UV set is used).
- **Materials:** each glTF primitive is one draw item with its metallic-roughness material: base color factor × base color texture, metalness, roughness, emissive (converted from glTF's linear light to the draw list's sRGB). Alpha mode `OPAQUE` is solid, `MASK` cuts out pixels below its cutoff (`Material::alpha_cutoff`, a `discard` in the shader), `BLEND` is drawn see-through (opacity capped just below 1 so it always takes the see-through path). Solid items always write alpha 1. Its metallic-roughness, normal, occlusion and emissive textures are used (see Texture maps). Not yet: vertex colors, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`, skins and animation.
- **Texture placement and sides** (graphics Phase 6): `Material::uv_scale`, `uv_rotation` (radians) and `uv_offset` place every map of an item. The vertex shader applies them after `DrawItem::uv_tile`: scale, then turn (counter-clockwise in a texture whose V runs down, as glTF's `KHR_texture_transform`), then offset. They're in `MapsUniform` (`uv_matrix`, `uv_offset`), so items that differ in them get their own material binding; untextured items ignore them. `Material::double_sided` draws an item with pipelines that don't cull back faces (a solid and a see-through one); the fragment shader turns the normal, and the normal map's frame, to face the camera on a back face (`front_facing`), so a back is lit as a front. Batches are split by sidedness. Shadows already draw both sides. glTF reads `doubleSided` and `KHR_texture_transform` (offset, rotation, scale) from the first of the base color, metallic-roughness and emissive textures that has one; one transform places all of a primitive's maps, and a transform that moves its texture to another coordinate set (`texCoord`) is left out. FBX reads the base color map's (else the normal map's) placement from `ufbx`'s `uv_to_texture`: its scale and translation (the inverse of the texture's own `Scaling` and `Translation`), with V flipped to the engine's top-left origin; rotation is left out, and FBX has no double-sided flag.
- **Texture maps** (Phase 19): `Material::maps` (`TextureMaps`) adds a normal map (`NormalMap`: scale, and `flip_green` for DirectX-style maps), metalness, roughness, ambient occlusion and opacity maps (`ChannelMap`: one channel of a texture, optionally inverted, so a glossiness or smoothness map works as roughness and one packed texture can serve several maps), an emission map and a specular map. A map multiplies its value (metalness, roughness, emissive); the opacity map replaces the base texture's alpha; occlusion darkens only ambient light, by `occlusion_strength`. A specular map scales how strongly non-metals reflect: plain 0.5 is the usual 4%, 0 none, 1 twice that (older Phong materials' specular maps, rather than a guessed metalness). A built-in shape uses those maps when its material has any.
- **Binding:** each texture is uploaded once as plain `Rgba8Unorm` with an sRGB view as well: base color and emission are read through the sRGB view (so samples are linear light), the other maps as plain values. Each distinct set of textures and map settings gets one bind group (group 1: 8 textures, the sampler and a small `MapsUniform` with each map's channel mask, inversions, normal scale and green sign, occlusion strength and which maps are present); maps an item lacks bind the 1×1 white texture. Items are batched by mesh and that set.
- **Tangents:** normal maps need them. `assets` works them out after fitting, for primitives with a normal map, from the triangles' texture coordinates (area-weighted, made perpendicular to each normal; `w` is the side the texture's "up" is on). File tangents are ignored, so seams on maps baked with MikkTSpace can show slightly. Vertices carry them at location 13; the shader turns them by the model matrix and builds the tangent frame per pixel.
- **glTF:** base color, metallic-roughness (roughness in green, metalness in blue), normal (with `scale`), occlusion (red, with `strength`; often the same image as metallic-roughness) and emissive textures. All of a primitive's textures must use the same texture coordinate set as its first one; others are left out.
- **FBX:** what `ufbx` maps to PBR, plus the FBX material's own slots: normal map (or a bump map that looks like a normal map: mostly blue, red and green near half; a grey height map is left out), metalness, roughness (inverted for a glossiness map, and for Lambert/Phong, whose shininess map is glossiness), ambient occlusion, emission (a map with no emission color glows in its own colors), opacity (its own map, or the base texture's alpha when it's the same image; alpha if the map has any, else red; any opacity map cuts out at 0.5) and, for Lambert/Phong, specular. A value with a map takes the map's value alone (factor 1), as FBX programs do.
- **Texture roles by name** (Phase 20, FBX; `model/maps.rs`): every texture a material links is also judged by its file name, the last telling word deciding (`Bench_BaseColor_4K`, `T_Bench_N`, `rock_ORM`; words split at `_ - .`, spaces and lowercase-to-capital, sizes such as `4K` and numbers skipped; one-letter suffixes such as `_N`, `_R`, `_M` only at the end). Channel-packed names are split into their channels: ORM/ARM (occlusion, roughness, metalness in R, G, B), RMA, MRA, metallic-roughness (G, B), Unity's metallic-smoothness (metalness R, smoothness A, inverted), HDRP's mask map (metalness R, occlusion G, smoothness A), specular-smoothness (specular RGB, smoothness A) and albedo-transparency (base color, opacity A, cut out). `_NormalDX` maps flip green. A packed name always wins over the slot the texture is linked to; for Lambert/Phong, whose few slots exporters misuse (Unity puts metallic-smoothness in specular), any name wins; for PBR materials a single-map name only fills slots the links left empty. A texture linked to a property the engine doesn't read is used for its name's role, or noted as unknown ("has a texture `wood.png` it doesn't know the use of…") and left out; height maps (by name, or linked as bump/displacement and not normal-looking) are noted as unused.
- **Unlinked textures** (FBX): textures no material links, often embedded ones, go to the file's only material, or the material whose name (without words such as `mat` and `shader`) is the longest part of the texture's name, and fill only empty maps.
- **SurfaceAppearance** (Phase 21, `scene`): a SurfaceAppearance inside a Part replaces that part's flat color with its maps (the part's `Color` still tints the color map; a metalness or roughness map uses factor 1). `TileSize` on the Part (studs per repeat, default 8; 0 leaves the coordinates unscaled) scales each face by that edge's world length divided by `TileSize`, so a long part repeats the texture instead of stretching it once. Imported models keep their own coordinates. Inside a MeshPart, a SurfaceAppearance replaces the maps of the model's material named its `MaterialName` (others keep the file's). It decides every map: an empty one is no map, so deleting the SurfaceAppearance is the way back to the part's Color and Material, or the file's own. Its `Color` multiplies the material's color. A metalness or roughness map gives the value alone (factor 1); without one, the material's own value is used. `MetalnessChannel`, `RoughnessChannel`, `AmbientOcclusionChannel` and `OpacityChannel` pick channels (`Enum.TextureChannel`), `InvertRoughness` reads a glossiness map, `InvertOpacity` an opacity map that's white where see-through, `NormalStrength` and `NormalMapDirectX` set the normal map, `AmbientOcclusionStrength` the occlusion, and `EmissiveStrength` multiplies the glow (an emission map on a material that doesn't glow glows in the map's colors). `Tiling` (a Vector2, default 1, 1) repeats its maps that many times as often across and down, and `Offset` (a Vector2, default 0, 0) then shifts them, in copies of the texture; on a Part they apply after `TileSize`, and on a MeshPart they replace the file's own texture transform for that material. An opacity map on a solid material cuts it out at 0.5. Map files are read through the project's `Assets` (`Assets::texture`), decoded once, and a file that can't be loaded is reported once ("Could not load the texture `…`: …") and left out. The editor calls `Assets::load_textures_in_background`: a texture not read yet is then left out (drawn as if it had no map) while up to four worker threads decode it, and each one finished wakes the editor to draw again; `Assets::texture_progress` says how many are done. The player keeps reading them before its first frame, on the loading screen. Debug builds optimize dependencies and `render` (`[profile.dev.package]` in the workspace `Cargo.toml`), since unoptimized JPEG decoding and mipmapping take seconds per 4K map.
