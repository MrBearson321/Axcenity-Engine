# Sky and environment

The sky behind the world, and the light and reflections it gives surfaces.

- `DrawList::sky` (`Sky`) comes from the Lighting properties `SkyTopColor`, `SkyHorizonColor`, `GroundColor`, `EnvironmentIntensity` and `SkyImage` (`scene::lighting_settings`). The sky is drawn in the scene pass after solid items, as a full-screen triangle at the far plane that the depth test keeps to the pixels no solid item covers (`fs_sky` in shader.wgsl): the gradient (`gradient_sky` in `sky.wgsl`) plus a sun disc and glow along the sun's direction that bloom, or the sky image alone (it has its own sun, or none, so no disc is drawn over it), with any clouds in front ([clouds.md](clouds.md)).
- **Time of day** (graphics Phase 24; `scene/src/time_of_day.rs`). Lighting's `ClockTime` (hours; `World::set_property` wraps it into 0 to 24) and `GeographicLatitude` (degrees north) place the sun on an equinox day (`scene::sun_direction`): it rises due east (+X) at 6, is highest at 12, toward the south (+Z) north of the equator, `90° − latitude` up, and sets due west at 18. The moon is full, opposite the sun. Whichever is above the horizon is `DrawList::sun`, fading in over its first 3° (`smoothstep(0, 0.05, y)`), so at the switch, on the horizon, neither gives light: the sun (`[1, 0.97, 0.92]` × 3) or the moon (`[0.75, 0.85, 1]` × 0.15). Its disc (and glow) is drawn where the sun's was, and hidden below the horizon. The defaults (`core::DEFAULT_CLOCK_TIME` ≈ 10.27, `DEFAULT_LATITUDE` ≈ 14.04, 15 digits so they survive the world file) put the sun where the fixed sun always was (toward 0.5, 1, 0.25), so worlds saved before them look the same until `ClockTime` changes. Without an Atmosphere the sun keeps its color, the gradient's colors dim from a little before sunset to `NIGHT_SKY` (0.06) at night (`Sky::daylight`, applied in linear light). A creator's sky image dims by the same daylight (`Frame::night.z`, times the image where it's drawn and the environment maps where they're read, and the sky's harmonics on the CPU), so a picture darkens toward night; its colors stay the picture's, and the stars come out (`Sky::stars`, from sunset to 9° below). **Stars** (`stars` in lighting.wgsl) are drawn over the gradient or an atmosphere, never over a creator's sky image: 0.7% of the cells of a grid around the viewer hold one, about a pixel wide, most faint, fading out toward the horizon.
- **Atmosphere** (graphics Phase 24; `render/src/atmosphere.rs`, `renderer/atmosphere_sky.rs`). An `Atmosphere` inside Lighting (Explorer Insert menu or the Lighting ribbon; it always goes into Lighting) draws the sky as sunlight and moonlight scattered by air in place of the colors: a physically based model of Earth's (Hillaire 2020: Rayleigh scattering by molecules, Mie by dust, which mostly scatters forward, and ozone absorption, over a 6360 km planet with 100 km of air), worked out on the CPU in `f64` (Earth-radius distances lose metres in `f32`). A transmittance table (32 heights × 256 angles, gathered toward the horizon; `Air`) holds how much light crosses the air to each point; the sky is the light scattered toward the viewer along each ray (16 steps, more near the viewer), from the sun and from the moon (its light a share of the sun's), times `SKY_BRIGHTNESS` (4, so a noon sky is about as bright as the default gradient). Single scattering only. Its properties map onto the model (`time_of_day::atmosphere_of`): `Density` (0.395 is Earth's: the air's thickness over 0.395), `Offset` (0 to 1: how high the viewer is, up to 8 km, where the sky above darkens), `Color` (tints the whole sky, compared in linear light with its default grey 199) and `Decay` (tints the sky away from the sun, fading to no change toward it, compared with its default 106, 112, 125), `Glare` (0 to 10: the dust's glow around the sun × 1 + Glare) and `Haze` (0 to 10: dust × 1 + Haze, a paler horizon and a dimmer, redder sun). The sun's light is colored by the air (`Atmosphere::sunlight`: its transmittance from the sun's direction, scaled so straight overhead through Earth's air keeps its brightness; a noon sun is a little warmer, a setting one red and dim); `Sun::color` stays within sRGB and `intensity` takes the rest. The sky is worked out again only when the air, the ground color or the sun or moon move by about a tenth of a degree (`AtmosphereSky::look`, keyed like a sky image): above Low reflection quality it's a 256×128 sky image (drawn, baked into the environment maps and the probes, and lighting surfaces as any sky image does, `Frame::night.y` drawing the sun or moon disc and the stars over it), with below the horizon a fade into the ground color lit by the sun, moon and sky as the gradient fades; at Low its colors straight up, around the horizon and on the ground stand in for the gradient's. A creator's `SkyImage` is still drawn in its place, but the sun's light is colored by the air. Night darkens it on its own: only the moon lights the air. A world without an Atmosphere draws the gradient as before.
- **Sky image** (graphics Phase 16). `SkyImage` is a project path to a picture of the whole sky, wrapped around the world (equirectangular, 2:1: its middle toward -Z, its edges toward +Z, top straight up). OpenEXR (`.exr`) and Radiance (`.hdr`) files keep light brighter than white, so the sun in them lights and blooms; PNG and JPEG are read as sRGB (8-bit ones through a 256-entry table), but nothing in them is brighter than white, so they light less and look flatter. `Assets::sky_image` (`assets/src/sky_image.rs`) reads it once, on the background texture workers when the host loads textures in the background (the editor and player do: the sky colors show until it's ready, and the "Loading textures" note counts it), reports a file it can't read once (Output) and drops back to the colors, warns once when it isn't about 2:1 (it's stretched around the sky), and reloads it when the file changes. An image wider than `MAX_SKY_WIDTH` (4096) is scaled down as it's read, in its own format, so an 8K file never takes 400 MB of linear light. Reading uses the `image` crate's `exr` and `hdr` features (`exr` brings the `exr` crate, BSD-3-Clause, with `lebe`, `zune-inflate`, `bit_field`, `half` and `pulp`; see [../project/licensing.md](../project/licensing.md)). The renderer halves an image larger than the GPU's textures until it fits (borrowing the draw list's pixels, not copying them) and uploads it as `Rgba16Float` with every mip level. The sky pass reads the level whose pixels are about the size of the screen's (from how far the direction turns between pixels, `fwidth`), blurrier toward straight up and down where the image's columns crowd together (at most 4 levels more), so detail doesn't shimmer and the poles don't break into moiré; the bake picks its levels the same way. The image is shown only while the reflection quality bakes environment maps: at Low the colors are drawn and lit instead.
- **The default sky**. The engine carries one sky image, `assets/sky/partly-cloudy-sky.hdr` in the repository (*Kloofendal 48d Partly Cloudy (Pure Sky)* by Greg Zaal and Jarod Guest, Poly Haven, CC0; 2048 × 1024 Radiance, 5.4 MB; credited in that folder's `README.md`). The `project` crate embeds it (`project/src/default_sky.rs`: `DEFAULT_SKY`, `DEFAULT_SKY_PATH`, `add_default_sky`). New Project's Baseplate and Empty templates write it to the same path in the new project and set it as `SkyImage`, with `ClockTime` 10.52 and `GeographicLatitude` −36.77 (`DEFAULT_SKY_CLOCK_TIME`, `DEFAULT_SKY_LATITUDE`) so the sun, and so the shadows, stand where the picture's sun is (47.9° up, toward −Z; the picture was taken south of the equator). The genre templates keep their own times of day and the sky colors. Lighting's `SkyImage` row has a sun button (shown unless the default sky is set) that writes the file into the project if it isn't there (a file already at that path is kept) and asks "Use it as the sky?"; yes sets `SkyImage` and the sun as one undo step. Changing `ClockTime` later moves the sun and dims the picture toward night, but doesn't move the picture's own sun or change its colors. Being a project file, it's exported with the game like any sky.
- **Choosing one in the editor** (`editor/src/app/sky.rs`): Lighting's `SkyImage` row in Properties has a folder button (the system's file picker, for `.exr`, `.hdr`, PNG or JPEG; a file from outside is copied into `assets/sky`, `assets::import_sky_image`) and a button back to the sky colors, and takes a sky dragged from the Assets panel. While a picture is set, a note under the row says it keeps its colors, with **Follow time of day** (`Edit::UseTimeOfDaySky`): it clears `SkyImage` and adds an Atmosphere to Lighting if there's none, so the sky turns orange at sunrise and sunset and dark at night as `ClockTime` changes. A sky tile double-clicked or dropped in the viewport, and an `.exr` or `.hdr` imported or dropped on the window, all ask "Use *name* as the sky?" first; Use as Sky (or Enter) sets it as one undo step. See [../editor/assets.md](../editor/assets.md).
- **Environment lighting** comes from the sky, or inside a light probe volume (below) from its probes for diffuse light. The sky's diffuse light and reflections are multiplied by `EnvironmentIntensity`; the flat ambient is kept only as a small floor (0.05). Both are combined with the split-sum approximation (`ambient_specular` in lighting.wgsl), so metals reflect the sky.
- **Environment maps** (graphics Phase 16; `renderer/environment.rs`, `environment.wgsl`). Above Low reflection quality, the sky is baked when it changes (its colors, its image or the reflection quality; never every frame, and the sun and `EnvironmentIntensity` aren't baked in): drawn into a source cubemap, halved into each of its mip levels, then blurred into the reflection cubemap by GGX importance sampling, one roughness per level (0 at the top level, 1 at the 4² level), each sample reading a blurrier source level the wider it stands for (filtered importance sampling). Shading reads level `roughness × last level` (`sky_specular`). Diffuse light is the sky's second-order spherical harmonics, worked out on the CPU (`environment::harmonics`, over a 64×32 grid of the gradient or the image's 128-wide level), convolved with the cosine lobe and divided by π, and read per pixel by `sky_irradiance`. Frame bindings 10 to 12 are the reflection cubemap, its sampler and the sky image, 1×1 blanks while there are none; `FrameUniform::environment` and `sky_light` carry the rest.
- **Reflection quality** is a graphics setting ([settings.md](settings.md)): Low (the analytic path: diffuse from a ground-to-sky blend by the normal, reflections from the gradient blurred toward that blend by roughness, `environment_diffuse` and `environment_specular`), Medium (64² cubemap, 32 samples per texel), High (128², 64) and Ultra (256², 128). Low allocates nothing; `effective` lowers a cubemap larger than the GPU's textures. The maps and sky image count in `RenderStats::target_bytes`.
- **Reflection probes** (graphics Phase 17; `renderer/probes.rs`, `renderer/probe_bake.rs`, `reflected` in lighting.wgsl). A `ReflectionProbe` inside a Part reflects, in that part's box, the world as the editor baked it from the part's middle, in place of the sky. Its `Bake` names the bake file and `BlendDistance` (default 2) is how many studs inside the box's faces it fades in from what's around it. In the editor, **Reflection Probe** on the ribbon inserts one into the selected part, or into a new invisible, non-colliding 16×10×16 `ReflectionProbeBox`; **Bake Probes** bakes every probe in parts (`editor/src/app/probes.rs`): `TextureRenderer::bake_reflection_probe` draws the scene six times from the middle, a quarter turn wide along each axis, through the normal pipeline on a renderer of its own (the editor's graphics settings at 100% render scale, without UI or other reflection probes, but with light probe volumes, which are baked first) and reads the HDR scene back before post-processing, in linear light, at `PROBE_BAKE_SIZE` (128²). Each bake is saved as `assets/probes/<part name>.probe.hdr` (a probe that already has a file there keeps it), a Radiance `.hdr` holding the six cube faces stacked top to bottom (+X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z, as a cubemap keeps them), so it's exported with the other assets and any HDR viewer opens it. The new `Bake` values are one undo step (the files stay). Bakes don't follow the world: bake again after changing it. `Assets::reflection_probe` reads a bake once (`assets/src/probe.rs`); a missing or broken one is reported once in Output and the sky is reflected there. Bakes aren't listed in the Assets panel or offered as skies. The renderer uploads each bake once, as a cubemap with every mip level, and blurs it like the sky (`Environment::filter`: the same downsample and GGX passes) into its own layer of one cubemap array at the reflection quality's size, which exists only while probes are drawn. Up to `MAX_PROBES` (8) are drawn, the nearest to the camera; the frame uniform lists them smallest box first, with each box's world-to-box matrix. A surface inside a box reads that probe where its reflection leaves the box (box-projected, so walls line up), the roughness's level; smaller boxes come first and bigger ones, then the sky times `EnvironmentIntensity`, fill what each leaves at its edges. A probe's light is the scene's own, not scaled by `EnvironmentIntensity`. Reflection probes only change reflections; diffuse light comes from the sky or a light probe volume. They're left out at Low reflection quality (below Medium), where nothing is allocated. Probes need cube array textures, which DirectX 12, Vulkan and Metal all have.
- **Light probe volumes** (graphics Phase 19; `renderer/light_probes.rs`, `bake_light_probes` in `renderer/probe_bake.rs`, `surrounding_diffuse` in lighting.wgsl, `scene/src/light_probes.rs`). Indirect light without lightmap UVs. A `LightProbeVolume` inside a Part fills that part's box with a grid of probes, about `Spacing` (default 4) studs apart along each of the box's own sides (`ceil(side / Spacing)`, 1 to `MAX_LIGHT_PROBES_PER_SIDE` = 16 a side), each in the middle of its cell (`scene::light_probe_points`). `Bake` names the bake file and `BlendDistance` (default 2) is how many studs inside the box's faces its light fades in. In the editor, **Light Probes** on the ribbon (beside Reflection Probe, and in the Explorer's Insert menu) inserts one into the selected part, or into a new invisible, non-colliding 16×10×16 `LightProbeBox`; **Bake Probes** bakes every volume, then every reflection probe, a probe or so a frame with a progress window and Cancel ([building.md](../editor/building.md)); `LightProbeBaker` keeps one renderer and copy of the scene for all the volumes' probes. `TextureRenderer::bake_light_probes` draws each probe's six faces at `LIGHT_PROBE_BAKE_SIZE` (16²) on one renderer of its own, like a reflection probe (the editor's graphics settings at 100%, without UI, reflection probes or other volumes): the sky, and every surface lit by the sun, lamps and sky, so one bounce. The sky is drawn without the sun's disc there (`ScenePipeline::sun_disc`, `Frame::light_probes.y`), since the sun lights surfaces directly. Each probe's faces are projected on the CPU onto the first four spherical harmonics (`cube_light`: each texel weighted by the share of directions it covers, scaled to 4π), convolved with the cosine lobe and divided by π like the sky's, so a white matte surface facing `n` returns `c0·0.282095 + 0.488603·(c1 n.y + c2 n.z + c3 n.x)`. Each bake is saved as `assets/probes/<part name>.lightprobes.json` (`assets/src/light_probes.rs`: the grid's `counts`, the probes X first, then Y, then Z, four rgb coefficients each, and a `fingerprint`), kept by a volume that already has one; the new `Bake` values are one undo step. `Assets::light_probes` reads a bake once; a missing or broken one is reported once in Output and the sky lights the volume. The renderer writes the nearest `MAX_VOLUMES` (8) volumes' probes into one storage buffer (frame binding 16, four `vec4`s a probe) only when the volumes drawn change, and shrinks it to one blank probe without them; `Frame::volumes` lists them smallest box first, each with its world-to-box matrix, grid and first probe. Every surface (parts, models and terrain, still or moving) inside a box blends the eight probes around it (held at the outermost ones near the faces) and evaluates them at its normal in place of the sky's diffuse light; smaller boxes come first and bigger ones, then the sky times `EnvironmentIntensity`, fill what each leaves at its edges. A probe's light is the scene's own, not scaled by `EnvironmentIntensity`, and it still darkens with ambient occlusion. Volumes change only diffuse light, at every reflection quality. A probe inside a solid sees through its back faces, so the bake marks it invalid (roadmap R18.2, `physics::inside_solids`) and doesn't draw it: inside a colliding Part or MeshPart (its collision shape; never the volume's own part) or under a Terrain's ground. The bake file lists them as `invalid` (left out when there are none, so older bakes load as before), the renderer sets the first coefficient's `w` to 1 for them, and `surrounding_diffuse` leaves them out of the eight around a point and divides by the shares left; with all eight invalid, what's around the volume (a bigger volume or the sky) lights the point. Real-time GI keeps the flag when it traces a probe again. A wall thinner than the gap between probes, with no probe inside it, can still leak: probe visibility is a later stage. Lightmaps are left out unless volumes prove too coarse for interiors.
- **Real-time GI** (graphics Phase 20; `renderer/realtime_gi.rs`, `light_probe_sum.wgsl`). The machine's `realtime_gi` setting (off at every preset; `UserGameSettings.RealtimeGI`) keeps the drawn volumes' probes following the light as it changes: a moving sun, lamps, the sky. Each frame, before the scene is drawn, `PROBES_PER_FRAME` (2) probes are traced again, counting through every drawn volume's probes in buffer order (`LightProbes::point`): a `ProbeCamera` of its own (the one a bake uses, kept between frames) draws each probe's six 16² faces through the normal pipeline, one bounce as a bake sees it, with the frame's settings but at most Medium shadows; each face's HDR picture is copied into a texture array, and a compute shader (`sum_probe`, a copy of `cube_light`) sums them into the four coefficients and writes them over the probe in the light probes' buffer, so nothing is read back. It traces each probe once and rests until the light changes (`LightState`: the sun, sky, clouds, weather and lamps), so a scene with still light pays nothing further. A heavy scene (over `HEAVY_SCENE_ITEMS` items) starts from its bake without that first pass, and its sky picture arriving a few frames later (read on another thread) isn't counted as a change; when its light really changes it draws one face every `HEAVY_FACE_EVERY` (4) frames instead of 12 faces a frame, so the window keeps moving while its probes catch up. A volume of n probes is retraced in n/2 frames (a 4×4×4 volume in about half a second at 60 frames a second; in a heavy scene, 24n frames), and a probe traced at run time matches a bake drawn with the same settings. Only baked volumes are drawn, so a volume needs a bake (its starting light) to be traced. The traced probes last while the same volumes are drawn: when the set changes, its bakes are written again and traced from there; turning the setting off frees the tracer and writes the bakes back. Its cost is a second renderer's copy of the scene's meshes and textures and 12 small scene draws a frame (each with its own shadow maps), so it's an opt-in setting; on a GPU without compute shaders it stays off with a note. With Ray traced GI on (`ray_traced_global_illumination`, [ray-tracing.md](ray-tracing.md)), rays light the probes instead and Real-time GI rests.
- **Stale bakes.** A bake's `fingerprint` is `scene::light_bake_fingerprint` of the world it was baked from: a 64-bit FNV-1a hash (the same in every build) of every Instance in the Workspace and Lighting (class, name, place in the tree, properties, and a terrain's heights, paint and foliage), leaving out scripts and every `Bake`, which baking sets. The editor works it out again after each history change (`EditorApp::stale_light_probe_volumes`, `History::revision`) and puts a warning badge on each volume whose bake was made from a different world in the Explorer (on its closed parent too), until it's baked again or the change is undone. The fingerprint survives saving and reopening.
- **Light Probes overlay** (roadmap R18.1, [global-illumination.md](../research/global-illumination.md); `editor/src/viewport/probe_overlay.rs`, Test > Overlays > Light Probes, Settings > Show light probes). Each volume's probes, at `scene::light_probe_points`, are discs about 0.6 studs across (the nearest 4,000), filled with the light the bake gives a white matte surface facing the camera (the shader's sum, Reinhard-mapped to sRGB), so a probe that's dark, too bright or inside a wall shows where it stands. A volume's box is outlined green when baked, orange when its bake is stale (the editor's list above), grey with hollow probes before its first bake, and red with hollow probes when `Bake` names a file that can't be read or whose grid doesn't match. The legend counts each kind, all probes, and the probes and kilobytes the renderer keeps for the nearest 8 baked volumes (64 bytes a probe). Probes the bake marked invalid (R18.2) are magenta crosses, and the legend counts them. With real-time GI on, it still shows the bakes: the traced values stay on the GPU.
- The default sky looks as it did before the maps: `hello-graphics` is within about 1.6 per channel of its references at every preset (Low is exact).

## Tests

- `render/src/atmosphere.rs`: a sun overhead keeps its brightness and a low one reddens, none below the horizon; without air the sun is white and the sky black; the transmittance table matches marching to the sun; a noon sky is blue, brightest toward the horizon and about as bright as the gradient; a sunset sky reddens toward the sun and the moonlit night sky is dark; haze dims the sun, glare brightens its glow, tints multiply. `renderer/atmosphere_sky.rs`: without an atmosphere the colors are the sky's times its daylight; an atmosphere is worked out once until the sun moves a tenth of a degree; at Low it gives colors and no image; a sky image is drawn in its place; below the horizon the image fades into the ground.
- `scene/src/time_of_day.rs`: the default time and place give the old sun exactly; the sun rises east, stands south at noon (or north, south of the equator) and sets west, and goes round past 24; at midnight the moon lights the world and the stars are out, and at sunset neither gives light; through an atmosphere the sun reddens and sinks toward dusk; the Atmosphere's properties set its air. `player/src/time_of_day_tests.rs` (GPU): from noon to dusk (17:30, 40° north) the sun and the sky toward it redden and a pole's shadow stretches across the floor; at midnight the sky is dark, the moon lights the world and the stars are out. `project/src/tests.rs`: `ClockTime` and an Atmosphere save with the world and come back. `script/src/tests.rs` and `blocks/src/tests.rs`: scripts and blocks set them.
- `render/src/renderer/tests/environment_maps.rs`: metals and plastics under the default sky look as they do from the gradient (within the snapshot harness's 6 per channel at Medium, High and Ultra); a sky image shows in a mirror and behind the scene, and not at Low; a sky image darkens at night (`Sky::daylight`) behind the scene and in a mirror, and comes back by day; no sun is drawn over a sky image; a pixel-fine checkerboard sky reads as an even grey, not noise; a new sky bakes once however many frames are drawn, the sun and intensity don't bake, and Low frees the maps.
- `editor/src/viewport/probe_overlay.rs`: a probe lights a surface as the shader's sum does and draws Reinhard-mapped; each volume is baked, stale, unbaked or missing, and the legend counts them and the renderer's share; with no volumes the legend says how to add one.
- `render/src/renderer/environment.rs`: the harmonics of an even sky and a sky lit from above, pixel directions matching `equirect_uv`, 16-bit floats, halving.
- `render/src/renderer/tests/reflection_probes.rs`: a metal sphere in a red-walled room reflects the red walls with a probe baked there and the sky without one; each bake face sees its own wall, turned the way cubemaps keep it; a probe is filtered once however many frames are drawn, freed when it's gone, and left out at Low. `renderer/probe_bake.rs`: each face camera, mirrored, sees its cubemap face; 16-bit floats read back. `renderer/probes.rs`: box distances, the world-to-box matrix, broken bakes skipped.
- `assets/src/probe.rs` (a bake round-trips through its file; a missing or misshapen one says why), `scene/src/draw_list/tests.rs` (a probe takes its part's box; a missing bake is reported once and the sky is reflected) and `editor/src/app/probes.rs` (a probe with no part gets an invisible box and bakes into `assets/probes`, baking again keeps the file, undo; a baked probe saves, reopens, reaches the draw list and is packed into the built game).
