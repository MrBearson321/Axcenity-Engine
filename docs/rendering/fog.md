# Fog

What's far from the camera fading into the air (graphics Phase 25). `DrawList::fog` (`render::Fog`, `render/src/draw_list.rs`) comes from the world (`scene/src/fog.rs`); the renderer packs it into the frame (`renderer/fog.rs`, `FogRaw`, `Fog` in `fog.wgsl`), and every scene shader fades into it as it draws: parts and models (`fs_main`), terrain (`fs_terrain`), sprites (`sprite.wgsl`, premultiplied: the fog's light goes only where a sprite hides what's behind it) and the sky (`fs_sky`, as if it were `Fog::SKY_DISTANCE`, 5000 studs, away). Each surface fogs itself by its own distance, so see-through items, sprites and MSAA need no depth copy; reflections and the environment maps' sky aren't fogged.

## The world's fog

- **Lighting's distance fog**: `FogStart` (default 0), `FogEnd` (default 100000) and `FogColor` (default grey 192). Nothing is fogged nearer than `FogStart` studs, everything is `FogColor` from `FogEnd` on (the sky too, when `FogEnd` is under 5000), straight between. Fogged light is `FogColor` as an unlit color: it doesn't dim at night. A `FogEnd` of 100000 or more is no fog, so worlds made before fog look the same. An end before the start is whole from the start. Properties panel: Lighting's **Fog** section.
- **An Atmosphere's height fog**, in place of Lighting's (whose fog is then left out). It takes away `density` of the light per stud at and below its height and thins by `e` every 50 studs above (`FOG_FALLOFF`), worked out exactly along each ray (`fog_transmittance`: the fog's column below a height, `fog_column`, differenced between the two ends). `Density` 0.395 (Earth's) hides half of what's 500 studs away at the bottom of the air, and it scales with `Density` and `1 + Haze`; `Density` 0 is no fog. `Offset` (0 to 1) lifts the world above the bottom of the air, by up to 250 studs (`OFFSET_DEPTH`), so the fog thins. Far things fade into the sky's color around the horizon (the gradient's horizon, dimmed by night, or an atmosphere's horizon averaged around it: `SkyLook::colors[1]`), so they melt into the sky behind them. The sun (or moon) scatters 80% of its light in it (Henyey-Greenstein, `fog_phase`, forward by 0.6 + 0.03 × `Glare`), so the fog glows toward the sun and reddens with it at sunset.

## Dust in a box

A GraphicsVolume's `FogEffect` with a `Density` above 0 ([volumes.md](volumes.md)) is dust or mist filling the volume's box (`render::Dust`, `Fog::dust`), such as a room's air: light passing through it is lost at `Density` per stud, and `Color` of what's lost is scattered, forward by 0.6 + 0.03 × `Glare` (Henyey-Greenstein). Unlike the volume's other effects it's in the air, not a look the camera takes on, so it's drawn wherever the camera is (`scene/src/graphics_volumes.rs`'s `nearest_dust`: the box nearest the camera, one at a time). It thins to nothing across the box's faces over the volume's `BlendDistance` (half outside, half inside). It's separate from an Atmosphere: thick dust doesn't thicken the sky or redden the sun, as an Atmosphere's `Density` and `Haze` do.

- **Surfaces** behind it (`fogged_along`) lose the light of the stretch of the way inside the box (`dust_span`, a ray-box intersection), the dust taken as nearer than the rest of the fog.
- **Its light**: with volumetric fog on, the grid scatters the sun's light through it with its shadows (shafts through windows and gaps), the lamps', and the light around each cell (`surrounding_light`: light probe volumes' or ray traced GI's probes, the sky's where there are none), so a closed room's dust is dark where the sun doesn't reach it. Without volumetric fog it's lit flatly by the sky's average and the unshadowed sun (`FogRaw::dust_ambient`, `dust_sun`).
- Dust alone turns the volumetric fog on at Ultra (`volumetric_drawn`), with no Atmosphere needed.

## Volumetric fog

The machine's `volumetric_fog` setting (on at Ultra only; `UserGameSettings.VolumetricFog`; [settings.md](settings.md)) lights an Atmosphere's fog, and dust, with the sun's shadows (sun shafts) and with lamps (`renderer/fog.rs`, `volumetric_fog.wgsl`). Each frame, after the shadow maps, two compute passes fill a grid of 160 × 90 cells across the view by 64 slices along each ray, out to `VOLUMETRIC_REACH` (200 studs; slices about 0.15 studs deep at the camera and 15 at the far end): `cs_scatter` gives each cell its fog and dust and the light they scatter toward the camera: the sun's by how much of the cell it reaches (`cell_sun_visibility`: the nearest cascade's comparison at 8 points spread through the cell, the first of the Halton (2, 3, 5) sequence, the same in every cell; one point in the middle made shafts step from cell to cell and the steps crawl as the view moved, and shifting the points at random per cell left a speckle TAA's clamp can't average), each lamp listed for the cell's screen tile, with the lamp's own falloff, cone and shadows, and height fog's own color by how open the air is to the sky (`openness`: the light probes' light around the cell over the open sky's, 1 with no probes; before, a closed room's air glowed with the open sky's color, which hid every shaft); `cs_gather` adds them up along each ray, front to back, each slice dimmed by the fog in front of it (Hillaire's energy-conserving step). After the scene pass (MSAA resolved), `fs_composite` adds the light gathered up to each pixel's solid surface (the prepass's depth) or all of it in front of the sky. Within the reach, the surfaces' own shaders leave the sun's scattered light out (`Fog.height.w`), and height fog's own color too (`Fog.distance.w`, unless there's distance fog, which keeps its flat color), so open air looks the same with it on or off; only shadowed or closed-in air (and lamp light) changes. See-through items and sprites take the light up to the solid surface behind them. Lighting's distance fog is a flat color and isn't lit.

It costs nothing below Ultra or without an Atmosphere's fog: no grids, no passes, and the prepass only if ambient occlusion or contact shadows want it. While on, it needs the prepass's depth (the setting keeps the prepass), two 160×90×64 `Rgba16Float` grids (15 MB, in `RenderStats::target_bytes`) and the `VolumetricFog` pass in the profiler (its composite counts toward Scene). Its compute shaders see the frame's bindings through a copy of the frame's layout visible to compute (`frame_layout_entries`). On a GPU without compute shaders it stays off with a note.

## Tests

- `render/src/renderer/tests/fog.rs` (GPU): parts nearer than `FogStart` are clear, halfway is half fog, and past `FogEnd` parts, sprites and the sky are all fog; far parts fade into height fog, a part as far but higher is less fogged, and raising the fog's height fogs it again; at Ultra the air in front of a wall that shadows it loses the sun's light while open air looks the same as with volumetric fog off, and grey height fog's own light looks the same too (`height_fogs_own_light_looks_the_same_in_the_open_with_volumetric_fog`); dust glows only inside its box, with volumetric fog on or off, and at Ultra loses the sun's light in a wall's shadow; below Ultra or without fog no grids exist and fog adds no draws or target bytes, and at Ultra the grids are freed when the fog goes.
- `render/src/renderer/fog.rs`: no fog by default, distance fog keeps its color and range and scatters no sun, height fog takes the horizon's color and its share of the sun, height fog's own light moves into the grid only without distance fog, and dust packs its box, color and the sun's light it scatters. `render/src/renderer/tests.rs`: the setting-switching test checks the grids exist only while volumetric fog is on.
- `scene/src/graphics_volumes.rs`: a FogEffect with a Density fills its volume's box with dust seen from anywhere, keeping Lighting's fog.
- `scene/src/fog.rs`: a new world has none; Lighting's fog takes its start, end and color; an Atmosphere's replaces it, thicker with Haze, tighter with Glare, lifted by Offset, and none at Density 0.
- `render-types/src/graphics.rs`: volumetric fog is on only at Ultra, and off with a note without compute shaders.
