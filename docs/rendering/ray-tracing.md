# Hardware ray tracing

Optional ray traced effects on GPUs that can trace rays. Every effect is off unless a setting turns it on, and today's renderer is the fallback for each. Why ray queries, which effects come in which order and what they cost: [research/hardware-ray-tracing.md](../research/hardware-ray-tracing.md) (the RT0 to RT5 plan). This page is what's built.

## Which GPUs (RT0)

The renderer uses wgpu's ray queries (`Features::EXPERIMENTAL_RAY_QUERY`): rays traced from ordinary compute and fragment shaders against acceleration structures. They work on DirectX 12 (ray tracing tier 1.1, shader model 6.5), Vulkan (`VK_KHR_ray_query`) and Metal (macOS 15 or later). Ray tracing pipelines, which only Vulkan has in wgpu, aren't used.

- **Opening the device, only when asked.** `render::adapters::with_ray_queries(adapter, descriptor)` adds the feature, the adapter's acceleration structure limits and wgpu's experimental opt-in (`ExperimentalFeatures::enabled`, the workspace's one `unsafe` call outside the script debugger) when the adapter has it and hardware ray tracing is wanted (`adapters::want_ray_tracing`). The player (`Renderer::new`), the editor (`wgpu_options`), the benchmarks and the ray tracing tests all open their device through it. A device opened with ray queries behaves differently even while nothing traces rays: on an RX 9070 XT under DirectX 12 a white square flashed in the editor's viewport now and then, and didn't with the device opened without them. So it's off until `GraphicsSettings::ray_tracing` (Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing > **Hardware ray tracing**, `graphics.json`'s `ray_tracing`) turns it on, and it changes at the next start: the player hands the saved setting to `want_ray_tracing` before opening its window, and the editor keeps it in `hardware-ray-tracing.txt` beside eframe's settings (its device exists before its settings are read). `adapters::adapter_can_ray_trace` says whether the adapter could, so Settings can say what a restart would do. `AXCENITY_RAY_TRACING=1` opens devices with it whatever the setting, and `=0` without it.
- **Capability.** `Capabilities::ray_queries` says whether the device was opened with it (`read_capabilities`), and `EffectiveGraphics::ray_queries` carries it to the renderer. `TextureRenderer::limit_capabilities` can turn it off for fallback tests.
- **Settings.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_tracing_distance` (an override; `graphics.json`'s `ray_tracing_distance`, in studs, 50 to 5,000) is how far from the camera items are put in the ray traced scene. Presets: Low 150, Medium 250, High 400, Ultra 800. It's the machine's choice, set in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing or `graphics.json`, not from scripts (like the texture memory budget), and so is Hardware ray tracing. Each ray traced effect adds its own switch and quality there, off at every preset.

## The ray traced scene (RT0)

`renderer/ray_tracing.rs`'s `RayScene` is made only while something traces rays on a device that can (`ScenePipeline::traces_rays`: ray traced shadows or ambient occlusion, or the editor's Ray Hits or Path Traced view), and freed as soon as nothing does, so a frame without ray tracing records nothing for it.

- **One BLAS per mesh** (bottom-level acceleration structure), keyed by `MeshKey`, built from the mesh's own vertex and index buffers (their position comes first in each corner; `BufferUsages::BLAS_INPUT` is added to mesh buffers on devices with ray queries). New meshes are built up to `BLAS_TRIANGLES_PER_FRAME` (2 million triangles, about 5 ms on an RX 9070 XT) a frame, so a big import builds over a few frames; one mesh bigger than that is built whole on a frame of its own. Its items are left out until it's built (`RayTracingStats::meshes_waiting`). A caller's mesh whose `MeshSource::version` changes is built again: wgpu has no refit, so every update is a full build. A BLAS unused for 600 frames is freed.
- **One TLAS** (top-level structure) with a slot per item, in draw-list order, holding the item's transform, its BLAS and a mask: solid (1), cut-out (2), see-through or water (4), or not casting shadows (8). It's rebuilt only when a slot changes (an item moved, came, went or its mesh was rebuilt); wgpu uploads instances from the lowest changed slot up, so a still scene costs nothing after its first frame. The slot's index is the instance's custom data.
- **Swaying plants** (`renderer/ray_sway.rs`, 2026-11-14): while the wind blows, an item that sways (`DrawItem::sway`) gets a bent copy of its mesh each frame: `ray_sway.wgsl` bends every corner with the same `swayed_at` (sway.wgsl) the scene and shadow passes use, back into the item's own space, into a positions buffer of its own, and that is built into a BLAS of its own (`PREFER_FAST_BUILD`) which the item's TLAS slot uses (`Slot::bent`), so the TLAS is rebuilt every frame then. Nearest items first, up to `BENT_TRIANGLES` (400,000 triangles a frame, about 1 ms on an RX 9070 XT); the rest keep their mesh's rest pose. Copies are kept by mesh and by how many items of that mesh came before it that frame, and freed after 120 frames unused. With no wind (or a vertical one) nothing is bent and every item uses its mesh's shared BLAS. Before, rays saw every plant at rest, so ray traced leaf shadows stood still while the leaves moved, and the swayed surface could shade itself. `RayTracingStats::bent` counts them.
- **Left out:** items whose bounding sphere is past the ray tracing distance, the copy fading out of a level-of-detail switch, and posed (skinned) items, whose corners are only posed in the vertex shader. Cut-outs let rays through their holes (below).
- **Cut-outs' holes** (`renderer/ray_cut_outs.rs`): a mesh drawn by a cut-out item (`alpha_cutoff`, solid otherwise) gets a BLAS of its own built without the opaque flag (`BlasKey` is the mesh and whether it's for a cut-out; one drawn both ways has both). When it's built, `ray_cut_out_uvs.wgsl` copies its texture coordinates, three a triangle, into one shared buffer (so mesh buffers are storage-readable on devices with ray queries), and the base color texture's alpha is shrunk on the CPU to 512 × 512 (`ALPHA_SIZE`, the mean of the pixels each texel covers) into a layer of an `R8Unorm` array, once per texture. Each TLAS slot gets a `CutOutRaw` (the mesh's first triangle, the layer, the cutoff and the material's texture coordinate turn, scale and offset). A query that doesn't force opacity sees a cut-out's triangles as candidates, and `cut_out_covers` confirms a hit only where the alpha at its texture coordinates reaches the cutoff. Items tiled by size (`uv_tile`) and cut-outs without a texture are traced as solid. Ray traced shadows test the holes (their own copy in ray_shadows.wgsl), and so do ray traced ambient occlusion and GI (LE7) through `ray_cut_outs.wgsl`'s `trace_through_holes`, which runs a whole query on the including shader's `ray_scene` (naga takes no pointer to a ray query, so a query can't be handed to a helper); the Ray Hits view still forces opacity (cut-outs as solid cards). Ray traced GI's compute pass binds only the frame bindings it reads (`ray_gi::FRAME_BINDINGS`, through `ScenePipeline::frame_subset`): with all five of the frame's storage buffers, its own and the cut-outs' would pass wgpu's default eight a stage.
- **Timing:** the builds are recorded first in the frame, as `FramePass::AccelerationStructures` ("Acceleration structures" in the profiler), `None` when nothing was built. `RenderStats::ray_tracing` (`RayTracingStats`) counts the traced items, the built meshes and their triangles, the meshes waiting and whether the TLAS was rebuilt; all zero while nothing traces rays.

## Ray Hits view (RT0)

Viewport > View Mode > **Ray Hits** (`DebugView::RayHits`, `ray_hits.wgsl`) traces one ray per pixel from the camera through the ray traced scene: each item a color of its own (from its TLAS slot), its triangles alternately lighter and darker, dimmer toward the ray tracing distance, and a dark blue-grey where a ray hits nothing. It shows what ray traced effects will see, so an item missing from it (past the distance, posed, or a mesh still waiting) won't cast ray traced shadows either. Its legend counts the scene, or says the GPU can't ray trace; on such a GPU the view is plain dark.

## Path Traced view (LE6)

Viewport > View Mode > **Path Traced** (`DebugView::PathTraced`, `renderer/path_tracer.rs`, `path_tracer.wgsl`) is a reference picture: what the scene's light would look like followed ray by ray, to compare the lit view's shortcuts against. It's a debug view, so it exists only while shown (made in `set_debug_view`, as it reads the frame's bindings) and only on a GPU with ray queries; elsewhere it's plain dark like Ray Hits.

- **Each frame** a compute pass adds one path a pixel to a sum (a `vec4` a pixel: light and count), and a fullscreen pass (`path_traced_view.wgsl`) shows the average through the classic tone mapping curve over the finished frame. A path starts at the camera a little off the pixel's middle each frame (so edges smooth out), and at each hit adds the item's glow, the sun (a shadow ray toward a random point of its disc, as wide as the shadows' softness, so soft shadows come out right) and every lamp in reach (each with its own shadow ray, whatever its `Shadows`), then bounces on in a cosine-weighted direction carrying the item's color, for up to 4 bounces; after 2 it may stop, the likelier the darker it's become (Russian roulette). A path that leaves the scene sees the sky: as drawn for the camera's own rays, and times the environment intensity (without the sun's disc) once bounced. A path brighter than 64 is dimmed to it, so one lucky bounce doesn't leave a speck.
- **Starts again** when the camera moves, the output's size changes, the TLAS is rebuilt (an item moved, came or went) or the light changes (`path_tracer::lighting_key`: the sun, the sky and every lamp's bytes); after `MOST_PATHS` (2,048) a pixel the picture stays as it is and nothing is traced. A picture too big for one storage buffer is traced at half the size (or less) and shown stretched. The editor's legend counts the paths so far (`RayTracingStats::path_traced_paths`).
- **Normals**: rays don't give a hit's normal, so each traced mesh's are copied once on the GPU (`path_tracer_normals.wgsl`, three a triangle packed as 16-bit pairs, like cut-outs' texture coordinates) and each TLAS slot gets its mesh's first triangle; a hit's normal is its corners' blended by where it was hit, turned into the world by the instance's world-to-object matrix (normals turn by the inverse transpose), and flipped to face the ray.
- **What it reads**: the TLAS, each slot's `SurfaceRaw` (color times the texture's average, and glow: the ray traced scene keeps them while the view is shown, as for ray traced GI), cut-outs' holes (`ray_cut_outs.wgsl`, for paths and shadows alike), and of the frame's bindings only the uniform, the lamps and the sky (`FRAME_BINDINGS`), so the pass stays within the default eight storage buffers a stage.
- **Left out**: every surface is matte (no gloss, metal or texture detail beyond its average color); see-through items and water aren't hit; fog, clouds' shadows and the flat ambient light aren't added; posed items and anything past the ray tracing distance aren't in the scene (as for every ray traced effect).

## Is it on? The viewport badge

While Hardware ray tracing is on (or turned on, waiting for a restart), the editor's viewport shows a small badge at the bottom right (`viewport/ray_badge.rs`), so whether rays are drawing is never a guess:

- **Ray tracing: restart to turn on** (warning color), with a **Restart now** button: the setting is on but the GPU was opened without it. Restart now starts the editor again with the project and its unsaved world and script edits (`docs/editor/scripts.md`, Restart now); Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing has the same button while a restart would turn ray tracing on or off.
- **Ray tracing: kept off by AXCENITY_RAY_TRACING=0** (warning color), with **Restart now**: the setting is on, but the editor was started with the override (`adapters::ray_tracing_override`). Restart now starts it without the variable (`relaunch::restart` removes `RAY_TRACING_VARIABLE` from the new process), since a restart is for the settings to apply.
- **Ray tracing: not on this GPU** (warning color): the GPU or driver can't ray trace.
- **Ray tracing on: no effects**: the GPU ray traces, but no ray traced effect is turned on, so nothing looks different yet.
- **Ray tracing: Shadows High, AO Medium · 812 items traced**: the effects drawing and how many items are in the ray traced scene this frame ("nothing in reach yet" when none).

While effects draw, the badge's **Compare** toggle draws the view without them (`Viewport::rays_hidden`, `GraphicsSettings::without_ray_traced_effects` for the viewport only; the badge then says "Ray tracing: hidden to compare"), so what they add can be seen side by side without a restart or a settings change. Hovering it gives the reach, the scene's meshes and triangles, any meshes still waiting to be built, and where to change it. With the setting off the badge is hidden.

## One click: the ray traced look

Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing has **Use the ray traced look** (`GraphicsSettings::ray_traced`): Hardware ray tracing on, ray traced shadows and ambient occlusion at Medium, shadow and reflection quality raised to at least High, and TAA, keeping every other setting. **Ray traced effects off** (`without_ray_traced_effects`) clears the two effect overrides without a restart. While the setting is on but the GPU was opened without ray queries on a card that offers them, the section says to restart, and to check a Desktop shortcut opens this version (an older installed copy shares the settings folder but can't ray trace). The Learn guide `documentation/learn/ray-tracing.md` walks through it, the badge, and getting good shadows and sky.

## Ray traced sun shadows (RT1)

The sun's shadows traced as rays from each pixel instead of read from the shadow map cascades: exact up close, with no cascade blur, acne or peter-panning, and soft by how far the caster is from the receiver.

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_shadows` (`RayTracedShadows`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_shadows`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, that is while Hardware ray tracing is off or the GPU can't (`effective` adds the note "Ray traced shadows are off: Hardware ray tracing is off or this GPU can't ray trace…"). How soft shadows look stays the world's choice (`Lighting.ShadowSoftness`, ADR-0013); the setting only picks the rays a pixel where shadows are soft: Low 1, Medium 2, High 4. Hard shadows (softness 0) always trace one. It's in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedShadows` (`Enum.RayTracedShadows`) for LocalScripts and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedShadows* to".
- **When.** `renderer/ray_shadows.rs`'s `RayShadowPlan::of` traces them while the setting is on, the GPU can ray trace and the world's shadows are on (`ShadowDistance` above 0). They reach the ray tracing distance or the world's `ShadowDistance`, whichever is nearer. The prepass is drawn for them (`targets_used`).
- **The pass** (`ray_shadows.wgsl`, `FramePass::RayTracedShadows`, "Ray traced shadows" in the profiler, after ambient occlusion): `cs_trace` rebuilds each prepass pixel's world point and normal and traces toward the sun through solid items and cut-outs (instance masks 1 and 2; see-through items and items with `CastShadow` off aren't hit), starting a little off the surface by distance. A surface facing away from the sun is in its own shadow without a ray. Soft shadows spread their rays over the sun's disc (`MAX_SUN_SIZE` × softness wide) in a golden-angle spiral turned per pixel, and per frame under TAA, and keep the mean distance to what blocked them. `cs_blur_across` and `cs_blur_down` then smooth that across and down by each pixel's soft edge (blocker distance × sun size, in pixels, up to 12 each way), leaving out neighbours off the pixel's surface (farther from its plane than 1% of the distance plus 0.05 studs; comparing distances from the camera instead left slopes seen side-on unsmoothed, a dither) or facing another way. Hard shadows skip both.
- **The mask** is a `u32` per pixel (frame binding 17, a storage buffer since the item shader already reads 16 textures): the surface's distance from the camera in the top 24 bits and how lit it is in the low 8. `lighting.wgsl`'s `sun_visibility` uses it only where the pixel's own distance matches (so see-through surfaces, water and anything the prepass didn't draw keep the cascades) and blends into the cascades from 85% of the reach to the reach. `Frame::ray_shadows` is (on, width, reach, unused). The shadow maps are still drawn: they cover past the reach and see-through surfaces.
- **Cut-outs** shade only where their texture isn't cut out (`cut_out_covers`, above) (since LE7 ray traced ambient occlusion and GI let rays through the holes too, through the shared `ray_cut_outs.wgsl` and its four bindings, `ray_cut_outs::layout_entries`): a tree's leaves cast leaf-shaped shadows with light between them, where a solid card left one blob. A cut-out nearer than `CUT_OUT_NEAR` (0.35 studs) to the surface still isn't counted, for shadows and ray traced ambient occlusion alike: a leaf card lying on the ground, or a grass clump's foot, would shade the ground it stands in, and its own card can graze the ray's start (masks: solid 1, cut-out 2, see-through 4, not casting 8 solid and 16 cut-out).
- **Lamps (LE1).** Point, spot and rect lights whose `Shadows` is on are traced in the same pass (`traced_lamps` in ray_shadows.wgsl), with no shadow map needed and no limit by shadow quality: every pixel within the reach traces the first 8 lamps casting shadows that its light tile lists (`TRACED_LAMPS`), each ray from the surface to the light stopping short of it by how far the light's own part reaches (`LightRaw::shadow.z` is 1 plus that, 0 for a lamp casting none; `ray_shadows::traced_start`), so the fixture a lamp sits in doesn't shade it. With soft shadows (`ShadowSoftness` above 0) a point or spot light's rays spread over a bulb `ShadowSoftness` × `MAX_LAMP_SIZE` (1 stud) wide, and a rect light's over its whole rectangle (true area shadows); hard ones trace one ray to the light (a rect light's nearest point). Soft lamps trace the setting's rays each (1, 2 or 4) and aren't smoothed: TAA averages their per-frame turned samples. Results go in the mask's second half (`Frame::ray_shadows.w` is where it starts, a 256-byte-aligned offset in the same buffer, so the frame keeps one binding), 4 bits a lamp; `lamp_arrival` reads its lamp's by counting the tile's shadow-casting lamps before it (`lamp_slots`), and uses the shadow maps (and contact shadows) where the mask has nothing, past the 8th lamp, and fading in toward the reach, as the sun does. The editor's Insert and the Lighting tool now make lights with `Shadows` on (the classes' default stays off, so saved worlds look the same): turn `Shadows` on for existing lamps to give them ray traced shadows.
- **Shafts in the air (LE3).** While volumetric fog is drawn ([fog.md](fog.md)), the same pass also traces the air: `cs_air` gives each cell of the fog's 160 × 90 × 64 grid within the reach 4 rays toward the sun, from the first 4 of the points `cell_sun_visibility` uses, shifted each frame by the fog's turn (`air_shift`, a copy of `cell_shift`; the turn is `RayShadowUniform::lamp[2]`) so the fog's history averages rays from all through each cell, and writes how many got through (0 to 65535, `u32::MAX` past the reach) to the mask's third part (`RayShadows::air_words`, 256-byte aligned after the lamps', 3.7 MB). `volumetric_fog.wgsl` reads it where the fog's `dust_count.y` says (0: not traced) and fades into the cascades from 85% of the reach on, as surfaces do. So shafts come through windows (glass is see-through and isn't traced, as it casts no shadow), between leaves (cut-outs' holes) and through gaps with no shadow-map bias or blur, and with shadow quality Off too. Hard edges only (the sun's size isn't sampled); lamps' light in the air still uses their shadow maps.
- **Water reflections (LE5)** are traced where the screen's reflection finds nothing, with hardware ray tracing on and reflection quality High or Ultra: [water.md](water.md).
- **Not yet:** posed (skinned) characters casting ray traced shadows (they aren't in the ray traced scene; their shadow-map shadow still shows past nothing).
- **Cost.** One ray a pixel for hard shadows; Low, Medium and High trace 1, 2 and 4 for soft ones, plus two smoothing passes. The RX 9070 XT's numbers come from the PC check.

## Ray traced ambient occlusion (RT3)

Corners and creases darkened by short rays instead of guessed from the screen: an occluder off screen, or behind something else, darkens too, and nothing in front darkens what's behind it (screen-space ambient occlusion's halos).

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_ambient_occlusion` (`RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_ambient_occlusion`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, with a note. Low, Medium and High trace 2, 4 and 8 rays a pixel. How far rays look (`OCCLUSION_RADIUS`, 1.5 studs) and how dark it gets (`Lighting.AmbientOcclusion`, the world's strength) are the same as screen-space's, so turning it on changes accuracy, not the look (ADR-0013). On, ambient occlusion is drawn even while the screen-space setting is Off (`lighting::occlusion_drawn`, `targets_used`). It's in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion` (`Enum.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`) and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedAmbientOcclusion* to".
- **The pass** (`renderer/ray_ao.rs`, `ray_ao.wgsl`; timed inside `FramePass::AmbientOcclusion`) is a fragment pass at half the scene's size into ambient occlusion's own raw target, through its bindings (the prepass's depth and normals, `OcclusionUniform`) and its own (the TLAS, `RayAoUniform`). Each pixel traces cosine-weighted rays over the hemisphere above its surface (a golden-angle spiral turned per pixel, and per frame under TAA) against solid items, cut-outs and items not casting shadows (masks 1, 2 and 8: what the prepass draws), taking the nearest hit: a hit at distance t hides 1 − t / radius. It's alpha-blended over what's there, so screen-space ambient occlusion (drawn first when on) shows past the ray tracing distance, and the rays fade into it from 85% of the distance; with screen-space off the target is cleared to nothing hidden first. Ambient occlusion's blur (`OcclusionPasses::blur`) then smooths the result as before.
- **Cost.** At half size, a quarter of a pixel's rays: High (8) costs about as much as two full-size rays a pixel. Numbers from the PC check.

## Ray traced global illumination (RT2)

Bounced light that follows the world as it changes: light probes lit by rays each frame (the camera's own and any light probe volumes', instead of by their bake), so a moved wall, a lamp turned on or the sun going down colors the light around them. It takes Real-time GI's place (`realtime_gi.rs`, six drawn faces a probe) while on, and is far cheaper per probe.

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_global_illumination` (`RayTracedGlobalIllumination`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_global_illumination`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, with a note ("Ray traced GI is off: …"). Low, Medium and High trace 128, 256 or 512 probes a frame (`probes_per_frame`) with 64, 128 or 256 rays each (`rays`). It's in Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedGlobalIllumination` (`Enum.RayTracedGlobalIllumination`) and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedGlobalIllumination* to". It's left out of the one-click look (its cost depends on the world's volumes); **Ray traced effects off** clears it too.
- **The camera's probes** (`renderer/light_probes.rs`): while it's on, the camera brings a grid of its own, 16 × 8 × 16 probes 3 studs apart (`CAMERA_PROBES`, `CAMERA_SPACING`: a box of 48 × 24 × 48 studs, fading in over 6), centred on the spacing nearest the camera. They come first in the light probes' buffer and are read first, so rooms near the camera get their own light with no volume placed; light probe volumes in the world are drawn and traced too, behind them, and the sky past them all. They have no bake: each starts untraced. When the camera crosses a spacing, the box moves with it and `follow_camera` copies each probe that stays to its new place on the GPU (each row along X from a copy of the buffer, before anything reads it that frame), so only the row it moves into starts again.
- **Probes left out.** The w of a probe's first coefficient is over a half for a probe not to be used: 1 for one a bake found inside a solid (R18.2, which ray traced GI keeps so), 2 for one not traced yet or one ray traced GI finds inside something (more than a quarter of its rays find the backs of solid things, `GI_INSIDE`: in a wall or under the ground). `surrounding_diffuse` leaves those out and shares their part among the other probes around the surface; where all eight are left out, the volume gives way to the next or the sky. So probes stuck in walls don't darken the rooms beside them. A probe so marked takes its next update whole. Cut-outs (leaf cards, `SurfaceRaw`'s w) are lit on either side and never count as inside. Bakes are uploaded with 0.
- **The pass** (`renderer/ray_gi.rs`, `ray_gi.wgsl`, compute; `FramePass::RayTracedGi`, "Ray traced GI" in the profiler) runs before the scene. Each probe of the batch (counting through the camera's probes and every drawn volume's, wrapping round) traces rays in a spherical Fibonacci pattern turned each update, against the ray traced scene. A hit is lit from its item's surface (`ray_tracing.rs`'s `Surfaces`, one per TLAS slot while this is on: base color times its texture's average color, plus emissive): the sun and up to 16 lamps through shadow rays, plus the probes' own light at the hit (`surrounding_diffuse`), so light bounces again with each update. A miss reads the sky (`sky_specular`, rough). The rays sum into L1 spherical harmonics, blended over what the probe had (`keep`: settling in about half a second however often a probe comes round, never keeping more than 92%), into the pass's own buffer, then copied over the batch's places in the light probes' buffer (four rgb coefficients a probe, unchanged), so nothing is read back. Turning it off frees it (and the camera's probes) and writes the bakes back.
- **Cost.** A probe costs its rays, a few hundred, instead of six drawn faces: High (512 × 256 rays) is about 131,000 rays a frame (plus a shadow ray toward the sun and each lamp from every hit), a small fraction of a 1440p shadow pass. Medium goes round the camera's 2,048 probes every 8 frames. Numbers from the PC check.

## Tests

`render/src/renderer/tests/ray_tracing.rs`, on a device opened with ray queries (`ray_test_device`; skipped where there's none, but Mesa's lavapipe has them, so they run without a GPU): the capability follows the device, nothing is built while nothing traces rays, the TLAS is rebuilt only when an item moves or comes and a mesh is shared between items, everything is freed when the view goes back to Lit, items past the distance are left out, Ray Hits colors what rays hit and leaves misses dark, the lit frame is unchanged after it, and without ray queries the view is plain and builds nothing. The Path Traced view (`mod path_traced`): a ball lit from the side is bright on that side and dark on the other (its own normals), a block's shadow falls on the wall only while it casts shadows, the paths add up while nothing changes and start again when an item or the camera changes, going back to Lit frees it and draws as before, a lamp alone lights a dark wall, and without ray queries the view is plain. Ray traced shadows (`mod sun_shadows`): hard ones shade like the shadow maps with no map drawn, rays pass through a cut-out's holes and not where it isn't cut out (`rays_pass_through_a_cut_outs_holes`; `ray_cut_outs.rs` checks the alpha's shrinking), soft ones have soft edges and hard ones don't, the shadow maps take over past the reach, point, spot and rect lights cast ray traced shadows with no map drawn (`mod lamp_shadows`), a lamp without `Shadows` lights through, and a lamp's own fixture doesn't shade it, turning them off frees them and draws as before, a world without shadows traces nothing, and without ray queries the setting is off with a note. `render-types` checks they're off at every preset and without ray queries; `script` checks `UserGameSettings.RayTracedShadows`. Ray traced ambient occlusion (`mod ambient_occlusion`): rays alone darken the corner under a block and leave open floor alone, they find a wall just off screen that screen-space ambient occlusion can't, they pass through a cut-out's holes (LE7) and are hidden where it isn't cut out, past the ray tracing distance they fade out to nothing, and turning them off frees the pass and draws as before; `render-types` checks they're off at every preset and without ray queries, `script` checks `UserGameSettings.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`, and the editor's In use line lists each effect. Ray traced GI (`mod global_illumination`): a moving sun relights the probes toward a fresh bake, a red wall bounces red light toward the probes beside it, without a volume the camera brings its probes and one inside a block is left out, the camera's probes move with it keeping their light (and the row it moves into starts untraced), and without ray queries nothing is traced; `light_probes.rs` checks the rows copied for each move and the box's middle; `render-types` checks it's off at every preset and without ray queries, and `ray_gi.rs` checks the blend. `render-types` checks the distance's presets and range; the editor checks the view's label, its legend, the settings surviving a restart and each state of the viewport badge.
