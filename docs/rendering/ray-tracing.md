# Hardware ray tracing

Optional ray traced effects on GPUs that can trace rays. Every effect is off unless a setting turns it on, and today's renderer is the fallback for each. Why ray queries, which effects come in which order and what they cost: [research/hardware-ray-tracing.md](../research/hardware-ray-tracing.md) (the RT0 to RT5 plan). This page is what's built.

## Which GPUs (RT0)

The renderer uses wgpu's ray queries (`Features::EXPERIMENTAL_RAY_QUERY`): rays traced from ordinary compute and fragment shaders against acceleration structures. They work on DirectX 12 (ray tracing tier 1.1, shader model 6.5), Vulkan (`VK_KHR_ray_query`) and Metal (macOS 15 or later). Ray tracing pipelines, which only Vulkan has in wgpu, aren't used.

- **Opening the device, only when asked.** `render::adapters::with_ray_queries(adapter, descriptor)` adds the feature, the adapter's acceleration structure limits and wgpu's experimental opt-in (`ExperimentalFeatures::enabled`, the workspace's one `unsafe` call outside the script debugger) when the adapter has it and hardware ray tracing is wanted (`adapters::want_ray_tracing`). The player (`Renderer::new`), the editor (`wgpu_options`), the benchmarks and the ray tracing tests all open their device through it. A device opened with ray queries behaves differently even while nothing traces rays: on an RX 9070 XT under DirectX 12 a white square flashed in the editor's viewport now and then, and didn't with the device opened without them. So it's off until `GraphicsSettings::ray_tracing` (Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing > **Hardware ray tracing**, `graphics.json`'s `ray_tracing`) turns it on, and it changes at the next start: the player hands the saved setting to `want_ray_tracing` before opening its window, and the editor keeps it in `hardware-ray-tracing.txt` beside eframe's settings (its device exists before its settings are read). `adapters::adapter_can_ray_trace` says whether the adapter could, so Settings can say what a restart would do. `AXCENITY_RAY_TRACING=1` opens devices with it whatever the setting, and `=0` without it.
- **Capability.** `Capabilities::ray_queries` says whether the device was opened with it (`read_capabilities`), and `EffectiveGraphics::ray_queries` carries it to the renderer. `TextureRenderer::limit_capabilities` can turn it off for fallback tests.
- **Settings.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_tracing_distance` (an override; `graphics.json`'s `ray_tracing_distance`, in studs, 50 to 5,000) is how far from the camera items are put in the ray traced scene. Presets: Low 150, Medium 250, High 400, Ultra 800. It's the machine's choice, set in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing or `graphics.json`, not from scripts (like the texture memory budget), and so is Hardware ray tracing. Each ray traced effect adds its own switch and quality there, off at every preset.

## The ray traced scene (RT0)

`renderer/ray_tracing.rs`'s `RayScene` is made only while something traces rays on a device that can (`ScenePipeline::traces_rays`: ray traced shadows or ambient occlusion, or the editor's Ray Hits view), and freed as soon as nothing does, so a frame without ray tracing records nothing for it.

- **One BLAS per mesh** (bottom-level acceleration structure), keyed by `MeshKey`, built from the mesh's own vertex and index buffers (their position comes first in each corner; `BufferUsages::BLAS_INPUT` is added to mesh buffers on devices with ray queries). New meshes are built up to `BLAS_TRIANGLES_PER_FRAME` (2 million triangles, about 5 ms on an RX 9070 XT) a frame, so a big import builds over a few frames; one mesh bigger than that is built whole on a frame of its own. Its items are left out until it's built (`RayTracingStats::meshes_waiting`). A caller's mesh whose `MeshSource::version` changes is built again: wgpu has no refit, so every update is a full build. A BLAS unused for 600 frames is freed.
- **One TLAS** (top-level structure) with a slot per item, in draw-list order, holding the item's transform, its BLAS and a mask: solid (1), cut-out (2), see-through or water (4), or not casting shadows (8). It's rebuilt only when a slot changes (an item moved, came, went or its mesh was rebuilt); wgpu uploads instances from the lowest changed slot up, so a still scene costs nothing after its first frame. The slot's index is the instance's custom data.
- **Left out:** items whose bounding sphere is past the ray tracing distance, the copy fading out of a level-of-detail switch, and posed (skinned) items, whose corners are only posed in the vertex shader. Cut-outs are traced as solid: their holes don't let rays through yet.
- **Timing:** the builds are recorded first in the frame, as `FramePass::AccelerationStructures` ("Acceleration structures" in the profiler), `None` when nothing was built. `RenderStats::ray_tracing` (`RayTracingStats`) counts the traced items, the built meshes and their triangles, the meshes waiting and whether the TLAS was rebuilt; all zero while nothing traces rays.

## Ray Hits view (RT0)

Viewport > View Mode > **Ray Hits** (`DebugView::RayHits`, `ray_hits.wgsl`) traces one ray per pixel from the camera through the ray traced scene: each item a color of its own (from its TLAS slot), its triangles alternately lighter and darker, dimmer toward the ray tracing distance, and a dark blue-grey where a ray hits nothing. It shows what ray traced effects will see, so an item missing from it (past the distance, posed, or a mesh still waiting) won't cast ray traced shadows either. Its legend counts the scene, or says the GPU can't ray trace; on such a GPU the view is plain dark.

## Is it on? The viewport badge

While Hardware ray tracing is on (or turned on, waiting for a restart), the editor's viewport shows a small badge at the bottom right (`viewport/ray_badge.rs`), so whether rays are drawing is never a guess:

- **Ray tracing: restart to turn on** (warning color), with a **Restart now** button: the setting is on but the GPU was opened without it. Restart now starts the editor again with the project and its unsaved world and script edits (`docs/editor/scripts.md`, Restart now); Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing has the same button while a restart would turn ray tracing on or off.
- **Ray tracing: kept off by AXCENITY_RAY_TRACING=0** (warning color), with **Restart now**: the setting is on, but the editor was started with the override (`adapters::ray_tracing_override`). Restart now starts it without the variable (`relaunch::restart` removes `RAY_TRACING_VARIABLE` from the new process), since a restart is for the settings to apply.
- **Ray tracing: not on this GPU** (warning color): the GPU or driver can't ray trace.
- **Ray tracing on: no effects**: the GPU ray traces, but no ray traced effect is turned on, so nothing looks different yet.
- **Ray tracing: Shadows High, AO Medium · 812 items traced**: the effects drawing and how many items are in the ray traced scene this frame ("nothing in reach yet" when none).

While effects draw, the badge's **Compare** toggle draws the view without them (`Viewport::rays_hidden`, `GraphicsSettings::without_ray_traced_effects` for the viewport only; the badge then says "Ray tracing: hidden to compare"), so what they add can be seen side by side without a restart or a settings change. Hovering it gives the reach, the scene's meshes and triangles, any meshes still waiting to be built, and where to change it. With the setting off the badge is hidden.

## One click: the ray traced look

Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing has **Use the ray traced look** (`GraphicsSettings::ray_traced`): Hardware ray tracing on, ray traced shadows and ambient occlusion at Medium, shadow and reflection quality raised to at least High, and TAA, keeping every other setting. **Ray traced effects off** (`without_ray_traced_effects`) clears the two effect overrides without a restart. While the setting is on but the GPU was opened without ray queries on a card that offers them, the section says to restart, and to check a Desktop shortcut opens this version (an older installed copy shares the settings folder but can't ray trace). The Learn guide `documentation/learn/ray-tracing.md` walks through it, the badge, and getting good shadows and sky.

## Ray traced sun shadows (RT1)

The sun's shadows traced as rays from each pixel instead of read from the shadow map cascades: exact up close, with no cascade blur, acne or peter-panning, and soft by how far the caster is from the receiver.

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_shadows` (`RayTracedShadows`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_shadows`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, that is while Hardware ray tracing is off or the GPU can't (`effective` adds the note "Ray traced shadows are off: Hardware ray tracing is off or this GPU can't ray trace…"). How soft shadows look stays the world's choice (`Lighting.ShadowSoftness`, ADR-0013); the setting only picks the rays a pixel where shadows are soft: Low 1, Medium 2, High 4. Hard shadows (softness 0) always trace one. It's in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedShadows` (`Enum.RayTracedShadows`) for LocalScripts and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedShadows* to".
- **When.** `renderer/ray_shadows.rs`'s `RayShadowPlan::of` traces them while the setting is on, the GPU can ray trace and the world's shadows are on (`ShadowDistance` above 0). They reach the ray tracing distance or the world's `ShadowDistance`, whichever is nearer. The prepass is drawn for them (`targets_used`).
- **The pass** (`ray_shadows.wgsl`, `FramePass::RayTracedShadows`, "Ray traced shadows" in the profiler, after ambient occlusion): `cs_trace` rebuilds each prepass pixel's world point and normal and traces toward the sun through solid items and cut-outs (instance masks 1 and 2; see-through items and items with `CastShadow` off aren't hit), starting a little off the surface by distance. A surface facing away from the sun is in its own shadow without a ray. Soft shadows spread their rays over the sun's disc (`MAX_SUN_SIZE` × softness wide) in a golden-angle spiral turned per pixel, and per frame under TAA, and keep the mean distance to what blocked them. `cs_blur_across` and `cs_blur_down` then smooth that across and down by each pixel's soft edge (blocker distance × sun size, in pixels, up to 12 each way), leaving out neighbours off the pixel's surface (farther from its plane than 1% of the distance plus 0.05 studs; comparing distances from the camera instead left slopes seen side-on unsmoothed, a dither) or facing another way. Hard shadows skip both.
- **The mask** is a `u32` per pixel (frame binding 17, a storage buffer since the item shader already reads 16 textures): the surface's distance from the camera in the top 24 bits and how lit it is in the low 8. `lighting.wgsl`'s `sun_visibility` uses it only where the pixel's own distance matches (so see-through surfaces, water and anything the prepass didn't draw keep the cascades) and blends into the cascades from 85% of the reach to the reach. `Frame::ray_shadows` is (on, width, reach, unused). The shadow maps are still drawn: they cover past the reach and see-through surfaces.
- **Cut-outs** are traced as solid cards (their holes aren't looked up), so a cut-out nearer than `CUT_OUT_NEAR` (0.35 studs) to the surface isn't counted, for shadows and ray traced ambient occlusion alike: leaves lying on the ground and grass clumps would otherwise shade it in black, card-shaped blotches that flicker as the rays turn. Solid items count from the surface; cut-outs past 0.35 studs still cast a solid shadow (masks: solid 1, cut-out 2, see-through 4, not casting 8 solid and 16 cut-out).
- **Not yet:** cut-outs' holes (a tree's leaves cast a solid blob), posed (skinned) characters casting ray traced shadows (they aren't in the ray traced scene; their shadow-map shadow still shows past nothing), and lamps' shadows.
- **Cost.** One ray a pixel for hard shadows; Low, Medium and High trace 1, 2 and 4 for soft ones, plus two smoothing passes. The RX 9070 XT's numbers come from the PC check.

## Ray traced ambient occlusion (RT3)

Corners and creases darkened by short rays instead of guessed from the screen: an occluder off screen, or behind something else, darkens too, and nothing in front darkens what's behind it (screen-space ambient occlusion's halos).

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_ambient_occlusion` (`RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_ambient_occlusion`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, with a note. Low, Medium and High trace 2, 4 and 8 rays a pixel. How far rays look (`OCCLUSION_RADIUS`, 1.5 studs) and how dark it gets (`Lighting.AmbientOcclusion`, the world's strength) are the same as screen-space's, so turning it on changes accuracy, not the look (ADR-0013). On, ambient occlusion is drawn even while the screen-space setting is Off (`lighting::occlusion_drawn`, `targets_used`). It's in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion` (`Enum.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`) and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedAmbientOcclusion* to".
- **The pass** (`renderer/ray_ao.rs`, `ray_ao.wgsl`; timed inside `FramePass::AmbientOcclusion`) is a fragment pass at half the scene's size into ambient occlusion's own raw target, through its bindings (the prepass's depth and normals, `OcclusionUniform`) and its own (the TLAS, `RayAoUniform`). Each pixel traces cosine-weighted rays over the hemisphere above its surface (a golden-angle spiral turned per pixel, and per frame under TAA) against solid items, cut-outs and items not casting shadows (masks 1, 2 and 8: what the prepass draws), taking the nearest hit: a hit at distance t hides 1 − t / radius. It's alpha-blended over what's there, so screen-space ambient occlusion (drawn first when on) shows past the ray tracing distance, and the rays fade into it from 85% of the distance; with screen-space off the target is cleared to nothing hidden first. Ambient occlusion's blur (`OcclusionPasses::blur`) then smooths the result as before.
- **Cost.** At half size, a quarter of a pixel's rays: High (8) costs about as much as two full-size rays a pixel. Numbers from the PC check.

## Tests

`render/src/renderer/tests/ray_tracing.rs`, on a device opened with ray queries (`ray_test_device`; skipped where there's none, but Mesa's lavapipe has them, so they run without a GPU): the capability follows the device, nothing is built while nothing traces rays, the TLAS is rebuilt only when an item moves or comes and a mesh is shared between items, everything is freed when the view goes back to Lit, items past the distance are left out, Ray Hits colors what rays hit and leaves misses dark, the lit frame is unchanged after it, and without ray queries the view is plain and builds nothing. Ray traced shadows (`mod sun_shadows`): hard ones shade like the shadow maps with no map drawn, soft ones have soft edges and hard ones don't, the shadow maps take over past the reach, turning them off frees them and draws as before, a world without shadows traces nothing, and without ray queries the setting is off with a note. `render-types` checks they're off at every preset and without ray queries; `script` checks `UserGameSettings.RayTracedShadows`. Ray traced ambient occlusion (`mod ambient_occlusion`): rays alone darken the corner under a block and leave open floor alone, they find a wall just off screen that screen-space ambient occlusion can't, past the ray tracing distance they fade out to nothing, and turning them off frees the pass and draws as before; `render-types` checks they're off at every preset and without ray queries, `script` checks `UserGameSettings.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`, and the editor's In use line lists each effect. `render-types` checks the distance's presets and range; the editor checks the view's label, its legend, the settings surviving a restart and each state of the viewport badge.
