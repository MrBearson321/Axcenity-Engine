# Animation runtime

Rigs, clips, animators, transitions, blend trees, and root motion.

- **Rig** (`assets`, `model/rig.rs`): an FBX model with a skin, or whose objects a take moves, gets a `Rig`: its bones (every bone a skin cluster uses, every animated mesh object, and every node above them up to the scene root, which holds `ufbx`'s axis and unit conversion), parents first, each with its rest transform (`ufbx`'s local transform: translation, rotation, scale); its joints (each skin cluster, and each rigidly animated mesh object, with an inverse bind matrix from the file's space into the bone's space at bind time: the cluster's `geometry_to_bone` × the inverse of the mesh's `geometry_to_world`, or for a rigid object the inverse of its rest `node_to_world`); and the fit into the 1×1×1 cube. Each vertex keeps its 4 strongest joint weights (`Primitive::joints`/`weights`, normalized; all 0 for a vertex no bone moves, which stays put). Skinned corners of different file vertices are never merged. A **glTF skin** (Phase 62, `gltf_rig` in `model.rs`; the Model Studio saves one) gives a rig too: the skin's joint nodes and every node above them, parents first, each resting at its node's transform, its inverse bind matrices, and each corner's `JOINTS_0` and `WEIGHTS_0` (normalized). A skinned mesh is placed by its joints, so its own node's transform is left out (glTF's rule). A glTF file's animations are takes too (see Takes).
- **Takes** (`assets::load_takes`, `Assets::takes`, cached per file): each FBX animation stack is baked by `ufbx` at the file's frame rate (30 if it doesn't say), from 0 seconds, into translation, rotation and scale keys per node it moves, named by the node. Keys blend linearly (rotations by slerp) and hold before the first and after the last. A take binds to a rig by bone name (`Take::bind`), so an animation-only file for the same skeleton (the usual way characters ship) moves a model loaded from another file. Each take also keeps the skeleton its file was made for (`Take::skeleton`: the nodes it moves, the file's limb nodes and every node above them, where they rest), shared by the file's takes, so it can be retargeted. **glTF animations** (`model/gltf_takes.rs`) are takes the same way: each animation, by its name (`Animation <n>` without one), gets a track per node its channels move (translation, rotation, scale; morph target weights are left out), keyed at the file's times moved so the earliest is 0 seconds, with `STEP` keys held and `CUBICSPLINE` keys read at their values (their tangents left out, as linear). Its frame rate is 1 over the smallest gap between keys, rounded (30 when there's one key). Its skeleton is the moved nodes, the skins' joints and every node above them, as `gltf_rig` builds them, so an animation-only `.glb` exported for a skeleton (Blender's export with no meshes) plays and retargets like an FBX one.
- **Humanoid retargeting** (G16, `anim` `retarget.rs`, roles in `retarget/roles.rs`): a take whose bones don't bind by name to any of a humanoid's bones on the model plays through **humanoid roles** instead. `HumanoidMap::detect` gives each of 52 roles a bone by its name: 22 for the body (Hips, Spine, Chest, UpperChest, Neck, Head, and on each side Shoulder, UpperArm, LowerArm, Hand, UpperLeg, LowerLeg, Foot, Toes) and, since AP1, 30 for the fingers (on each side Thumb, Index, Middle, Ring and Little, each Proximal, Intermediate and Distal; Unity's and VRM's names, `LeftIndexProximal`). Names are read whatever tool made them: Mixamo (`mixamorig:LeftForeArm`, `LeftHandIndex1`), Unreal (`lowerarm_l`, `index_01_l`), Blender and Rigify (`forearm.L`, `DEF-shin.R`, `f_index.01.L`, `thumb.01.L`), Unity/VRM (`LeftIndexProximal`, `J_Bip_L_Index1`), DAZ (`lForearmBend`, `lMid1`) and 3ds Max Biped (`Bip01 L Forearm`, `Bip01 L Finger12`: finger 1, its third bone); namespaces before `:` or `|`, sides as `Left`/`L`/`_l`/`.R` and trailing numbers are read through, and end bones (`Index4`, `Finger0Nub`), metacarpals and bones with no side play no role. The spine chain's bones go to Spine, Chest and UpperChest in order. **Rig definitions** (AP1, `assets/rigs/**/*.rig.json`, read by `Assets::rig_definitions`, format in `docs/project/animation-files.md`) set roles by hand: `HumanoidMap::for_skeleton` uses the project's definition that fits the skeleton (every bone it names is there; the one naming most wins), its roles over the name matches, and names for the rest. Both skeletons need the 15 required roles (all but Chest, UpperChest, Neck, Shoulders, Toes and fingers). `Retarget` then measures each body in its rest pose (left from the right upper arm to the left, or thigh to thigh; up from the hips to the head; forward from those) to find the turn from the take's world to the model's (so Y-up and Z-up files meet), and the scale from their leg lengths. Each frame it turns each mapped bone (fingers too) by the take's turn from its own rest, in the world, carried into the model's world and put onto the model's bone's rest (so bones whose own axes point other ways still bend the same way), and moves the Hips by the take's travel, turned and scaled. Bones the take doesn't move keep the pose they had. `Clip::bind` retargets on its own, matching by names alone; `Clip::bind_with` (what `load_clip`'s `LoadedClip::bind` calls, so every Animator and state machine) uses the project's rig definitions too. A take that moves none of the model's bones and can't be retargeted is refused, saying which roles are missing ("can't be retargeted because the model's skeleton isn't a humanoid one by its bone names (no LeftUpperArm, …)"). Root motion follows the retargeted Hips. A changed rig definition binds every running clip again (`Animators::reload`). **Checks** (`retarget/check.rs`): `check_rig` reports a skeleton's missing roles (error), a bone in two roles (error), a definition's unknown role names and bones the skeleton lacks, a role's bone not below the role above it, left and right swapped (the left leg on the body's right, judged from head-up and toes-forward), zero-length limb bones, how it rests (`rest_arm_angle`: T-pose, or an A-pose and its angle) and each hand's finger bones; `check_pair` compares the take's skeleton with the model's (rest poses 10° or more apart, finger counts, legs more than 25% longer or shorter). **Retarget profiles** (AP2, `retarget/profile.rs`, format in `docs/project/animation-files.md`): a clip's link can name a `*.retarget.json` profile (`"retarget"`), read by `load_clip` (a profile that can't be read fails the clip, saying so). With one, the take is retargeted even onto a skeleton whose bone names match. Without one, the defaults apply. **Match rest poses** (on by default): each role's bone first turns, in the model's world, from the way it rests to the way the take's skeleton's bone rests (the arc between the directions from the bone's joint to the next role's joint, e.g. upper arm to forearm, hand to middle finger; a role with no next role, like a fingertip or the head, takes its parent role's turn; the hips none), so a take made in a T-pose plays right on a model resting in an A-pose instead of the difference playing as motion. **Hips** travel `Scaled` (by leg length, the default), `Unscaled` or `InPlace` (only along the model's up, so a walk plays on the spot). **Plant feet** puts each foot, with two-bone IK on its leg (`ik::reach`), where the take's foot is: its rest place moved by the take's foot travel, turned and scaled as the hips' is, so a planted foot stays planted when legs differ in shape; the foot keeps the turn the take gave it. **Match hands** puts each hand where the take's is relative to its hips, scaled like the body, so touching hands keep touching. **Fingers** (0 to 1) scales every finger's weight. Each role can have a **weight** (0 leaves the bone to other layers and out of the bones the clip moves; between, it blends from the pose it had) and an **offset** (degrees about its own X, Y, Z, after the take's turn). A changed profile binds running clips again. Tests: `retarget_tests.rs`, `retarget/profile.rs`.
- **Posing** (`Model::pose`): a pose is one bone transform per rig bone; each joint's matrix is fit × the bone's world transform × its inverse bind × the inverse fit (`Posed::joints`), and each vertex is the weighted sum of its joints' matrices (normals by their inverse transposes, tangents turned). The renderer does that sum on the GPU (below). `Posed::primitive` still does it on the CPU, when asked, for tests and tools that need the corners themselves. The collision hull and `Size` stay those of the bind pose.
- **Blend shapes** (morph targets; facial animation F1, `assets` `model/morph.rs`, `docs/research/facial-animation.md`): glTF morph targets (`POSITION` and `NORMAL`; names from the mesh's `extras.targetNames`, which Blender writes; default weights from the mesh's `weights`) and FBX blend shapes (each blend deformer channel's full shape, its in-betweens left out; a `blendShape1.` style prefix dropped; `DeformPercent` as the default weight) are read into `Model::morph_targets` (names, one list per model: shapes of one name in several meshes are one target, so a face split into skin, lashes and teeth moves as one) and `Primitive::morphs` (per primitive and shape, only the corners it moves, with their moves and normal changes at weight 1). A glTF accessor with no data (no buffer view and no sparse values, which Blender's exporter writes for a shape that doesn't move a primitive) reads as no moves, as the glTF spec says, though the `gltf` crate's checks refuse it (`read_gltf` skips only that check). A file without a shape's normals (or with all-zero FBX normal offsets) gets them worked out: each moved corner's normal turns as the triangles around it do, so flat shading stays flat. Corners of different FBX vertices are never merged when the mesh has shapes. Moves are fitted with the model (scaled; normal changes worked out again through the fit). Simplified levels of detail keep the shapes of the corners they keep (`morph::remapped`); cooked models keep them. `Posed` carries one weight per target (`morph_weights`, the file's defaults until `set_morph_weights`), and `Posed::primitive` leans the corners toward the shapes before skinning; `Model::rest_posed` poses a model with shapes and no skeleton. The Import dialog counts a model's blend shapes. They are drawn on the GPU (F2, `render/src/renderer/skinning.rs`): `ModelMesh` hands the renderer each shape's moved corners (`MeshSource::morph_count` and `morph`, a `MorphView`), which it uploads once per mesh with the skin (group 2: binding 1 is each corner's range of moves, binding 2 the moves, each naming its shape), and a `Skin` carries the pose's weights (`Skin::set_morph_weights`) after its joints in the shared joints buffer, sixteen weights to an entry. `InstanceRaw::skin.y` packs the joint count (low 16 bits) and the weight count (high 16 bits). `pose_corner` and the motion pass's `previous_corner` lean each corner by its weighted moves before skinning, so the scene, shadows, the prepass and motion vectors all see the shape. A model with shapes and no Animator is drawn with its file's default weights (`Skins::rest`, made again each frame). Culling grows a morphed mesh's box by each corner's largest move. Hardware ray tracing (sun shadows, reflections, GI) still sees the unmorphed mesh: it poses joints only.
- **Drawing posed models** (GPU skinning, graphics Phase 14; `render/src/renderer/skinning.rs`): `scene::build_draw_list_posed` takes `Poses` (posed models by MeshPart, from the host's `anim::Animators`) and draws each primitive of those MeshParts with the model's own mesh (`ModelMesh`, which hands over its corners' joints and weights through `MeshSource::skin_joints` and `skin_weights`) and a `DrawItem::skin`: a `render::Skin` of the pose's joint matrices, one `Arc` per MeshPart per frame, shared by its primitives. The renderer uploads each animated mesh once, with its joints and weights in a storage buffer of its own (group 2 in `shader.wgsl`, a stand-in of one unweighted corner for other meshes), and each frame puts every drawn skin's joints (and their normal matrices) in one shared buffer (binding 9 of the frame's and the shadow passes' group 0, grown when it's too small). Each instance carries where its skin's joints start and how many (`InstanceRaw::skin`, location 14; 0 for an item that isn't animated), and `vs_main` and `vs_shadow` blend them per corner as `Posed` does on the CPU (`pose_corner`), so shadows, the prepass and every level of detail are posed too. So parts posed differently share one GPU copy per primitive and batch together. Culling uses the posed box: at upload each joint gets the box of the corners weighted to it (`GpuSkin`), and an item's box is those boxes moved by its joints, with the unweighted corners' box (`ScenePipeline::item_bounds`). A pose of another model (the `MeshId` just changed) is ignored. A skinned model with no Animator is drawn still, in its bind pose. Storage buffers in the vertex shader need a backend with full limits (DirectX 12, Vulkan, Metal), which the light lists already needed. Tests: `a_posed_character_looks_the_same_skinned_on_the_gpu_as_on_the_cpu` (player), and `render`'s `tests/skinning.rs`. Meshes whose corners change some other way (terrain chunks) still keep their key and change `MeshSource::version`, and the renderer rewrites their corners (`update_custom_mesh`).
- **One clip type** (v3 Phase 7, `anim` `clip.rs`): `anim::Clip` makes a clip file ready to play on one thing: its property tracks checked (`PropertyClip`), and on a skeleton its linked take and bone tracks bound to the rig's bones (bone tracks for bones the take moves are left out). Playback counts in phases (0 the clip's start, 1 its end): `sample` poses the bones it moves (take first, then bone tracks), `properties` gives its property values, `events_between` its events, `root_motion` the root's travel. A linked clip's take time is `start` + phase × (`end` − `start`), so its duration sets its speed. `Clip::animated_bones` says which bones it moves, for blending.
- **Property tracks**: Parts (Position, Orientation, Size, Color, Transparency), a Model's pivot, and UI elements (UDim2 Position and Size, colours and transparencies). With the game UI setting reduced motion on, a GUI Instance's Animations and AnimationTracks don't move (`Motion::Reduced` in `animators.rs`): one that doesn't loop jumps to its end, one that loops holds; see [../ui.md](../ui.md). Sampling is pure (`evaluate`); Orientation keys use quaternion slerp. `apply_evaluated` keeps an `anim::Applied` per animated thing: where a Model's or moving Part's carried parts sat when it first played (so they never drift), and the targets it couldn't find (reported once). `blend_evaluated` mixes values by a weight from what each property is now (`current_numbers`), for a track fading in or out. See `docs/scripting/api-animation.md` and `examples/hello-door`.
- **Animators** (`anim`, owned by the player and by each editor Play session, like physics; one host for everything since v3 Phase 7): see `docs/scripting/api-animation.md` and `docs/project/animation-files.md`. `Animators::update` finds every Animator and Animation in the Workspace and groups them by the thing they animate (an Animator's parent, an Animation's parent, or for an Animation under an Animator, the Animator's parent); each thing has one runner. Each frame a runner (1) runs its Animator's state machine, if `StateMachine` names one: read through `Assets` (again only when the path or the MeshPart's model changes; clips bound to the skeleton if the thing is a MeshPart whose model has one), started in its default state, taking `Play` requests, moved on by the frame's time × `Speed` (not while `Enabled` is off), then its transitions; (2) on the first frame, plays the Animations with `AutoPlay`; (3) moves on each Animation played with `animation:Play()` (from its first `Play`, holding its last frame after it ends or stops) and each AnimationTrack (its time by its own speed, its weight toward its target over its fade, its end or its fade-out firing `Stopped`). It then applies, in order: the state machine's current state's property values; each track and played Animation, oldest start first, by its weight (so the latest start wins); then on a skeleton the pose: the state machine's (or the rest pose), with each layer's bones mixed toward its pose by its weight. A state's clip plays from its start to its end at its speed (× the state's speed); looping clips go round, others hold their last frame. `Play` cross-fades from the pose now (blending each bone's transform) over its blend, 0.25 s by default. Problems (a file that can't be read, a take the file doesn't have, a take for another skeleton, a state that isn't there, a target that isn't under the thing) are reported once each and leave that part still.
- **Transitions** (Phase 40, `anim` `controller.rs`): each frame, after the clock moves on, the machine looks for a transition to take: from Any State (never to the state already playing) or from the current state, whose conditions all hold (`IsTrue`/`IsFalse` for a Bool, `Triggered` for a Trigger, `Greater`/`Less` than `value` for a Number; a missing or wrong-type parameter doesn't hold) and whose exit time, if it has one, is reached: for a looping state with an exit time below 1, from that point to the end of each pass; otherwise once the state has played that much in all (a state with no clip lasts a second and loops). The highest `priority` wins, then the first in the file. Taking one uses up the Triggers its conditions test and cross-fades into its state over `blend` seconds. While a blend that isn't `interruptible` runs, nothing else is taken (a script's `Play` always is, and can itself be interrupted). A transition naming a state that isn't there, or with no conditions and no exit time, is reported and left out. Parameters a script sets that the controller doesn't declare are reported once each.
- **Blend trees** (Phase 41): a state's motion can be a 1D blend tree: a Number parameter and clips at thresholds (`files::blend_weights`). Below the lowest threshold or above the highest, that clip plays alone; between two, they share the pose by how close the value is to each (exact at a threshold); two children at the same threshold count as the upper one. A child whose clip didn't load gives its share to the others. Every child is sampled at the state's one phase and the poses are mixed (bone by bone, translation and scale lerped, rotation slerped), and the state's pass lasts the clips' lengths by their shares, so a walk and a run blended together stay in step (feet don't slip). Root motion is the clips' travel by their shares; events, looping and exit time come from the clip counting most. A blend tree whose parameter isn't a Number is reported, and plays as if the value were 0.
- **2D blend spaces** (Phase 78, `anim` `blend_space.rs`): a state's motion can be a blend space: two Number parameters (`parameter_x`, `parameter_y`, such as sideways and forward speed) and clips at points. The points are joined into triangles (Bowyer-Watson Delaunay, `triangulate`); a value inside a triangle shares the pose among its three corners by barycentric weights (`blend_space_weights`), so on a point that clip plays alone, exactly, and its pose, pass length and root motion are the clip's own (no foot sliding at the sample points). Outside every triangle the value goes to the nearest edge and its two ends share it; points all on one line blend as a 1D tree along it; a point given twice counts once. Sampling, pass length (feet in step), root motion, events and exit time work as for blend trees. A blend space whose parameters aren't Numbers is reported and plays as if they were 0. Test: `layer_tests.rs`'s `a_character_strafes_in_eight_directions_from_one_blend_space_without_foot_sliding` (a 3×3 grid of root-motion strafes: each point plays its clip alone, the travel per pass is the clip's, and between points the travel follows the parameters).
- **Layers** (Phase 78, `anim` `controller.rs`): a state machine can have layers over its states (the base layer). Each layer has its own states, transitions and default state, sharing the file's parameters (compiled as a state machine of its own, `LayerDef::machine`, into `CompiledLayer`), a weight (0–1), a mode and a mask. Each frame the Animator runs every layer's machine by the same clock (no root motion from layers; their clips' events fire as `AnimationEvent` too; only the base layer sets `CurrentState` and fires `StateChanged`), then mixes them over the base pose in file order (`CompiledLayer::mix`): on each bone its mask picks (a bone path counts with every bone under it; an empty mask is the whole skeleton) and its current clips move (so an empty state moves nothing and fading into or out of one fades the layer's hold on those bones, `Machine::layer_pose`), **Override** moves the bone toward the layer's pose by the weight, and **Additive** adds how far the layer's pose turns, moves and scales the bone from the rig's rest pose, by the weight (an aim offset is an additive blend space by pitch and yaw). Layer weights live in `AnimatorState::layer_weights` (`core::LayerWeight`: the weight now, its target and the seconds left), filled in from the file's weights the first time the Animator runs; `animator:SetLayerWeight` fades them at an even pace by the Animator's time. A mask naming a bone the skeleton doesn't have, and a weight set for a layer the file doesn't have, are reported once. A file with no layers plays exactly as before (`a_state_machine_with_only_a_1d_blend_tree_poses_as_its_machine_alone`). Tests: `an_upper_body_reload_layer_plays_while_running_and_the_legs_keep_running` (the noodle's Run on the base layer, a two-second Reload masked to Top: Top waves, Root and Mid keep running though the reload would hop them, and a script's fade leaves Top halfway), `an_additive_layer_adds_its_turn_from_the_rest_pose`.
- **Followers** (default humanoid HD4, `anim` `Animators::follow`): a skinned MeshPart directly inside a posed skinned MeshPart, with nothing animating it itself (no Animator, Animation or IKControl of its own), is posed as its parent is: each of its bones takes the parent's final pose (after IK and foot placement) of the bone with the same name, and bones the parent hasn't stay at rest. It's also kept where the parent's skeleton puts it: its `Orientation` is the parent's and its `Size` and `Position` are what its own model's fit needs for the two files' spaces to line up (`place_follower`, written only when they differ, so it can't be moved by hand). So parts made on one skeleton in separate objects (`file.glb#Armature/Hair`) wear as one character from one Animator, and swapping one is setting its `MeshId`. Followers lead their own skinned children in turn. A follower's blend shapes stay at rest. Tests: `a_part_inside_the_body_follows_its_pose_and_place` (`player/src/default_humanoid_tests.rs`).
- **Runtime IK** (Phase 79, `anim` `ik_control.rs` and `feet.rs`, run from `Animators::update_one` after the pose and any AnimationTracks, and before drawing; nothing runs while the MeshPart's Ragdoll is on). An `IKControl` in a skinned MeshPart, or in its Animator, gives that thing a runner even with no Animator. It uses the solver of Phase 8c (`ik::reach_toward`, which adds a `Pole` point the middle joints swing toward). `Type`: **Position** reaches `Target` with the bones from `ChainRoot` down to `EndEffector` (the two above it if `ChainRoot` is empty); **Rotation** turns the end effector as the Target is turned from the MeshPart; **Transform** is both; **LookAt** faces the end effector at the Target, sharing the turn evenly among the bones from `ChainRoot` down, and holds at `MaxAngle` degrees from the way the character faces (`Forward`, in the MeshPart's axes; zero works it out from where the rest pose's toes point, or else -Z). Bones are named by path, name, or name without a namespace (`Head` finds `mixamorig:Head`). `Weight` (and `Enabled`, or having no Target) fades at an even pace over `SmoothTime`, and the Target's place is followed with a time constant of a third of it; a control first run starts where it's asked to be, with no fade. The solved pose is mixed in bone by bone by the weight. IKControls run in tree order, after the feet. A bad name, a Target that's neither a Part nor an Attachment and an IKControl with no skeleton are reported once. **Foot placement**: a character controller's `FootPlacement` (or `StarterPlayer.CharacterFootPlacement`, which turns it on for each player's character) on the MeshPart or a Model above it. `Animators::with_ground(character::foot_ground)` is how hosts give it rays, since `anim` has no physics. Legs are found by name (`LeftFoot`, `foot.L`, with the calf and thigh as the bones above, and the hips where the thighs meet). Each foot casts down from half a leg above to half a leg below the MeshPart's bottom; each foot's lift is how much higher or lower the ground under it is than that bottom, so a walk's lifted foot stays lifted, and flat ground changes nothing. If nothing within 5% of a leg (at least 0.05 studs) of the bottom is under the feet or the middle, the character is in the air and the feet stay with the animation. The hips go down by the lowest foot's drop, the legs reach with the knees toward the toes (`Pole`), and a foot on the ground leans with the ground's normal, fading away once the animation lifts it. Feet and hips ease over 0.06 s. It runs on each device, from what that device has. Tests: `ik_control_tests.rs` (`an_npc_with_a_look_at_ikcontrol_turns_its_head_to_follow_the_player_within_its_limits`, `turning_ik_off_returns_to_the_plain_animation_within_smooth_time`), `character`'s `tests/feet.rs` (`a_character_standing_on_stairs_has_both_feet_on_a_step_with_no_knee_bending_backwards`).
- **Root motion** (Phase 39, `anim` `clip.rs`): a clip with `root_motion` on finds its root bone: the first bone (top-most) whose track moves it across the ground (more than 0.001 in X or Z at any of 9 samples through the clip). Its pose keeps that bone over where it started and facing the way it started (the twist around world Y removed), keeping its height and the rest of its turn. The travel and turn it would have made move the MeshPart instead: each frame, the root's horizontal travel from the clip's start (in the file's space, turned into the way the model faced when that stretch began) and its change of heading, counting every whole pass of a looping clip (so there's no slide back at the loop), and blended by each state's share during a fade. The travel is fitted into the model's box, stretched by the part's `Size` and turned by its `Orientation`, then added to `Position`; the turn is added to `Orientation.Y` (a turn around the world's up). Setting `Position` teleports an unanchored part and stops it (see Physics), so root motion suits anchored characters. A clip with root motion on and no such bone plays normally.
- **Animation events** (Phase 39): each event's time is a fraction of the clip; as the current state's phase passes it (counted from the phase before the frame, not counting the phase after, once per pass of a looping clip, once for a clip that doesn't loop; in mirror for a negative speed), the host fires the Animator's `AnimationEvent` with the event's name, in time order, after `StateChanged`. The state being faded from fires none.
- **Ragdolls** (Phase 41, `anim` `ragdoll.rs`): a `Ragdoll` in a skinned MeshPart. **Build** (its Properties) makes, from the rig's rest pose, a capsule Part per main bone named after the bone (hips, chest, head, upper arms, forearms, thighs, calves; transparent, no shadow, no bounce) with an Attachment `Bone` where the bone is, and a joint above each from the humanoid preset (`ragdoll::JOINTS`): BallSocketConstraints for the waist (cone 25°, twist ±30°), neck (35°, ±45°), shoulders (70° leaning 40° down from a T-pose, ±45°) and hips (50° leaning 25° ahead, ±30°), and HingeConstraints bending -3° to 140° for elbows and knees (a cone can't hold a limb that bends one way). Bones are found by name: the namespace (`mixamorig:`) dropped, `Left`/`Right` in front or `.L`/`_L`/`.R`/`_R` behind, then `head`, `arm`/`upperarm`, `forearm`/`lowerarm`, `upleg`/`thigh`/`upperleg`, `leg`/`shin`/`calf`/`lowerleg`; the hips are where the thighs meet and the chest where the arms meet. Each capsule runs from its bone to the next (the neck for the chest, the bone's first child for a head, forearm or calf), as wide as a share of the hips' width. Bodies can be resized and moved, and joints' limits changed, like any Part and constraint; Build again replaces them (one undo step). The editor draws a ragdoll's capsules, cones and arcs while it, its MeshPart or something in it is selected, or with the Constraints overlay (`editor/src/constraints.rs`). While the game runs, the Animators give every MeshPart with a Ragdoll a runner: while `Enabled` is off the bodies follow the animated bones (moved with the speed they moved at, so turning it on carries the motion on; physics leaves them out); while it's on, each body's `Bone` frame poses its bone (`pose_from_bodies`: other bones keep their animated place relative to their parent, every bone its scale), overriding the Animator's pose, and the MeshPart follows the hips' travel across the ground. Turned off, the Animator plays `GetUpState` (if named, with no blend of its own) and the pose blends from where the ragdoll lay back to the Animator's over `BlendTime` seconds (0.5), standing where it fell. A character controller doesn't move a character whose Ragdoll is on, and never counts a Ragdoll's bodies as its own. `examples/hello-character`'s Dummy stands idle and a Script knocks it over backward every few seconds with `ragdoll:ApplyImpulse("Head", …)`.
