# Physics

How parts move, touch, collide, and react to wind.

- `Anchored` parts are fixed colliders; unanchored parts are dynamic bodies. New parts default to `Anchored = false`, like Roblox. A third-person controller is not a body: before each physics step the host anchors the parts it moves, so they do not fall or tip, and other parts keep this gravity and friction (`docs/scripting/api-character.md`).
- **Gravity** (Phase 34): `Workspace.Gravity`, 196.2 studs/s² by default (Roblox's, `physics::GRAVITY`), read before every frame's steps (`physics::gravity`; a NaN or infinite value counts as the default). The character controllers fall by it too, with the launch speed of a `JumpHeight` jump at the default, so in lower gravity they jump higher, as parts fly further.
- Fixed 60 Hz steps from an accumulator, at most 10 per frame. The same steps give the same results at any frame rate. There's no interpolation yet, so above 60 fps moving parts update in 60 Hz steps.
- `physics` finds script changes by comparing each Part with what it last read or wrote, not through events. Setting `Position` or `Orientation` teleports a part **and stops it** (decided in Phase 6; Roblox keeps the speed). Toggling `Anchored` also starts the part from rest.
- **Touched** (decided in Phase 7): after every fixed step, `physics` compares the pairs of parts with a solver contact against the pairs touching before, and queues the new ones (`Physics::take_touches`). Frame step 4 fires `Touched` on both parts of each pair. Contacts already there in the very first step (parts resting on each other when the game starts) aren't reported. A pair stops counting as touching only after 3 steps in a row apart, so resting parts that lose contact for a step don't fire again, while real bounces do. Two anchored parts never touch (rapier doesn't test fixed against fixed), as in Roblox.
- **Collision groups** (Phase 38): every Part and MeshPart is in the group its `CollisionGroup` names (`"Default"` at first; a name that isn't registered counts as Default). The world keeps the project's groups (`core::CollisionGroups`, at most 32, Default first, saved as `collision-groups.json`; `docs/project/layout.md`): any two collide until set not to, in the editor's Collision Groups window (Model ▸ Build) or by `PhysicsService` during Play. Each collider gets a rapier interaction group (its group's bit, and a mask of the groups it collides with, cached in `CollisionGroups::mask`), so parts set not to collide pass through each other, sensors included, and never touch. A part's mask is part of what physics compares each frame, so changing its group or the table updates its collider in place. Terrains and foliage are Default. The characters' probes pass through parts whose group doesn't collide with their body's first part's (`character`); navigation agents already walk through characters, so players and NPCs set apart walk through each other and still stop at walls.
- **CanTouch** (Phase 38): a part with `CanTouch` off still collides but touches nothing: no `Touched` or `TouchEnded` on it or on what touches it.
- **TouchEnded** (Phase 38): a touching pair ends when it's been apart for 3 steps in a row (the same rule that stops resting parts from touching again), or at once when one part can't touch the other any more (`CanTouch` off, or set not to collide). `Physics::take_touch_ends` lists the ended pairs (smaller ID first), and frame step 4 fires `TouchEnded` on both parts after `Touched`. A pair touching when the game started ends like any other. A part that leaves the Workspace or is destroyed ends nothing.
- **CanCollide** (Phase 35): a part with `CanCollide` off has a sensor collider: nothing bounces off it (an unanchored one falls through the floor), but its overlaps count as touches, so `Touched` still fires, as in Roblox. Switching it rebuilds nothing; the collider just becomes or stops being a sensor.
- **Wind** (added on request after Phase 37): `Workspace.GlobalWind` (studs/s, default none) pushes unanchored parts with `WindAffected` on (default off). Before each step every dynamic body's forces are reset, and each affected part pushes its body with a drag force `WIND_DRAG` (2) × its average face area × (wind − its velocity), which wakes it. Linear drag keeps it simple and stable; thin, light parts blow away, heavy blocks resting on the ground stay. A NaN or infinite wind counts as none. There are no rigs yet; when bones exist they should read the same wind.
- **Assemblies** (Phase 35, `physics/src/assembly.rs`): parts joined by enabled WeldConstraints (union-find over `assembly::welds`, Part and MeshPart only) are one rapier body, placed at the assembly's root part, with each part's collider offset on it where the part was when the body was built. The root is anchored first, then not Massless, then largest by volume, then smallest ID; `GetRootPart` and `Mass::of_assembly` use the same rule. The body is fixed if any part is anchored. A Massless part that isn't the root has a zero-density collider; a part alone keeps its mass. After each step every part's pose is the body's times its offset, and all share the body's velocities.
- An assembly is rebuilt (body and colliders) when its parts change (a weld added, removed, enabled or disabled, a part joining or leaving), its root changes, or a script moves or (un)anchors one of its welded parts, so welds hold parts where they are then. The new body keeps the root's velocity, starts from rest if a part was moved or (un)anchored, and takes a velocity a script set. A part alone keeps its body through teleports and anchoring, as before. Colliders on one body never touch each other. Parts with NaN or infinite numbers are left out. A little angular damping too, because rapier has no rolling resistance.
- **Joints** (Phase 36, `physics/src/joints.rs`): HingeConstraint, BallSocketConstraint, PrismaticConstraint, CylindricalConstraint, SpringConstraint, RopeConstraint and RodConstraint each join `Attachment0` to `Attachment1` with one rapier impulse joint between their parts' bodies, its frames the attachments' frames on the bodies (`Body::offset` times the attachment's pose). rapier's free axis is the frames' X, so a hinge turns and a prismatic slides along Attachment0's `Axis`; a spring, rope or rod couples the three linear axes, so its limits and motor act on the distance. Joints are synced after the assemblies: one whose bodies changed (an assembly rebuilt) is made again, one whose properties changed is updated in place, and a disabled one, one on a single body (welded together) or one whose attachment isn't on a part is removed. A ball socket's `UpperAngle` is a real cone (rapier's coupled Y and Z axes: the angle between the attachments' X axes; Phase 41, it was each way limited on its own, which let a limb stick where both were near their limits), and its twist is measured from the middle of `TwistLowerAngle`..`TwistUpperAngle` (Attachment0's frame turned there, the limits shifted to match), since rapier measures the cone's ways as parts of the whole turn and a twist far from 0 stretched them. Hinge, ball socket, prismatic and cylindrical joints turn off contacts between their two bodies (an axle in a wheel, a door in its frame); springs, ropes and rods keep them. A Motor is a force-based velocity motor with its max torque or force (`math.huge` is no limit). A Servo is a stiff position motor (stiffness 1e9, no damping, so it sags about a tenth of a degree under load) whose setpoint moves toward the target at its speed each step, starting from where the joint is; a velocity-only servo sagged a constant ~g·dt/(2r) because of the solver's order. A spring is rapier's force-based spring with `Stiffness` and `Damping` toward `FreeLength`, limited to `MinLength`..`MaxLength`. A winch pays the rope out at `WinchSpeed`, reels slack in at it, and while the rope is taut pulls the load with a velocity motor (at most `WinchForce`) while the rope's limit shortens behind it, so a winch too weak to lift holds the load where it is. rapier closes a violated joint at 3 studs a second at most (`normalized_max_corrective_velocity`), so a rope made too short, or a joint enabled with its attachments apart, pulls together at that speed. `CurrentAngle`, `CurrentPosition` and `CurrentLength` are worked out from where the attachments are (`joints::current`), not stored.
- **Movers** (Phase 37, `physics/src/movers.rs`): LinearVelocity, AngularVelocity, VectorForce, Torque, AlignPosition and AlignOrientation act on the body of the part their `Attachment0` is on (and everything welded to it). They keep no rapier objects: they're read from the world every frame after the joints (`movers::read`, enabled ones whose Attachment0 is on a part with a dynamic body), and before every fixed step, after the wind and the servos, each adds a force or torque for that step (`movers::apply`). VectorForces and Torques go first: a force at Attachment0 (or the center of mass with `ApplyAtCenterOfMass`) or a torque, in `RelativeTo`'s axes (World, Attachment0, or Attachment1, without which a mover set to it does nothing). Velocities and aligns then work out the force through the center of mass (or the torque, through the world inertia) that ends the step at the speed they want, counting gravity and the forces already added (`user_force`, `user_torque`), clamped to `MaxForce` or `MaxTorque` (length, not per axis). So a LinearVelocity or AlignPosition holds its part up, and an AlignOrientation holds it level against an off-center VectorForce; with two on one body pushing the same way, the last one in ID order wins. Turning aims a little faster to make up for the angular damping rapier applies after the step. An align closes its gap as a critically damped spring of natural frequency `Responsiveness`, solved implicitly (speed' = (speed + dt·r²·gap) / (1 + 2r·dt + r²·dt²)) so it's smooth and never overshoots at any Responsiveness; `RigidityEnabled` closes the gap in one step; either is capped at `MaxVelocity` or `MaxAngularVelocity`. AlignPosition moves Attachment0 to Attachment1 (TwoAttachment, following its speed) or to `Position` (OneAttachment); AlignOrientation turns it to face as Attachment1 does, or as `CFrame`'s rotation. There are no reaction forces on Attachment1's part. Contacts and joints push in rapier's solver after the movers, so each velocity or align compares the speed its body ends a step with against what gravity and the added forces would give, and moves an extra force halfway toward the difference each step (`movers::Learned`, within its max): an AngularVelocity spins a top on the floor at its speed despite the friction, and the extra fades when nothing pushes. It's forgotten when a script moves or throws the body. Max forces default to 100000 and speeds to 100000 (JSON can't save `math.huge`, which they accept).
- **Ragdolls** (Phase 41, `anim/src/ragdoll.rs`, docs in `docs/rendering/animation.md`): a Ragdoll in a skinned MeshPart holds a body Part per main bone and a joint per joint. Physics leaves out the parts of a Ragdoll that's off (the Animators move them with the animation) and, while one is on, its MeshPart (which follows the hips, so its bind-pose hull doesn't push the bodies apart); the bodies of one that's on get 16 more solver iterations (`RAGDOLL_ITERATIONS`, so a chain of light limbs holds its joints when it lands; 32 made a knocked ragdoll leap off the ground). Landing hard still pushes a joint past its limit for a few frames (rapier pulls it back at 3 radians a second at most); lying still, every joint is within its limits. A part's `Shape` can be `Capsule` (Phase 41): rapier's capsule along X, `Size.X` long ends included, as wide as the smaller of `Size.Y` and `Size.Z`; it's drawn and picked as a cylinder between two balls (`scene::draw_list::part_pieces`). Tests: `player/src/ragdoll_tests.rs`.
- **Vehicles** (Phase 40, `physics/src/vehicles.rs`): a `VehicleSeat` drives the body it's in (its assembly, the chassis) on the `VehicleWheel`s parented to that body's parts, with rapier's ray-cast vehicle controller (`DynamicRayCastVehicleController`), decided over Phase 36 hinges because it stays stable at speed; cars built from hinges still work. Each wheel is a spring along its -Y from `SuspensionRestLength` above its `Position` and a ray `Radius` further, rolling toward its -Z on its X axle; the rays skip the chassis, sensors (CanCollide off) and the wheels' parts, and follow the chassis part's collision group, so no wheel collider touches the ground. Like movers, cars are read every frame (`vehicles::read`; a second seat in one car does nothing) and the controller is made again then, keeping only each wheel's spin and steering angle (`Rolling`); `vehicles::drive` runs before each step after the movers. `Throttle` (-1 to 1) pushes the `Driven` wheels with `Torque` × mass (so Torque is studs/s² on flat ground), less toward `MaxSpeed`; `Steer` turns the `Steers` wheels toward `SteerAngle` (right is clockwise from above) at `TurnSpeed` degrees a second. `HandBrake`, or an empty seat with no Throttle, holds the car with the `Brakes` wheels (120 studs/s²), and below 1 stud/s along the ground with most wheels down it stops dead; otherwise a car with no throttle coasts at 8 studs/s². Three fixes keep rapier's controller steady at these sizes: the tyres also see the step's downhill pull of gravity (or a braked car creeps down a slope), the side grip is half rapier's (its full amount wobbles harder each step on four wheels), and each wheel's damping is capped at its share of what stops the car moving along its spring in one step (a narrow car rolls easily, and more rocks it). Springs push at most four car weights. After each step each wheel's `WheelPart` (alone in its body, not welded into the car) is moved to the wheel, spun and steered, in the world and the simulation, and remembered as seen so it isn't taken for a script's move. A car at rest sleeps and is left alone until its throttle or steering changes. Tests: `physics/src/vehicle_tests.rs`.
- **Physical properties** (Phase 34): each part's collider takes its density, friction and restitution from `CustomPhysicalProperties`, or from its Material's `core::Material::physical` when that's nil (MeshParts use Plastic's). Plastic, Glass and Neon keep what every part had before (density 1, friction 0.5, elasticity 0.25, weights 1: `PhysicalProperties::LEGACY`); Metal is dense (7.85, 0.4, 0.25), Ice slippery (0.92, 0.02, 0.15, friction weight 3) and Rubber grippy and bouncy (1.3, 0.9, 0.85, weights 2 and 3). Terrains and foliage keep the old values. Where two parts touch, friction and elasticity are averages weighted by each part's weights. rapier's own even average is that when both weights are 1; a collider with other weights keeps them in `user_data` and turns on the contact hook (`physics/src/material.rs`), so pairs without weights simulate exactly as before. Projects from format 3 give their Metal parts the old values when they're upgraded (`docs/project/layout.md`).
- **Velocities** (Phase 34): after every step physics writes each moving part's `AssemblyLinearVelocity` (studs/s) and `AssemblyAngularVelocity` (radians/s about the world axes) back, like `Position`; anchored and sleeping parts read zero. A part made with a velocity (set in the editor, or before it's in the Workspace) starts with it, and a script that sets one throws the part from the next step, even in the same frame as a teleport. Scripts' `ApplyImpulse`, `ApplyImpulseAtPosition` and `ApplyAngularImpulse` add impulse ÷ the assembly's mass (and the inertia's turn) to those properties through `physics::Mass`, which reads a part's mass, center of mass and inertia the way its collider would have them, without stepping.
- **MeshParts** (Phase 17) are bodies like Parts. They collide as the **convex hull** of their model's corners, stretched to `Size` (hollows such as under a table are filled in). A flat model, or one that isn't loaded (no `MeshId`, a missing or broken file), collides as its box. The host hands physics the project's `Assets` (`Physics::with_assets`); `Physics::new()` has none, so every MeshPart is a box. A model that loads later counts as a change, and the collider is rebuilt.
- **Queries** (v2 Phase 6): `cast_ray` and `debug_colliders` read the world the same way the simulation does, without stepping it or writing back. A ray's direction length is how far it reaches. Both include sensors (`CanCollide` off) and terrain heightfields. Phase 38 (`physics/src/query.rs`): `raycast`, `shapecast` (a ball, an oriented box, or a part's own collider swept along a direction; parts it starts inside aren't hits, and a part never hits itself), `parts_in_box` and `parts_in_radius` (parts whose world bounding boxes overlap a box or ball) and `parts_in_part` (parts whose colliders overlap one part's, not itself) take a `QueryParams`: a filter list (each Instance and everything inside it) that's excluded or the only parts found, a collision group (only parts in groups that collide with it), whether sensors and parts with `CanQuery` off are passed by, and the most parts listed. Overlap queries list each part once, in tree order, and never Terrains. `QueryParams::default()` finds everything (`cast_ray`); scripts' params respect `CanQuery`; the characters' probes respect `CanCollide` and groups but not `CanQuery`, so a solid part always stops them. The editor draws collider wireframes from `debug_colliders`; scripts call `workspace:Raycast` (see `docs/scripting/api-core.md`). `solids` (unified Phase 12) lists the solid colliders (no sensors, no visual foliage) for the navigation mesh, and `Solid::column` gives where a vertical line passes through one (`docs/architecture/navigation.md`).
- **Foliage** (v2 Phase 9): painted foliage with collide on is extra solid colliders parented to the Terrain. Visual foliage has none. Ground collision from the terrain itself is unchanged. A model uses the same convex hull as a MeshPart; a built-in prop uses its axis-aligned box.
