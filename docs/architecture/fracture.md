# Fractures

How a part breaks into pieces (Phase 42): cutting them in the editor (`mesh_edit`), and shattering in the game (`physics/src/fracture.rs`). Breakable joints and welds are in `docs/architecture/physics.md` (Breaking).

## The Fracture

A `Fracture` inside a Part or MeshPart. Its settings: `Pieces` (12), `Seed` (0), `Pattern` (`FracturePattern.Uniform` or `Impact`), `InteriorMaterial` (Plastic), `Threshold` (60 studs/s), `DebrisLifetime` (5 s; 0 keeps the pieces) and `PiecesId`, the file Generate wrote. It fires `Shattered`; scripts call `fracture:Shatter(point?, force?)`.

## Generate (`mesh_edit`)

The Fracture's Properties have **Generate** (editor only, not during Play; `EditorApp::generate_fracture`, one undo step that sets `PiecesId`; the file stays when it's undone). It calls `axcenity_mesh_edit::generate`:

- The source mesh is the part's shape at its `Size` (Block, Wedge, Ball, Cylinder; a Capsule can't be fractured yet), or a MeshPart's model in its file's units (`Mesh::from_model`).
- `seed_points` scatters `Pieces` points in the mesh's bounds (and inside it) with a small built-in generator (SplitMix64), so the same `Seed` always makes the same pieces. `Uniform` spreads them evenly; `Impact` clusters them toward the middle, for more, smaller pieces there. In a thin part (its smallest side under a quarter of the next), every point is on the middle plane, so the cuts go straight through, as cracks in glass do, and no piece rests on a slanted one. The shapes use `sin` and `cos`, so a Ball's or Cylinder's pieces may differ in the last bits between platforms; a Block's and Wedge's don't.
- `voronoi_pieces` cuts each point's cell out of the mesh: the mesh cut (`Mesh::cut`) by the plane halfway to every other point, keeping the near side. A cut closes the hole it leaves with `Surface::Interior` triangles (`fill.rs`: each loop of the cut ear-clipped in the plane, so concave loops, several loops and loops with holes work), then `merge.rs` refills each flat face so repeated cuts don't pile up needle triangles. A convex part gives convex pieces that tile it exactly: no gaps, no overlaps.
- `write_glb` saves them as `<name>.fracture.glb`: next to the MeshPart's model, or in `assets/models` for a Part. Each piece is the object `Piece1`, `Piece2`, … where it was in the part, with a primitive per material it keeps and one for its interior faces. A Part's outside is its `Color`, `Metalness`, `Roughness` and Material's opacity (blended below 1) and glow; a MeshPart keeps its model's materials (base color texture by its file, next to the model). The inside is `InteriorMaterial`'s look in the outside's color. Generating again overwrites the same file.

## Shattering (`physics`)

- At each sync physics notes the parts with a Fracture whose `PiecesId` is set (`fracture::breakables`). Before each step it keeps every dynamic body's velocities and center of mass (only when there's such a part). After the step, for each contact on one of those parts, the speed is how fast the two touching points were closing along the contact normal before the step. Past `Threshold` (0 or less: impacts never shatter it) the part shatters at the deepest contact point, with the force the speed times the two bodies' reduced mass (the moving one's mass against something anchored). What hit it gets its velocity from before the step back, so it carries on through, and physics syncs again so the pieces are bodies for the next step.
- `Physics::shatter` (a script's `Shatter`, handed over by `ScriptRuntime::apply_shatters` before physics steps) does the same at once, from the part's middle unless given a point, at the part's own velocity.
- Shattering loads the objects of `PiecesId` (`list_objects`, then each as `<file>#PieceN`), stretches them as a MeshPart's model is stretched (the whole file's `size` to the box the part's shape fills at its `Size`: a Ball's smallest side, a Cylinder as wide as the smaller of Y and Z), and makes each a MeshPart named after its object, where it was in the part, turned as the part was, in a new Model named `<part>Pieces` in the part's parent. They copy the part's `Transparency`, `CastShadow`, `CanCollide`, `CanTouch`, `CanQuery`, `CollisionGroup` and physical properties (its `CustomPhysicalProperties`, or its Material's). Each moves at the part's velocity plus `force` ÷ the part's mass away from the point: all of it for the nearest pieces, half for the farthest. The part's parent becomes nil, keeping its Fracture, and physics queues the Fracture (`take_shattered`); hosts fire `Shattered` after `TouchEnded` (`ScriptRuntime::fire_breaks`).
- Pieces touch face to face with no gap. They collide as their own triangles (`shapes::own_convex_mesh`: a model of at most 512 triangles that's convex collides as itself, not as parry's hull of its corners, which for an outline with nearly straight corners came out with faces that sank through the floor and rocked), so pieces shattered at rest lie still.
- Debris (`fracture::age`, every step): over the last second of `DebrisLifetime` (all of it, if shorter) each piece's `Transparency` goes from where it started to 1, then the Model is destroyed. A Model a script destroys first is forgotten.
