# Rendering

Read before working in the `render` crate or changing material and light properties.

## Boundary

`render` takes a **draw list** (meshes, materials, transforms, lights, camera, UI draw data) and draws it with `wgpu`. It has no knowledge of Instances, scripts, physics or the editor. The editor viewport and the player both feed it the same way.

Draw list conventions: Y up, right-handed, and colors as sRGB from 0 to 1 (the renderer converts them to linear). Built-in shapes are 1×1×1 at the origin, so a part's transform is translation × rotation × scale(Size). The Instance-to-draw-list step lives in the `scene` crate (`build_draw_list`), shared by the player and the editor. UI entries are pixel-positioned rectangles and shaped text; the renderer draws them over the 3D scene.

## Targets (decided in Phase 8)

- `Renderer` draws into a window surface it creates itself (the player).
- `TextureRenderer` draws into a texture on a GPU device someone else created (the editor, where eframe owns the device and egui shows the texture). Both share the private `ScenePipeline`, so they draw identically.
- The texture is `Rgba8Unorm` holding sRGB-encoded colors, because that's what egui 0.32 expects of native textures. Drawing goes through an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` view so the GPU encodes the shader's linear output. Sampling an sRGB texture would hand egui linear values and everything would look far too dark.
- **Known issue (found in Phase 8):** on at least one driver (AMD, Vulkan, Windows), the background clear through an sRGB view is *not* sRGB-encoded while shaded pixels are. The player's window surface behaves the same way, so on that machine the sky shows as `(90, 154, 227)` instead of the intended `(161, 204, 242)`. Other drivers may encode it, so the sky color can differ between machines. Fix when the sky becomes a drawn pass (stage 4), or sooner by drawing the background with a full-screen triangle instead of a clear.

## Materials (PBR from day one)

Every material carries: base color, metalness, roughness, emissive, opacity. Presets like plastic, metal, glass and neon are just preset values for these fields. Don't add stage-specific material fields that a later stage would have to undo.

How Part properties map onto these (decided in Phase 3):

- `Color`, `Metalness` and `Roughness` are Part properties and feed the draw list directly.
- `Material` (Plastic, Metal, Glass, Neon) is a preset. Choosing it sets `Metalness` and `Roughness` to the preset's values, which you can then adjust (for example, a rougher Metal). See `Material::preset()` in `core`.
- Glass is drawn see-through, and Neon gives off its `Color` as emissive light instead of being shaded.
- `Transparency` (Phase 35, Parts and MeshParts, 0 to 1) multiplies the preset's (or the model's) opacity by `1 - Transparency`; a fully transparent part isn't drawn at all, but can still be picked in the editor.
- `EmissiveColor` and `Emission` (Phase 35, Parts) add `EmissiveColor × Emission` to the emissive light on top of any Neon glow, so any material can glow in any color; above 1 it blooms.
- Metals reflect the sky through environment lighting (stage 4).

Transparency: opaque items are drawn first. Then see-through items are drawn, farthest to nearest by their centers, with blending on and depth writes off. Overlapping see-through items can still blend in the wrong order; that's accepted for now.

## Geometry

Built-in primitives are block, sphere, cylinder and wedge, plus imported glTF and FBX models (MeshParts, Phase 17; see Imported models). Part shape rules match Roblox: a Ball's diameter is the smallest of its `Size` axes, a Cylinder lies along X (`Size.X` is its length, its diameter is the smaller of Y and Z), and a Wedge's slope rises from the bottom front (-Z) edge to the top back (+Z) edge. `Orientation` is degrees, applied around Z, then X, then Y.

## Imported models (decided in Phase 17)

- **Draw list:** `Shape::Mesh(Arc<dyn MeshSource>)` is a mesh the caller owns (positions, normals, optional texture coordinates, `u32` indices), and `Material::base_color_texture` an `Arc<dyn TextureSource>` (sRGB RGBA8). They're traits so `scene` hands over the loaded model's data without copying it each frame. The renderer uploads each on first sight, keyed by `key()`, and frees what hasn't been drawn for 600 frames. Broken data (an index past the last corner, the wrong number of pixels) is logged once and not drawn.
- **Vertices** gained texture coordinates (location 12; the built-ins' are all 0). Textures are bound as group 1 with a repeating, mipmapped linear sampler; items without a texture (all built-in shapes) bind a 1×1 white texture. Mipmaps are made on the CPU at upload by 2×2 averaging (in sRGB, slightly off but not visibly). Levels bigger than the GPU allows are left out, so huge textures show less sharp instead of failing.
- **Model space:** `assets` fits every model into the 1×1×1 cube centered on the origin, like the built-ins, so `Size` stretches its box. It walks the default scene's node tree (or every mesh, if there's no scene), applying node transforms (mirrored nodes get their triangles flipped back); missing normals are made smooth from the triangles. Only triangle lists are drawn; points, lines, strips and fans are skipped.
- **FBX** files are read by `ufbx`, which converts them to glTF's space (Y up, right-handed, meters) whatever axes and units they were saved in, and triangulates polygons. Every visible node with a mesh is drawn, at its world transform (pivots and geometry offsets included), one draw item per material it uses. Normals the file lacks are made by `ufbx`. Materials become metallic-roughness: `ufbx` maps Lambert/Phong and other FBX shading models (and Blender's Principled BSDF) to PBR values; base color × factor, opacity (below 1 is `BLEND`; an opacity map makes it `MASK` at 0.5), metalness (default 0), roughness (default 1, Phong shininess converted), emission × factor, and the base color texture with V flipped to the engine's top-left origin. Their other maps are read too (see Texture maps). Skeletons, skin weights and takes are read too (see Animation). Not yet: per-UV-set texture choice (the first UV set is used).
- **Materials:** each glTF primitive is one draw item with its metallic-roughness material: base color factor × base color texture, metalness, roughness, emissive (converted from glTF's linear light to the draw list's sRGB). Alpha mode `OPAQUE` is solid, `MASK` cuts out pixels below its cutoff (`Material::alpha_cutoff`, a `discard` in the shader), `BLEND` is drawn see-through (opacity capped just below 1 so it always takes the see-through path). Solid items always write alpha 1. Its metallic-roughness, normal, occlusion and emissive textures are used (see Texture maps). Not yet: texture transforms, vertex colors, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`, double-sided materials (back faces are still culled), skins and animation.
- **Texture maps** (Phase 19): `Material::maps` (`TextureMaps`) adds a normal map (`NormalMap`: scale, and `flip_green` for DirectX-style maps), metalness, roughness, ambient occlusion and opacity maps (`ChannelMap`: one channel of a texture, optionally inverted, so a glossiness or smoothness map works as roughness and one packed texture can serve several maps), an emission map and a specular map. A map multiplies its value (metalness, roughness, emissive); the opacity map replaces the base texture's alpha; occlusion darkens only ambient light, by `occlusion_strength`. A specular map scales how strongly non-metals reflect: plain 0.5 is the usual 4%, 0 none, 1 twice that (older Phong materials' specular maps, rather than a guessed metalness). Built-in shapes ignore every map.
- **Binding:** each texture is uploaded once as plain `Rgba8Unorm` with an sRGB view as well: base color and emission are read through the sRGB view (so samples are linear light), the other maps as plain values. Each distinct set of textures and map settings gets one bind group (group 1: 8 textures, the sampler and a small `MapsUniform` with each map's channel mask, inversions, normal scale and green sign, occlusion strength and which maps are present); maps an item lacks bind the 1×1 white texture. Items are batched by mesh and that set.
- **Tangents:** normal maps need them. `assets` works them out after fitting, for primitives with a normal map, from the triangles' texture coordinates (area-weighted, made perpendicular to each normal; `w` is the side the texture's "up" is on). File tangents are ignored, so seams on maps baked with MikkTSpace can show slightly. Vertices carry them at location 13; the shader turns them by the model matrix and builds the tangent frame per pixel.
- **glTF:** base color, metallic-roughness (roughness in green, metalness in blue), normal (with `scale`), occlusion (red, with `strength`; often the same image as metallic-roughness) and emissive textures. All of a primitive's textures must use the same texture coordinate set as its first one; others are left out.
- **FBX:** what `ufbx` maps to PBR, plus the FBX material's own slots: normal map (or a bump map that looks like a normal map: mostly blue, red and green near half; a grey height map is left out), metalness, roughness (inverted for a glossiness map, and for Lambert/Phong, whose shininess map is glossiness), ambient occlusion, emission (a map with no emission color glows in its own colors), opacity (its own map, or the base texture's alpha when it's the same image; alpha if the map has any, else red; any opacity map cuts out at 0.5) and, for Lambert/Phong, specular. A value with a map takes the map's value alone (factor 1), as FBX programs do.
- **Texture roles by name** (Phase 20, FBX; `model/maps.rs`): every texture a material links is also judged by its file name, the last telling word deciding (`Bench_BaseColor_4K`, `T_Bench_N`, `rock_ORM`; words split at `_ - .`, spaces and lowercase-to-capital, sizes such as `4K` and numbers skipped; one-letter suffixes such as `_N`, `_R`, `_M` only at the end). Channel-packed names are split into their channels: ORM/ARM (occlusion, roughness, metalness in R, G, B), RMA, MRA, metallic-roughness (G, B), Unity's metallic-smoothness (metalness R, smoothness A, inverted), HDRP's mask map (metalness R, occlusion G, smoothness A), specular-smoothness (specular RGB, smoothness A) and albedo-transparency (base color, opacity A, cut out). `_NormalDX` maps flip green. A packed name always wins over the slot the texture is linked to; for Lambert/Phong, whose few slots exporters misuse (Unity puts metallic-smoothness in specular), any name wins; for PBR materials a single-map name only fills slots the links left empty. A texture linked to a property the engine doesn't read is used for its name's role, or noted as unknown ("has a texture `wood.png` it doesn't know the use of…") and left out; height maps (by name, or linked as bump/displacement and not normal-looking) are noted as unused.
- **Unlinked textures** (FBX): textures no material links, often embedded ones, go to the file's only material, or the material whose name (without words such as `mat` and `shader`) is the longest part of the texture's name, and fill only empty maps.
- **SurfaceAppearance** (Phase 21, `scene`): a SurfaceAppearance inside a MeshPart replaces the maps of the model's material named its `MaterialName` (others keep the file's). It decides every map: an empty one is no map, so deleting the SurfaceAppearance is the way back to the file's own. Its `Color` multiplies the material's color. A metalness or roughness map gives the value alone (factor 1); without one, the material's own value is used. `MetalnessChannel`, `RoughnessChannel`, `AmbientOcclusionChannel` and `OpacityChannel` pick channels (`Enum.TextureChannel`), `InvertRoughness` reads a glossiness map, `InvertOpacity` an opacity map that's white where see-through, `NormalStrength` and `NormalMapDirectX` set the normal map, `AmbientOcclusionStrength` the occlusion, and `EmissiveStrength` multiplies the glow (an emission map on a material that doesn't glow glows in the map's colors). An opacity map on a solid material cuts it out at 0.5. Map files are read through the project's `Assets` (`Assets::texture`), decoded once, and a file that can't be loaded is reported once ("Could not load the texture `…`: …") and left out.

## Animation (Phase 38)

- **Rig** (`assets`, `model/rig.rs`): an FBX model with a skin, or whose objects a take moves, gets a `Rig`: its bones (every bone a skin cluster uses, every animated mesh object, and every node above them up to the scene root, which holds `ufbx`'s axis and unit conversion), parents first, each with its rest transform (`ufbx`'s local transform: translation, rotation, scale); its joints (each skin cluster, and each rigidly animated mesh object, with an inverse bind matrix from the file's space into the bone's space at bind time: the cluster's `geometry_to_bone` × the inverse of the mesh's `geometry_to_world`, or for a rigid object the inverse of its rest `node_to_world`); and the fit into the 1×1×1 cube. Each vertex keeps its 4 strongest joint weights (`Primitive::joints`/`weights`, normalized; all 0 for a vertex no bone moves, which stays put). Skinned corners of different file vertices are never merged. glTF skins and animation aren't read yet.
- **Takes** (`assets::load_takes`, `Assets::takes`, cached per file): each FBX animation stack is baked by `ufbx` at the file's frame rate (30 if it doesn't say), from 0 seconds, into translation, rotation and scale keys per node it moves, named by the node. Keys blend linearly (rotations by slerp) and hold before the first and after the last. A take binds to a rig by bone name (`Take::bind`), so an animation-only file for the same skeleton (the usual way characters ship) moves a model loaded from another file.
- **Posing** (`Model::pose`): a pose is one bone transform per rig bone; each joint's matrix is fit × the bone's world transform × its inverse bind × the inverse fit, and each vertex is the weighted sum of its joints' matrices (normals by their inverse transposes, tangents turned). It runs on the CPU: simple, and plenty for a few characters (move it to the GPU when scenes need many). The collision hull and `Size` stay those of the bind pose.
- **Drawing posed models**: `scene::build_draw_list_posed` takes `Poses` (posed models by MeshPart, from the host's `anim::Animators`) and draws those MeshParts with a `PosedMesh`: the pose's corners with the model's texture coordinates and triangles, under a key made from the MeshPart and the primitive (top bit set), so each posed part has its own GPU copy. `MeshSource::version` changes with each pose; the renderer then writes the new corners over its copy's (`update_custom_mesh`, `queue.write_buffer`; vertex buffers are `COPY_DST`), or uploads the mesh again if the number of corners changed. A pose of another model (the `MeshId` just changed) is ignored. A skinned model with no Animator is drawn still, in its bind pose.
- **Property clips** (`anim::PropertyClip` / `PropertyPlayers`): authored `Position` / `Orientation` / `Size` tracks in a `.anim.json` with `"kind": "property"`. Sampling is pure (`evaluate`); Orientation keys use quaternion slerp. `PropertyPlayers` finds every `Animation` Instance, loads its `Clip`, advances time, applies values under its parent, and fires `Completed` / `AnimationEvent`. See `docs/scripting.md` (Animation) and `examples/hello-door`.
- **Animators** (`anim`, owned by the player and by each editor Play session, like physics): see `docs/scripting.md` (Animator) and `docs/project-and-export.md` (Animation files). `Animators::update` finds every Animator in the Workspace inside a MeshPart with a skeleton, reads its controller and clips through `Assets` (again only when its `Controller`, MeshPart or model changes), starts it in its default state, takes `Play` requests, moves it on by the frame's time × `Speed` (not while `Enabled` is off) and poses its part. A state's clip plays from its start to its end in the take at its speed (× the state's speed); looping clips go round, others hold their last frame; a negative speed plays backwards. `Play` cross-fades from the pose now (blending each bone's transform) over its blend, 0.25 s by default. Problems (a controller or clip that can't be read, a take the file doesn't have, a take for another skeleton, a state that isn't there) are reported once each and leave the part still or its state at rest.
- **Transitions** (Phase 40, `anim` `controller.rs`): each frame, after the clock moves on, the machine looks for a transition to take: from Any State (never to the state already playing) or from the current state, whose conditions all hold (`IsTrue`/`IsFalse` for a Bool, `Triggered` for a Trigger, `Greater`/`Less` than `value` for a Number; a missing or wrong-type parameter doesn't hold) and whose exit time, if it has one, is reached: for a looping state with an exit time below 1, from that point to the end of each pass; otherwise once the state has played that much in all (a state with no clip lasts a second and loops). The highest `priority` wins, then the first in the file. Taking one uses up the Triggers its conditions test and cross-fades into its state over `blend` seconds. While a blend that isn't `interruptible` runs, nothing else is taken (a script's `Play` always is, and can itself be interrupted). A transition naming a state that isn't there, or with no conditions and no exit time, is reported and left out. Parameters a script sets that the controller doesn't declare are reported once each.
- **Blend trees** (Phase 41): a state's motion can be a 1D blend tree: a Number parameter and clips at thresholds (`files::blend_weights`). Below the lowest threshold or above the highest, that clip plays alone; between two, they share the pose by how close the value is to each (exact at a threshold); two children at the same threshold count as the upper one. A child whose clip didn't load gives its share to the others. Every child is sampled at the state's one phase and the poses are mixed (bone by bone, translation and scale lerped, rotation slerped), and the state's pass lasts the clips' lengths by their shares, so a walk and a run blended together stay in step (feet don't slip). Root motion is the clips' travel by their shares; events, looping and exit time come from the clip counting most. A blend tree whose parameter isn't a Number is reported, and plays as if the value were 0.
- **Root motion** (Phase 39, `anim` `clip.rs`): a clip with `root_motion` on finds its root bone: the first bone (top-most) whose track moves it across the ground (more than 0.001 in X or Z at any of 9 samples through the clip). Its pose keeps that bone over where it started and facing the way it started (the twist around world Y removed), keeping its height and the rest of its turn. The travel and turn it would have made move the MeshPart instead: each frame, the root's horizontal travel from the clip's start (in the file's space, turned into the way the model faced when that stretch began) and its change of heading, counting every whole pass of a looping clip (so there's no slide back at the loop), and blended by each state's share during a fade. The travel is fitted into the model's box, stretched by the part's `Size` and turned by its `Orientation`, then added to `Position`; the turn is added to `Orientation.Y` (a turn around the world's up). Setting `Position` teleports an unanchored part and stops it (see Physics), so root motion suits anchored characters. A clip with root motion on and no such bone plays normally.
- **Animation events** (Phase 39): each event's time is a fraction of the clip; as the current state's phase passes it (counted from the phase before the frame, not counting the phase after, once per pass of a looping clip, once for a clip that doesn't loop; in mirror for a negative speed), the host fires the Animator's `AnimationEvent` with the event's name, in time order, after `StateChanged`. The state being faded from fires none.

## Stages

1. PBR shading with a sun and ambient light (implemented)
2. Sun shadows (implemented in Phase 30)
3. Many point and spot lights (implemented in Phase 31)
4. Sky and environment lighting (implemented in Phase 32)
5. Post-processing: HDR, tone mapping, bloom (implemented in Phase 33)
6. Sprites and particles (Phase 37)

Each stage must leave existing scenes rendering correctly.

## Lights (stage 3)

- `DrawList::lights` holds `Light { position, color, brightness, range, cone }`. `scene` builds them from PointLight and SpotLight Instances parented to a Part or MeshPart. The light sits just above the part (half the local Y size, plus 0.05 studs) so the part's outer faces are not back-facing an interior light. A SpotLight's `Direction` turns with the part. A light with `Enabled` false, or not inside a part, is skipped. A part that contains an enabled light also gets an emissive glow so the fixture itself is visible. Inserting a light with no part selected creates an anchored Neon Ball named Lamp and parents the light to it. Spot and point lights still cast no shadows of their own.
- Tiled lighting: the CPU projects each light's bounding sphere onto the screen and lists it in the 32 px tiles it touches (up to 64 lights per tile, 1024 lights total; the nearest to the camera win). The shader loops over only its tile's lights. This is simpler than clustered (3D) lighting; switch to clusters if depth-heavy scenes need it.
- Falloff is smooth to zero at `range`. Lights cast no shadows (only the sun does).
- **Editor light gizmo** (`viewport.rs`, `paint_light_reach`): while editing, a selected light, or one inside a selected part, shows where it shines in its color. A PointLight shows three rings at its range. A SpotLight shows its cone out to `Range` (see-through sides, 8 edges, the far rim and a ring halfway), an arrow down the beam's middle, and, where that middle ray first meets another part within range (`scene::pick_hit_skipping`, skipping the light's own part), a ring the cone's width laid on that surface. View > Light Cones shows every light's reach, fainter for unselected ones. `scene::LightMarker::part` names the light's part.

## Sky and environment (stage 4)

- `DrawList::sky` (`Sky`) comes from the Lighting properties `SkyTopColor`, `SkyHorizonColor`, `GroundColor` and `EnvironmentIntensity`. The sky is procedural: a gradient plus a sun disc, drawn as a full-screen triangle (which also fixes the background-clear encoding issue above). There is no HDR skybox yet.
- Environment lighting is analytic from the same gradient: diffuse from the hemisphere, specular from the gradient blurred by roughness, combined with the split-sum approximation. Metals therefore reflect the sky. The flat ambient is kept only as a small floor (0.05).

## Post-processing (stage 5)

- The scene draws into an `Rgba16Float` texture. A bloom chain (prefilter above `BloomThreshold`, downsample, additive upsample) feeds a composite pass that adds bloom, applies `Exposure` and tone mapping (colors under 0.6 are unchanged, brighter ones roll off toward white) into the real target, then the UI draws on top.
- Lighting properties `PostProcessingEnabled`, `Exposure`, `BloomEnabled`, `BloomIntensity` and `BloomThreshold` configure it. Off means a plain clamp with no bloom.
- `scene` draws Neon parts 2.5× brighter than their color when bloom is on, so they pass the threshold and glow.

## Sprites and particles (Phase 37)

- `DrawList::sprites` holds `Sprite { position, size, rotation_degrees, color, opacity, brightness, light_emission, texture }`: camera-facing pictures, unlit and unshadowed. `renderer/sprites.rs` and `sprite.wgsl` draw them last in the HDR scene pass, one instanced 6-corner quad each, turned to face the camera, depth-tested against solid items without writing depth, and sorted farthest first. The color is premultiplied by opacity and the alpha written is `opacity × (1 − light_emission)` with a `One, OneMinusSrcAlpha` blend, so light emission 0 paints over what's behind and 1 adds light. Runs of the same texture are one draw call; `None` (or a texture that couldn't be uploaded) is a plain square. Brightness above 1 blooms.
- **Wind**: an emitter with `WindAffectsDrag` slows its particles toward `Workspace.GlobalWind` instead of to a stop (`v = wind + (v − wind) / (1 + Drag·dt)`), so it needs a `Drag` above 0, as in Roblox. The editor's viewport applies the wind while editing too.
- `scene::Particles` (owned by the player and by the editor's viewport, which shows emitters while editing too) turns ParticleEmitters into sprites: it gives off and moves particles each frame in world space (at most 4000 per emitter, 1000 births per frame), and `sprites` reads each emitter's look. The six built-in pictures (`ParticleType`) are drawn in code (64×64, white, shape in the alpha) and shared (`scene::built_in_particle`). A `Texture` is loaded through `Assets` like any texture; Kenney's CC0 Particle Pack images work as they are (white shapes on transparency).

## Shadows (stage 2)

- `DrawList::shadows` (`Shadows { enabled, distance, softness }`) comes from the `Lighting` service's `ShadowsEnabled`, `ShadowDistance` and `ShadowSoftness` properties (`scene` reads them). The defaults are on, 150 studs, softness 0.2.
- **Cascades**: three 2048² depth maps (layers of one array texture) each cover a slice of the view out to `distance`, nearest first. The slices' ends are mostly geometric (`CASCADE_SPLIT_BLEND`), so the first covers only the closest dozen or so studs and is the sharpest. Each map covers a sphere around its slice (so turning the camera doesn't change its scale), snapped to whole texels so edges don't shimmer. Casters up to 500 studs toward the sun are included. The shader uses the nearest cascade that covers a point and blends into the next over the outer 20% of each, so the change in sharpness doesn't show.
- **Soft shadows** (percentage-closer soft shadows): the shader first searches the map around the point for what blocks the sun and averages its depth, then blurs over a width proportional to the distance from that blocker to the surface times the sun's apparent size (`ShadowSoftness` × `MAX_SUN_SIZE`). So shadows are crisp where a part touches what it shades and soften further away, like real ones; softness 0 is crisp everywhere. Both steps take 16 samples on a Poisson disc, turned per pixel by interleaved gradient noise, and each comparison is itself filtered between four texels. The blur is at most 32 texels.
- **Acne and peter-panning**: the point is offset along its surface normal by 1.5 texels, the depth bias grows with the blur width and the surface's slope to the sun, and the shadow pass adds a slope-scaled hardware bias.
- `DrawItem::casts_shadow` (from a Part's or MeshPart's `CastShadow`, default true) leaves an item out of the shadow pass: it's still shaded by others. See-through items never cast (cut-out textures cast as solid). Batches are split by it.
- Shadows fade out over the last 20% of `distance`. Only the sun casts shadows, and only sun light is darkened (not ambient). Shadows off allocates no shadow map and skips the passes.

## Platform

`wgpu` backends: DirectX 12 or Vulkan on Windows, Metal on macOS, Vulkan on Linux. Avoid features that aren't available on all three.
